# Choose your next subject

If you want practice turning everyday questions into mathematical models, start with the chapters of [Mathematics Foundations](README.md). Read its five chapters as a sequence, at whatever pace suits you; there is no timed schedule. Each one is short, and the point is to understand it, not to finish it by a date.

## After the project

Once you have finished the project, choose one subject from *Mathematics for Computing*, based on a question you want to answer, and work through its prerequisites before moving on to advanced material. The table pairs each question with a subject and the preparation it assumes.

| Your question | Next subject | Preparation |
|---|---|---|
| How do I manipulate formulas and describe growth? | [Precalculus](../mathematics-for-computing/00-precalculus/README.md) | Arithmetic and basic equations |
| How fast is something changing? | [Calculus 1](../mathematics-for-computing/01-calculus-1/README.md) | Algebra, functions, and trigonometry |
| How do several quantities fit together? | [Linear Algebra](../mathematics-for-computing/03-linear-algebra/README.md) | Algebra and basic equations |
| How can I justify a mathematical claim? | [Discrete Math 1](../mathematics-for-computing/05-discrete-math-1/README.md) | Algebra and willingness to write arguments |
| What can uncertain evidence tell me? | [Probability and Statistics](../mathematics-for-computing/07-probability-statistics/README.md) | Check that subject's prerequisites; continuous probability uses calculus |

If you are unsure, Precalculus is the gentlest next step: it revisits the functions and growth of the first two chapters here in much more depth. If the vectors and constraints chapter was the one you enjoyed most, go to Linear Algebra; if it was uncertainty, go to Probability and Statistics once you have the calculus it asks for.

## A sustainable routine

Whatever you choose, the rhythm that works is the one this book has used. Read one explanation, reproduce its worked example without copying, then attempt a new problem. When you make a mistake, write it down and explain the correction. Move on when you can explain the idea and solve a variation, rather than when a week has passed.

The [opening of *Mathematics for Computing*](../mathematics-for-computing/README.md) contains the full subject list and the prerequisite map. Its later chapters connect mathematics to language-model systems; they assume programming and more preparation, and are optional for introductory readers.
