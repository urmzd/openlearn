# Mathematics: choose your next subject

Start with the [introductory course](README.md) if you want practice turning everyday questions into mathematical models. Its five chapters are a reading sequence, not a timed schedule.

## After the introductory project

Choose one subject based on a question you want to answer. Work through its prerequisites before moving to advanced material.

| Your question | Next subject | Preparation |
|---|---|---|
| How do I manipulate formulas and describe growth? | [Precalculus](../mathematics-for-computing/00-precalculus/) | Arithmetic and basic equations |
| How fast is something changing? | [Calculus 1](../mathematics-for-computing/01-calculus-1/) | Algebra, functions, and trigonometry |
| How do several quantities fit together? | [Linear algebra](../mathematics-for-computing/03-linear-algebra/) | Algebra and basic equations |
| How can I justify a mathematical claim? | [Discrete math 1](../mathematics-for-computing/05-discrete-math-1/) | Algebra and willingness to write arguments |
| What can uncertain evidence tell me? | [Probability and statistics](../mathematics-for-computing/07-probability-statistics/) | Check that subject's prerequisites; continuous probability uses calculus |

## A sustainable routine

Read one explanation, reproduce its worked example without copying, then attempt a new problem. Record a mistake and explain the correction. Move on when you can explain the idea and solve a variation, rather than when a week has passed.

The [reference library](../mathematics-for-computing/README.md) contains the full subject list and prerequisite map. Advanced implementation chapters connect mathematics to LLM systems; those chapters assume programming and course-specific preparation and are optional for introductory learners.
