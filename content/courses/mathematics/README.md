# Mathematics Foundations

Learn to describe change, compare quantities, and reason about uncertainty. This introductory course is for anyone comfortable with arithmetic; no programming or university mathematics is required.

By the end, you will build a small model of a community event: estimate costs, compare pricing choices, and explain the uncertainty in attendance. A notebook and calculator are enough.

## Start here

Begin with [Quantities and relationships](01-quantities-and-relationships.md). Read the chapters in order the first time. Each introduces one idea, works through an example, and gives you a problem to try before continuing.

1. [Quantities and relationships](01-quantities-and-relationships.md): turn a situation into a simple model.
2. [Change and growth](02-change-and-growth.md): distinguish fixed additions from repeated multiplication.
3. [Vectors and constraints](03-vectors-and-constraints.md): combine quantities and find a feasible plan.
4. [Uncertainty and evidence](04-uncertainty-and-evidence.md): reason about averages without treating them as guarantees.
5. [Project: plan a community event](05-community-event.md): bring the ideas together and defend your choices.

## How to learn

Work at your own pace. Write down what each symbol means, including its units. Try each practice question before opening the answer. If an idea is unfamiliar, change the numbers in the example and work it again.

This is an introduction, not a replacement for full courses in algebra, calculus, linear algebra, or statistics. After the project, choose a subject from the [mathematics reference library](../../tracks/math/README.md). The [study guide](study-plan.md) explains where to go next.
