# Pass 2: Foundations

The tracer's bigram was fitted by counting, which works for a bigram and for nothing larger. Every later model is trained by gradient descent, and gradient descent needs an autograd engine you can trust, which in turn needs the mathematics underneath it: logarithms and units of information, derivatives and finite differences, Taylor series, linear algebra, probability, floating point, and optimization. This pass builds all of it. It is the longest pass in the course and the one that pays off most often later.

The pass reads in two movements. The first is mathematics, from *Mathematics for Computing*, with a problem set after each topic so you can check that the ideas hold under your own hands. The second is the autograd itself, from *Build Your Own LLM System*: tensors with broadcasting backward passes, an op library checked by gradcheck, fused losses, modules, a training loop, and atomic checkpoints with a token stream. At the end the autograd retrains the tracer's bigram by gradient descent, gets the count MLE back, and the engine serves the result unchanged, because the checkpoint contract fixed in Pass 1 has not moved.

> **Learning objectives**
> - Derive the calculus, linear algebra, probability, and numerics results the autograd relies on, and work each problem set.
> - Explain how gradcheck compares analytic gradients with central differences and how its tolerance is chosen.
> - Explain each op's backward pass, the fused softmax cross-entropy backward, and how the module system registers parameters.
> - Trace a training step and explain why the autograd bigram converges to the count bigram.
> - Explain why checkpoints are written atomically and how a resumed run ends bitwise equal to an uninterrupted one.

## Before you begin

This pass needs [Pass 1](../course-p01-tracer/README.md). In particular, the [byte bigram](../../p00-foundations/00-byte-bigram.md) and its checkpoint format are the target the autograd must reproduce.

## In this pass

Read the sections in order. The problem sets come right after the topics they exercise:

1. [Problem set: functions, logarithms, trigonometry, series, polynomials, and inequalities](../../../mathematics-for-computing/00-precalculus/00-solve-set.md). Afterwards you can work every problem in the set and explain each answer.
2. [Exponents, logarithms, and units of information](../../../mathematics-for-computing/00-precalculus/01-exponents-logs-and-units-of-information.md). Afterwards you can explain how logs convert between bases and units of information (bits, nats).
3. [Trigonometry, rotations, complex numbers, and Euler's formula](../../../mathematics-for-computing/00-precalculus/02-trig-rotations-and-eulers-formula.md). Afterwards you can explain how a 2D rotation is a multiplication by a unit complex number via Euler's formula.
4. [Sequences, geometric series, and frequency ladders](../../../mathematics-for-computing/00-precalculus/03-sequences-and-frequency-ladders.md). Afterwards you can explain how a geometric series builds a frequency ladder and what its sum is.
5. [Polynomials, Horner's rule, and stable quadratic roots](../../../mathematics-for-computing/00-precalculus/04-polynomials-horner-and-stable-roots.md). Afterwards you can explain why Horner's rule evaluates a polynomial in n multiplies and why the stable quadratic formula avoids cancellation.
6. [Derivatives and Finite Differences](../../../mathematics-for-computing/01-calculus-1/01-derivatives-and-finite-differences.md). Afterwards you can explain why the central difference is second-order accurate and how the step size balances truncation against rounding error.
7. [Newton's Method](../../../mathematics-for-computing/01-calculus-1/02-newtons-method.md). Afterwards you can explain why Newton's method converges quadratically near a simple root and when it fails.
8. [Taylor series, remainder bounds, and range reduction](../../../mathematics-for-computing/02-calculus-2/01-taylor-series.md). Afterwards you can explain how range reduction keeps a Taylor series short and how the remainder bound sets the number of terms.
