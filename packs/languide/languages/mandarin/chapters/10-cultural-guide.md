# Cultural Guide

This chapter covers the customs that change **what you say**: how to address people, how face shapes politeness, how refusals are given and heard, what to say with gifts and at banquets, how people write on WeChat, and how accents and vocabulary differ by region.

---

## Forms of Address and Titles

### Name Order

The **surname comes first**, then the given name. 王小明 (Wáng Xiǎomíng) is Mr. Wang, not Mr. Xiaoming. Titles go **after** the surname.

### Titles After Surnames

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| Mr. Wang | 王先生 | Wáng xiānsheng | Polite. Neutral and safe |
| Ms. Li | 李女士 | Lǐ nǚshì | Polite. Formal, any age |
| Miss Zhang | 张小姐 | Zhāng xiǎojiě | Polite with a surname. Normal in Taiwan |
| Teacher Chen | 陈老师 | Chén lǎoshī | Polite. For teachers. Also a respectful title for experts |
| Doctor Liu | 刘医生 | Liú yīshēng | Polite |
| Manager Zhang | 张经理 | Zhāng jīnglǐ | Polite. Job title replaces "Mr." at work |
| Director Wang, President Wang | 王总 | Wáng zǒng | Very polite. Short for any title that starts with 总 |
| Little Wang | 小王 | Xiǎo Wáng | Casual. A senior person to a younger colleague |
| Old Li | 老李 | Lǎo Lǐ | Casual. Between long-time peers. It is friendly, not rude |
| Brother Wang | 王哥 | Wáng gē | Casual. To a slightly older male peer |
| Sister Li | 李姐 | Lǐ jiě | Casual. To a slightly older female peer |

- Use the full name or surname plus title until the other person offers something else.
- A given name alone, such as 小明 (Xiǎomíng), is for family and close friends.
- Many people who work with foreigners use an English name at work. Use the name they give you.

### 你 or 您

| Use | With | Example |
|-----|------|---------|
| 你 (nǐ) | Friends, peers, colleagues at your level, younger people, most strangers of your age | 你好 (Nǐ hǎo) |
| 您 (nín) | Elders, senior managers, clients, teachers, anyone you serve or ask a favor from | 您好 (Nín hǎo) |

- When you are not sure, start with 您. Nobody is offended by too much respect.
- A senior person may say 别这么客气 (Bié zhème kèqi), "don't be so formal". Then change to 你.
- 您 is used most in Beijing and the north. In the south and in Taiwan, 你 with polite words is common in daily life, and 您 appears mostly in service and writing.

## Face (面子)

**Face** (面子, miànzi) is a person's public dignity and standing. Speakers protect their own face and the face of others. This is the reason for most of the indirect language in this chapter.

### Face Vocabulary

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| face, public standing | 面子 | miànzi | Standard |
| to give face | 给面子 | gěi miànzi | To show respect in public: accept an invitation, a toast, a request |
| to lose face | 丢面子 | diū miànzi | Also 丢脸 (diūliǎn) |
| to have face | 有面子 | yǒu miànzi | To look good in front of others |
| to refuse someone face | 不给面子 | bù gěi miànzi | To refuse or contradict someone in public |
| polite, courteous | 客气 | kèqi | 别客气 (Bié kèqi) = don't stand on ceremony |
| relationship, connection | 关系 | guānxi | Trust built over time |
| embarrassed, sorry | 不好意思 | bù hǎoyìsi | The most useful softener in the language |

### What Face Changes in Speech

- **Praise in public. Correct in private.** A correction in front of others costs the person face.
- **Do not say a flat "no" to a request.** Give a reason, a delay or an alternative.
- **Do not say a flat "you are wrong".** Say that something "seems" to have "a small problem".
- **Accept praise with modesty.** Give praise freely.
- **Let the other person refuse easily.** Ask in a way that makes "no" comfortable.

## Indirect Refusals

### What You Hear and What It Means

A clear "no" is rare. Listen for these phrases. Each one is a polite way to decline or to avoid a promise.

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| I will think about it | 我考虑一下 | Wǒ kǎolǜ yíxià | Usually no |
| Let's talk about it later | 再说吧 | Zàishuō ba | Probably no |
| We will see when the time comes | 到时候再说 | Dào shíhou zàishuō | No commitment |
| Next time | 下次吧 | Xià cì ba | No for now. Not a real plan |
| Another day | 改天吧 | Gǎitiān ba | No for now. Not a real plan |
| It is not very convenient | 不太方便 | Bú tài fāngbiàn | No |
| It is a bit difficult | 有点儿困难 | Yǒudiǎnr kùnnan | No |
| I will have a look | 我看看吧 | Wǒ kànkan ba | Unlikely |
| I have something on that day | 我那天有事 | Wǒ nà tiān yǒu shì | No. Do not ask what the "something" is |
| I will do my best | 我尽量 | Wǒ jǐnliàng | A weak yes. Do not rely on it |
| It should be OK | 应该可以 | Yīnggāi kěyǐ | Probably yes. Not a promise |
| The problem is not big | 问题不大 | Wèntí bú dà | Yes, with small issues to solve |
| No problem | 没问题 | Méi wèntí | A real yes |

A change of subject, a long pause or a laugh with no answer also means no.

### Refusing Politely Yourself

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| Sorry, I have something on that day | 不好意思，我那天有事 | Bù hǎoyìsi, wǒ nà tiān yǒu shì | Polite. No detail is needed |
| Thank you for your kindness | 谢谢你的好意 | Xièxie nǐ de hǎoyì | Polite. Say it before the refusal |
| Really, there is no need | 真的不用了 | Zhēn de búyòng le | Polite. Refusing an offer or a gift |
| I appreciate the thought | 心领了 | Xīnlǐng le | Polite. "My heart accepts it" |
| Next time for sure | 下次一定 | Xià cì yídìng | Polite. Softens the refusal |
| I am afraid I can't | 恐怕不行 | Kǒngpà bù xíng | Polite. Clear but soft |
| I have already eaten | 我吃过了 | Wǒ chī guo le | Polite. Refusing food |
| I can't drink alcohol | 我不会喝酒 | Wǒ bú huì hē jiǔ | Polite. Literally "I don't know how to drink" |

### Getting a Clear Answer

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| When would be convenient for you? | 您看什么时候方便？ | Nín kàn shénme shíhou fāngbiàn? | Very polite. Moves a vague answer toward a date |
| When can it be confirmed? | 什么时候可以确定？ | Shénme shíhou kěyǐ quèdìng? | Polite |
| Is there any difficulty? | 有什么困难吗？ | Yǒu shénme kùnnan ma? | Polite. Invites the real reason |

| So shall I wait for your message? | 那我等您的消息？ | Nà wǒ děng nín de xiāoxi? | Very polite. Closes the topic without pressure |

## Gifts

### What to Say

A gift is offered with modest words. The receiver often refuses once or twice before accepting. Offer again. Gifts are usually not opened in front of the giver unless the giver asks.

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| This is for you | 这是给您的 | Zhè shì gěi nín de | Polite. Give with both hands |
| A small token | 一点儿小意思 | Yìdiǎnr xiǎo yìsi | Polite. The standard modest phrase |
| A small sign of my regard | 这是我的一点儿心意 | Zhè shì wǒ de yìdiǎnr xīnyì | Polite |
| I hope you like it | 希望您喜欢 | Xīwàng nín xǐhuan | Polite |
| You are too kind | 太客气了 | Tài kèqi le | Polite. The standard reply on receiving |
| You should not have | 不用这么客气 | Búyòng zhème kèqi | Polite. The ritual first refusal |
| You have spent too much | 让你破费了 | Ràng nǐ pòfèi le | Polite. Thanks for a gift or a meal |
| Then I will accept it. Thank you | 那我就收下了，谢谢 | Nà wǒ jiù shōuxià le, xièxie | Polite. Accepting after the ritual refusal |
| May I open it? | 我可以打开吗？ | Wǒ kěyǐ dǎkāi ma? | Polite. Ask first |
| I like it very much | 我很喜欢 | Wǒ hěn xǐhuan | Polite |
| red envelope | 红包 | hóngbāo | Money in a red packet. Weddings, New Year, births |

### Words and Numbers to Avoid or Use

Some gifts and numbers are avoided because they **sound like** unlucky words.

| Item | Chinese | Pinyin | Why |
|------|---------|--------|-----|
| clock | 钟 | zhōng | 送钟 (sòng zhōng, "give a clock") sounds like 送终 (sòngzhōng, "attend a death") |
| umbrella | 伞 | sǎn | Sounds like 散 (sàn, "to part") |
| pear | 梨 | lí | Sounds like 离 (lí, "to separate") |
| green hat | 绿帽子 | lǜ màozi | A sign that a man's partner is unfaithful |
| four | 四 | sì | Sounds like 死 (sǐ, "death"). Avoid sets of four |
| eight | 八 | bā | Sounds like 发 (fā, "to prosper"). Lucky |
| six | 六 | liù | 六六大顺 (liù liù dà shùn) = "everything goes smoothly". Lucky |
| nine | 九 | jiǔ | Sounds like 久 (jiǔ, "long-lasting"). Lucky |

## Banquets and Shared Meals

A hosted meal has fixed roles. The host orders, urges guests to eat, starts the toasts and pays. The phrases below are the expected lines for each moment.

### Seating and Starting

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| Please sit | 请坐 | Qǐng zuò | Polite. Wait to be shown your seat |
| Please sit here | 您坐这儿 | Nín zuò zhèr | Very polite. The host places guests |
| Please take the seat of honor | 请上座 | Qǐng shàngzuò | Very polite. The seat that faces the door |
| After you | 您先请 | Nín xiān qǐng | Very polite. At doors, lifts and dishes |

## Regional Accents

Everyone learns standard Mandarin at school, but most people speak it with a regional accent. The two patterns below cover most of what a learner hears.

### Northern Speech: The r Ending

Beijing and northern speakers add an r sound to many words. Southern speakers and Taiwan speakers do not.

| English | With r (north) | Pinyin | Without r (south, Taiwan) | Pinyin |
|---------|----------------|--------|---------------------------|--------|
| where | 哪儿 | nǎr | 哪里 | nǎlǐ |
| here | 这儿 | zhèr | 这里 | zhèlǐ |
| there | 那儿 | nàr | 那里 | nàlǐ |
| a little | 一点儿 | yìdiǎnr | 一点 | yìdiǎn |
| a while | 一会儿 | yíhuìr | 一会 | yíhuì |
| to have fun | 玩儿 | wánr | 玩 | wán |
| matter | 事儿 | shìr | 事 | shì |
| doorway | 门口儿 | ménkǒur | 门口 | ménkǒu |

### Southern Speech: Merged Sounds

The spellings in the third column are approximate. They show what you may hear. Do not copy them.

| Feature | Standard | What you may hear | Where |
|---------|----------|-------------------|-------|
| zh, ch, sh said as z, c, s | 是 shì, 吃 chī, 知道 zhīdào | "sì", "cī", "zīdào" | Much of southern China, Taiwan |
| 十 and 四 sound alike | 十 shí, 四 sì | Both begin with s. Only the tone differs | Much of southern China, Taiwan |
| n and l mixed | 牛奶 niúnǎi | "liúlǎi" | Sichuan, Chongqing, Hunan, Hubei |
| f and h mixed | 飞机 fēijī | "huījī" | Fujian, parts of Hunan |
| Final n and ng merged | 今 jīn, 京 jīng | Both sound the same | Many southern regions, Taiwan |
| r said as l | 热 rè, 肉 ròu | "lè", "lòu" | Parts of southern China |
| Fewer neutral tones | 东西 dōngxi, 认识 rènshi | "dōngxī", "rènshì" | Southern China, Taiwan |

### Other Differences in Daily Speech

- Taiwan speakers end sentences with soft particles such as 喔 (ō) and 啦 (la) more often.
- Taiwan speakers say 不会 (bú huì) for "you're welcome". On the mainland it means only "cannot" or "will not".
- Taiwan speakers often use 有 (yǒu) before a verb for a past action: 我有吃 (Wǒ yǒu chī), "I ate". The mainland standard is 我吃了 (Wǒ chī le).
- 小姐 (xiǎojiě) and 先生 (xiānsheng) are the normal polite forms of address in Taiwan. 师傅 (shīfu) and 服务员 (fúwùyuán) are mainland usage.

## Politeness Tiers in Summary

| Tier | Pronoun | Typical words | Use with |
|------|---------|---------------|----------|
| Casual | 你 (nǐ) | 吧 (ba), short sentences, 小 or 老 before a surname | Friends, close colleagues, younger people |
