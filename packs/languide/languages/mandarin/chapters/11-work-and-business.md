# Work and Business

This chapter gives the Mandarin a learner needs at work: introductions and titles, describing a role, meetings, email and chat, calls, small talk, business meals, indirect refusals, office and software vocabulary, and numbers, dates and money. Work speech uses the same grammar as daily speech. The difference is in **titles**, **softeners** and a set of **formal words**.

### How Titles Are Used

- **Surname plus title** is the normal form of address at work: 王总 (Wáng zǒng), 李经理 (Lǐ jīnglǐ), 张工 (Zhāng gōng), 陈老师 (Chén lǎoshī).
- **总 (zǒng)** is short for any senior title that begins with 总. Use it for general managers, directors and company heads. It is the safe choice for any senior person at a client.
- **Deputy titles** contain 副 (fù), as in 副总 (fù zǒng). In direct address, people leave out 副 as a courtesy.
- **老师 (lǎoshī)** is a respectful title for a senior specialist in education, media, design and some technology firms.
- **Peers** use full names, 小 or 老 plus the surname, or English names. Many mainland technology firms ask everyone to use first names or nicknames. Follow what you hear.
- Do not use a given name alone for a senior person.

## Email and Chat

### Openers

A Chinese email opens with the name and title, a comma, the greeting and a colon. There is no word for "Dear" in everyday work email.

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| Dear Director Wang | 王总，您好： | Wáng zǒng, nín hǎo: | Very polite. The standard opener to a senior person |
| Dear Manager Li | 李经理，您好： | Lǐ jīnglǐ, nín hǎo: | Very polite |
| Hello [name] | [名字]，你好： | [míngzi], nǐ hǎo: | Polite. To a peer |
| Hello all | 各位好： | Gèwèi hǎo: | Polite. To a group |

## How "We Will Consider It" Works

A direct "no" costs both sides face. In business, a refusal is given as a delay or a difficulty. Learn to hear it, and to give it.

### What You Hear

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| We will consider it | 我们考虑一下 | Wǒmen kǎolǜ yíxià | Usually no, unless a date follows |
| We will go back and study it | 我们回去研究一下 | Wǒmen huíqu yánjiū yíxià | No decision today. Often no |
| We will discuss it internally | 我们内部讨论一下 | Wǒmen nèibù tǎolùn yíxià | A real step or a delay. Ask when |
| I need to ask my superiors | 我需要请示领导 | Wǒ xūyào qǐngshì lǐngdǎo | The speaker can't decide, or is using a shield |
| This is not easy to arrange | 这个不太好办 | Zhège bú tài hǎo bàn | No |
| There is a certain difficulty | 有一定难度 | Yǒu yídìng nándù | No, or yes at a higher price |
| In principle there is no problem | 原则上没问题 | Yuánzé shang méi wèntí | Yes, but conditions will follow |
| We will reply as soon as possible | 我们尽快给您答复 | Wǒmen jǐnkuài gěi nín dáfù | Neutral. Ask for a date |
| Let's work together when there is a chance | 以后有机会再合作 | Yǐhòu yǒu jīhuì zài hézuò | No |
| Yes, no problem | 可以，没问题 | Kěyǐ, méi wèntí | A real yes |

## Formal Register at Work

### The Rules

1. **Use 您 (nín)** with clients, senior managers and anyone you contact for the first time.
2. **Use surname plus title.** Do not use a given name alone for a senior person.
3. **Open requests with a softener:** 请 (qǐng), 麻烦您 (máfan nín), 打扰了 (dǎrǎo le).
4. **Add 一下 (yíxià)** after the verb. It makes the request sound small.
5. **Do not refuse with a bare 不.** Use 可能 (kěnéng), 恐怕 (kǒngpà) or 不太 (bú tài), and give a reason.
6. **Acknowledge every instruction** with 收到 (shōudào) or 好的 (hǎo de).
7. **Say 我们 (wǒmen), "we",** for your company's position. Say 我 (wǒ), "I", for your own tasks.
8. **Thank people for effort:** 辛苦了 (xīnkǔ le) closes meetings, calls and message threads.
9. **In writing, choose the formal word** from the table below.
