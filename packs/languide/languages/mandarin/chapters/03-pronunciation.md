# Pronunciation Guide

Mandarin has a small set of sounds: about 400 syllables before tones. Each syllable has three parts: an **initial** (the opening consonant), a **final** (the vowel part) and a **tone**. A wrong tone is a wrong word, so learn the tone as part of every word from the first day.

---

## The Tones

### The Four Tones and the Neutral Tone

| Tone | Mark | Pitch (1 low, 5 high) | How it sounds | Example | Meaning |
|------|------|-----------------------|---------------|---------|---------|
| First | ā | 5 to 5 | High and level, like a held sung note | 妈 mā | mother |
| Second | á | 3 to 5 | Rising, like the English question "What?" | 麻 má | hemp, numb |
| Third | ǎ | 2 to 1 to 4 | Low and dipping. In running speech it stays low | 马 mǎ | horse |
| Fourth | à | 5 to 1 | Sharp fall, like a firm "No!" | 骂 mà | to scold |
| Neutral | a | Short and light | No fixed pitch. Unstressed | 吗 ma | question particle |

### Advice for English Speakers

- **First tone:** Start higher than feels natural and do not let the pitch drop at the end.
- **Second tone:** Start in the middle and rise quickly. Do not dip first.
- **Third tone:** Think "low", not "down and up". The full dip and rise is used only before a pause.
- **Fourth tone:** Start high and fall all the way. English speakers often fall too little.
- **Neutral tone:** Say it short and quiet. Do not give it a tone of its own.
- **Questions:** Do not raise your voice at the end of a question. The particle 吗 (ma) makes the question. A rising voice can change the last tone.

### Where the Tone Mark Goes

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| If the syllable has a or e, the mark goes on it | hǎo, xiè, zài, gěi |
| In ou, the mark goes on o | dōu, zǒu |
| In all other cases, the mark goes on the last vowel | liù, duì, xué, guó |
| The dot of i is replaced by the mark | nǐ, yī |

## Tone Changes (Sandhi)

Three rules change tones in running speech. This guide writes the changes for 不 and 一 in the pinyin. It does not write the third tone change, by convention.

### Rule 1: Third Tone

| Situation | What happens | Example | Spoken |
|-----------|--------------|---------|--------|
| Third + Third | The first becomes a second tone | 你好 (nǐ hǎo) | ní hǎo |
| Third + Third | The first becomes a second tone | 很好 (hěn hǎo) | hén hǎo |
| Third + Third | The first becomes a second tone | 可以 (kěyǐ) | kéyǐ |
| Third + First, Second or Fourth | The third tone stays low, with no rise | 老师 (lǎoshī), 很忙 (hěn máng), 请问 (qǐngwèn) | low, then the next tone |
| Third + Neutral | The third tone stays low, and the neutral syllable is higher | 我们 (wǒmen), 喜欢 (xǐhuan) | low, then light and higher |
| Three third tones | Group the words. The first of each pair rises | 我很好 (wǒ hěn hǎo) | wǒ hén hǎo |
| Four third tones | Two pairs | 我也很好 (wǒ yě hěn hǎo) | wó yě hén hǎo |

### Rule 2: 不 (bù)

| Before | 不 is said | Examples |
|--------|------------|----------|
| First, second or third tone | bù (fourth tone, no change) | 不吃 (bù chī), 不行 (bù xíng), 不好 (bù hǎo) |
| Fourth tone | bú (second tone) | 不是 (bú shì), 不要 (bú yào), 不对 (bú duì), 不用 (búyòng) |
| Inside a fixed phrase | bu (neutral) | 对不起 (duìbuqǐ), 好不好 (hǎo bu hǎo), 听不懂 (tīng bu dǒng) |

### Rule 3: 一 (yī)

| Situation | 一 is said | Examples |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| Alone, in counting, in order numbers, at the end of a word | yī (first tone, no change) | 一 (yī), 第一 (dì yī), 十一 (shíyī), 星期一 (xīngqī yī) |
| Before a fourth tone | yí (second tone) | 一个 (yí ge), 一下 (yíxià), 一定 (yídìng), 一共 (yígòng) |
| Before a first, second or third tone | yì (fourth tone) | 一天 (yì tiān), 一年 (yì nián), 一起 (yìqǐ), 一点儿 (yìdiǎnr) |
| Between a repeated verb | yi (neutral) | 看一看 (kàn yi kàn), 试一试 (shì yi shì) |

- 个 (gè) is a fourth tone at root, so 一个 is yí ge even though 个 is said lightly.
- In phone numbers, room numbers and bus routes, mainland speakers say **yāo** for the digit 1.
- In dates and floors, 一 keeps the first tone: 一月 (yī yuè, January), 一号 (yī hào, the first), 一楼 (yī lóu, first floor).

### The Neutral Tone

The neutral tone has no pitch of its own. Its height depends on the tone before it.

| After | Neutral pitch | Example | Pinyin |
|-------|---------------|---------|--------|
| First tone | Mid-low | 妈妈 | māma |
| Second tone | Mid | 朋友 | péngyou |
| Third tone | Mid-high | 我们 | wǒmen |
| Fourth tone | Low | 谢谢 | xièxie |

Where the neutral tone appears:

| Type | Chinese | Pinyin | Meaning |
|------|---------|--------|---------|
| Particles | 的, 了, 吗, 呢, 吧 | de, le, ma, ne, ba | grammar words |
| Suffix 们 | 我们, 你们 | wǒmen, nǐmen | we, you (plural) |
| Suffix 子 | 桌子, 杯子, 饺子 | zhuōzi, bēizi, jiǎozi | table, cup, dumpling |
| Doubled family words | 爸爸, 哥哥, 姐姐 | bàba, gēge, jiějie | dad, older brother, older sister |
| Doubled verbs | 看看, 试试 | kànkan, shìshi | have a look, have a try |
| Second syllable of common words | 东西, 朋友, 喜欢 | dōngxi, péngyou, xǐhuan | thing, friend, to like |
| Second syllable of common words | 什么, 怎么, 多少 | shénme, zěnme, duōshao | what, how, how much |
| Second syllable of common words | 便宜, 麻烦, 认识 | piányi, máfan, rènshi | cheap, trouble, to know (a person) |

Speakers in Taiwan and southern China use fewer neutral tones. For example, they say 东西 as dōngxī and 认识 as rènshì.

## Initials

Mandarin has 21 initials. Many pairs differ only by **aspiration**: a puff of air. Hold your palm in front of your mouth. You must feel air on p, t, k, q, ch and c, and almost none on b, d, g, j, zh and z.

### All Initials

| Initial | How to say it | Example | Meaning |
|---------|---------------|---------|---------|
| b | Like p in "spy". No puff of air | 爸 bà | dad |
| p | Like p in "pie". Strong puff | 怕 pà | to fear |
| m | As in English | 妈 mā | mother |
| f | As in English | 发 fā | to send |
| d | Like t in "sty". No puff | 大 dà | big |
| t | Like t in "tie". Strong puff | 他 tā | he |
| n | As in English | 你 nǐ | you |
| l | As in English | 来 lái | to come |
| g | Like k in "sky". No puff | 高 gāo | tall |
| k | Like k in "kite". Strong puff | 看 kàn | to look |
| h | Rougher than English h. Friction at the back of the mouth | 好 hǎo | good |
| j | Like j in "jeep", tongue tip down behind the lower teeth, lips spread | 鸡 jī | chicken |
| q | Like ch in "cheese", tongue tip down, strong puff | 七 qī | seven |
| x | Like sh in "sheep", tongue tip down, lips spread | 西 xī | west |
| zh | Like j in "judge", tongue tip curled up and back | 中 zhōng | middle |
| ch | Like ch in "church", tongue tip curled up, strong puff | 吃 chī | to eat |
| sh | Like sh in "shirt", tongue tip curled up | 是 shì | to be |
| r | Tongue in the sh position, with voice. Lips not rounded | 人 rén | person |
| z | Like ds in "kids". No puff | 在 zài | at |
| c | Like ts in "cats". Strong puff | 菜 cài | dish, vegetable |
| s | As in English | 三 sān | three |

The letters **y** and **w** are not true initials. They are spelling forms of i, u and ü at the start of a syllable: 一 (yī), 我 (wǒ), 月 (yuè).

### The Sounds English Speakers Get Wrong

#### j, q, x: Flat Tongue

Put the tip of your tongue **down**, behind your lower front teeth. Raise the middle of the tongue to the roof of the mouth. Spread your lips as in a smile.

| Letter | It is not | It is close to | Example | Meaning |
|--------|-----------|----------------|---------|---------|
| j | the j of "jar" with rounded lips | "jee" said with a smile | 家 jiā | home |
| q | "kw" or "k" | "chee" said with a smile and a puff of air | 钱 qián | money |
| x | "ks" or "z" | "shee" said with a smile | 谢 xiè | to thank |

#### zh, ch, sh, r: Curled Tongue

Curl the tip of your tongue **up and back** to the hard ridge behind the upper teeth. Keep the lips neutral.

| Letter | It is not | It is close to | Example | Meaning |
|--------|-----------|----------------|---------|---------|
| zh | "z" | "j" in "judge" with the tongue curled | 这 zhè | this |
| ch | "k" | "ch" in "church" with the tongue curled | 车 chē | vehicle |
| sh | the flat "sh" of "sheep" | "sh" in "shirt" with the tongue curled | 上 shàng | up, on |
| r | the English r with rounded lips | a sound between the r of "run" and the s of "measure" | 热 rè | hot |

**A spelling rule separates the two families.** After j, q and x the vowel is always i or ü. After zh, ch, sh and r the vowel is never a true i or ü.

#### z, c, s: Tongue Behind the Teeth

| Letter | It is not | It is close to | Example | Meaning |
|--------|-----------|----------------|---------|---------|
| z | the English z of "zoo" | "ds" in "kids" | 早 zǎo | early |
| c | "k" or "s" | "ts" in "cats" with a puff of air | 错 cuò | wrong |
| s | (no problem) | "s" in "see" | 四 sì | four |

#### The Buzzing i

After z, c, s, zh, ch, sh and r, the letter **i** is not "ee". Hold the consonant and add voice. The tongue does not move.

| Chinese | Pinyin | Sounds close to | Meaning |
|---------|--------|-----------------|---------|
| 字 | zì | "dz" held with voice | character |
| 次 | cì | "ts" held with voice | time, occasion |
| 四 | sì | "s" then a short buzz | four |
| 知 | zhī | "j" curled, held with voice | to know |
| 吃 | chī | "ch" curled, held with voice | to eat |
| 十 | shí | "sh" curled, held with voice | ten |
| 日 | rì | "r" held with voice | day |

#### The Vowel ü

Say "ee". Keep your tongue in that position and round your lips tightly, as for "oo". That sound is ü.

| Chinese | Pinyin | Meaning | Spelling note |
|---------|--------|---------|---------------|
| 女 | nǚ | female | Written ü after n and l |
| 绿 | lǜ | green | Written ü after n and l |
| 去 | qù | to go | Written u after j, q, x, but said ü |
| 需 | xū | to need | Written u after j, q, x, but said ü |
| 句 | jù | sentence | Written u after j, q, x, but said ü |
| 鱼 | yú | fish | yu is always ü |
| 月 | yuè | month | yue is üe |
| 元 | yuán | yuan | yuan is üan |
| 云 | yún | cloud | yun is ün |

## Pinyin Spelling Rules

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| A syllable that starts with i is written with y | i becomes yi, ia becomes ya, ian becomes yan, iu becomes you |
| A syllable that starts with u is written with w | u becomes wu, uo becomes wo, ui becomes wei, un becomes wen |
| A syllable that starts with ü is written yu | ü becomes yu, üe becomes yue, üan becomes yuan |
| After j, q and x, the two dots of ü are dropped | ju, qu, xu, jue, quan, xun |
| An apostrophe separates syllables where the break is unclear | 西安 Xī'ān (a city) is not 先 xiān (first) |
| An apostrophe separates syllables where the break is unclear | 晚安 wǎn'ān (good night), 女儿 nǚ'ér (daughter) |

## The r Ending (Erhua)

Northern speakers, most of all in Beijing, add an r sound to the end of some words. The character 儿 (ér) is written but is not a separate syllable.

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| where | 哪儿 | nǎr | Without r: 哪里 (nǎlǐ) |
| here | 这儿 | zhèr | Without r: 这里 (zhèlǐ) |
| a little | 一点儿 | yìdiǎnr | The n is dropped in speech. Without r: 一点 (yìdiǎn) |
| a while | 一会儿 | yíhuìr | Without r: 一会 (yíhuì) |
| to have fun | 玩儿 | wánr | Without r: 玩 (wán) |
| matter, thing to do | 事儿 | shìr | Without r: 事 (shì) |

Both forms are correct. Southern mainland speakers and Taiwan speakers almost never use the r ending. Learn to understand it. You can choose not to say it.

### The Classic Number Trap

四 (sì, four) and 十 (shí, ten) differ in initial and in tone. Many southern speakers say both with an s sound, so only the tone separates them.

| English | Chinese | Pinyin | Notes |
|---------|---------|--------|-------|
| four | 四 | sì | Falling tone |
| ten | 十 | shí | Rising tone |
| fourteen | 十四 | shísì | Rising, then falling |
| forty | 四十 | sìshí | Falling, then rising |
| forty-four | 四十四 | sìshísì | Falling, rising, falling |
| Is it four or ten? | 是四还是十？ | Shì sì háishi shí? | Ask when you are not sure. Show the number with your fingers |
