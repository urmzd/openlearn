# Russian Communication Guide

### Practical Phrases for Everyday Conversation and Work

**Version 1.0, November 2026**

---

## About This Guide

This guide teaches **practical communication** in standard Russian. It gives you the phrases and patterns for real conversations: with colleagues, neighbours, shop staff, drivers and friends. Every phrase sits in a table, so you can scan, cover a column and test yourself.

The guide has two goals. The first goal is casual conversation. The second goal is enough formal Russian for professional use at work.

## Who This Guide Is For

- An English-speaking adult who starts Russian from zero
- A learner who studies about 30 minutes a day
- A learner who wants casual conversation first, then meetings, email and calls at work
- A learner who prefers ready phrases and reusable patterns over long grammar theory

## What Is Inside

- **Pattern phrase templates:** Reusable structures such as "May I [action]?" with a slot you fill in
- **The case of every slot:** Each pattern names the case the slot takes, for example "[place, prepositional]"
- **Scenario phrases:** Directions, transport, restaurants, shopping, accommodation and work
- **Four-column tables:** English, Russian in Cyrillic, transliteration with stress, and usage notes
- **Politeness tiers:** Casual (ты), polite (вы) and very polite or formal forms
- **Practice tables:** Ten recall prompts at the end of every chapter
- **Cultural context:** The habits that change what you say and how you say it

## How to Use This Guide

1. **Learn the alphabet first.** Read Chapter 4 during week one. Cyrillic takes a few days, not months.
2. **Read Chapter 3 next.** Stress and vowel reduction decide if people understand you.
3. **Work through Chapter 1.** Say every phrase aloud. Learn the pattern templates by heart.
4. **Read Chapter 2 in small pieces.** Return to it when a phrase in another chapter looks strange.
5. **Pick the scenario chapter you need this week.** Chapters 5 to 9 and Chapter 11 stand alone.
6. **Finish each session with the Practice table.** Cover the right column and produce the answer from memory.

### A 30-Minute Session

| Minutes | Activity | Purpose |
|---------|----------|---------|
| 5 | Review yesterday's Practice table | Recall keeps old phrases alive |
| 10 | Read one new section aloud, twice | New input with correct stress |
| 10 | Cover the Russian and transliteration columns and say each phrase | Production from memory |
| 5 | Write five phrases in Cyrillic by hand or with the keyboard | Spelling and reading speed |

### A 12-Week Path

| Weeks | Chapters | Result |
|-------|----------|--------|
| 1 | 4, then 3 | You read Cyrillic slowly and know where stress falls |
| 2 to 3 | 1 | You greet people, ask for help and handle emergencies |
| 4 to 5 | 2 | You know the six cases, two tenses and aspect in outline |
| 6 to 8 | 5 to 9 | You manage streets, transport, food, shops and housing |
| 9 | 10 | You choose between ты and вы and use names correctly |
| 10 to 12 | 11 | You introduce yourself at work, join meetings and write short emails |

## Key Features

### Pattern Phrases (Templates)

A pattern is a sentence with a slot. The slot shows the case that the word must take.

| Pattern | Russian | Transliteration | Example |
|---------|---------|-----------------|---------|
| [thing], please | [thing, accusative], пожалуйста | [thing], pozháluysta | Воду, пожалуйста (vódu, pozháluysta) = Water, please |
| May I [action]? | Можно [infinitive]? | mózhno [infinitive] | Можно войти? (mózhno voytí) = May I come in? |
| Where is [place]? | Где [place, nominative]? | gde [place] | Где метро? (gde metró) = Where is the metro? |

### Phrase Tables

Every phrase table has the same four columns.

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| Thank you | Спасибо | spasíbo | Any situation |
| Excuse me | Извините | izviníte | Polite, to get attention or to apologise |

### Transliteration

The third column writes Russian in Latin letters. An acute accent marks the stressed vowel: **á é í ó ú ý**. Words of one syllable carry no mark. Real Russian print shows no stress marks, so the Cyrillic column shows none.

| Russian letter | Written as | Russian letter | Written as |
|----------------|------------|----------------|------------|
| ж | zh | щ | shch |
| х | kh | ы and й | y |
| ц | ts | ь | ' |
| ч | ch | ё | yo |
| ш | sh | е | e, or ye at the start of a word and after a vowel |

The transliteration follows the spelling. A few common words sound different from their spelling. Chapter 3 lists them.

### Politeness Levels

Russian has two words for "you". The choice changes the verb.

| Tier | Form | Use it with | Example | Transliteration |
|------|------|-------------|---------|-----------------|
| Casual | ты | Friends, family, children, close colleagues | Ты можешь помочь? | ty mózhesh' pomóch' |
| Polite | вы | Strangers, staff, most colleagues, anyone older | Вы можете помочь? | vy mózhete pomóch' |
| Very polite | вы plus a softened request | Managers, clients, officials, formal letters | Не могли бы вы помочь? | ne moglí by vy pomóch' |

Start with **вы** for every adult you do not know well. Chapter 10 explains how to switch to **ты**.

### Speaker Gender

Some forms change with the gender of the speaker: the past tense and short adjectives such as "glad". The tables show the masculine form. The Notes column gives the feminine form.

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| I am glad | Я рад | ya rad | Feminine: рада (ráda) |
| I understood | Я понял | ya pónyal | Feminine: поняла (ponyalá) |

## A Note on Standard Russian

This guide uses standard Russian, the form taught in schools and used in the media. Russian varies little by region compared with English. People in Moscow, Almaty, Bishkek and Tbilisi understand the same standard language. Chapter 10 lists the words that differ by city and by country.
