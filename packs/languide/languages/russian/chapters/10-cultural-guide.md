# Cultural Guide

This chapter covers the habits that change what you say in Russian: the two words for "you", names, directness, toasts and superstitions. It ends with Russian as a shared language outside Russia.

---

## Ты and Вы

Russian has two words for "you". **Ты** is for one person you are close to. **Вы** is for one person you respect or do not know, and for any group. The wrong choice is the fastest way to sound rude or cold.

### Which One to Use

| Situation | Form | Notes |
|-----------|------|-------|
| An adult you do not know | вы | Always. Age and status do not matter |
| Shop staff, waiters, drivers, officials | вы | Using ты sounds rude |
| A person clearly older than you | вы | Also after years of friendship, unless they offer ты |
| A manager or a client | вы | Often with name and patronymic |
| A colleague at your level | вы at first | Many teams move to ты in the first week. Follow the team |
| Friends, family, partners | ты | Standard |
| A child or a teenager | ты | They answer adults with вы |
| Any group of two or more | вы | The only plural form |

### What Changes in the Sentence

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| How are you? (casual) | Как у тебя дела? | kak u tebyá delá | Casual, ты |
| How are you? (polite) | Как у вас дела? | kak u vas delá | Polite, вы |
| Do you know? (casual) | Ты знаешь? | ty znáyesh' | Verb in ешь |
| Do you know? (polite) | Вы знаете? | vy znáyete | Verb in ете |
| Tell me (casual) | Скажи | skazhí | Imperative without те |
| Tell me (polite) | Скажите | skazhíte | Imperative with те |
| your phone (casual) | твой телефон | tvoy telefón | твой, твоя, твоё, твои |
| your phone (polite) | ваш телефон | vash telefón | ваш, ваша, ваше, ваши |
| Hi (casual) | Привет | privét | Only with ты |
| Hello (polite) | Здравствуйте | zdrávstvuyte | With вы |

### How to Switch

The older or more senior person offers ты. After the switch, do not go back to вы. Going back signals a quarrel.

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| Shall we use ты? | Давайте на ты? | daváyte na ty | The standard offer |
| Maybe we can switch to ты? | Может, перейдём на ты? | mózhet, pereydyóm na ty | A softer offer |
| May I use ты with you? | Можно на ты? | mózhno na ty | Short offer |
| Yes, of course | Да, конечно | da, konéchno | Accepting |
| Let's do it | Давай | daváy | Accepting, already in the ты form |
| I am more comfortable with вы | Мне удобнее на вы | mne udóbneye na vy | A polite refusal |
| Do we use ты or вы? | Мы на ты или на вы? | my na ty íli na vy | When you have forgotten |
| Is it all right that I use ты? | Ничего, что я на ты? | nichegó, chto ya na ty | Checking after a slip |

- In formal letters to one person, Вы and Ваш take a capital letter.
- Ты with a first name and вы with a name and patronymic are the two stable combinations.
- Using ты to a stranger has its own verb, тыкать (týkat'). It names a rude habit.

### How to Address People

| Person | Address | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|--------|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| A manager, a client, an official, a doctor, a teacher | Name and patronymic, with вы | Здравствуйте, Иван Петрович | zdrávstvuyte, Iván Petróvich | The respectful standard |

### Asking About Names

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| How should I address you? | Как к вам обращаться? | kak k vam obrashchát'sya | The best question when you are not sure |
| What is your patronymic? | Как ваше отчество? | kak váshe ótchestvo | Polite |

- A foreigner has no patronymic. People use your first name with вы.
- In fast speech patronymics shrink. Иван Иванович sounds like "Iván Iványch". Understand it, but say the full form.
- Use the first name and patronymic together or not at all. The patronymic alone is very familiar.

## Directness and What Counts as Polite

Russian politeness sits in the grammar: вы, the word пожалуйста and the right name. It does not sit in long, soft sentences. Short requests are normal and polite.

### Habits That Surprise English Speakers

- **A direct "no" is normal.** Нет is an answer, not an insult.
- **People do not smile at strangers by default.** A smile shows real warmth. A neutral face is not unfriendly.
- **"How are you?" is a real question.** People ask it of friends and expect a true answer. Do not use it as a greeting to a stranger.
- **Opinions are stated plainly.** Disagreement at work is open and is not personal.
- **Apologies are short.** Say извините once. Repeated apologies sound nervous.
- **Requests use the imperative.** An imperative with пожалуйста is polite. English "could you possibly" has no everyday match.
- **Advice is a form of care.** Colleagues and strangers may tell you to dress warmer. Thank them.

### English Habit and Natural Russian

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| I was wondering if you could send me the file | Пришлите, пожалуйста, файл | prishlíte, pozháluysta, fayl | Polite. The short form is correct |
| Could I possibly get a coffee? | Кофе, пожалуйста | kófe, pozháluysta | Polite |
| I am afraid that might be difficult | К сожалению, не получится | k sozhaléniyu, ne polúchitsya | A clear "no" with regret |
| That is interesting, but I see it differently | Я не согласен | ya ne soglásen | Normal at work. Feminine: не согласна (ne soglásna) |
| Hi, how are you? (to a stranger) | Здравствуйте | zdrávstvuyte | Greet only. Do not ask как дела |
| Sorry, sorry, excuse me | Извините | izviníte | Once is enough |

## Toasts

At a dinner with alcohol, people do not drink without a toast. A toast is a short speech that ends with за and a noun in the accusative. A guest is often asked to give one. Prepare one sentence.

### Toast Pattern

| Pattern | Russian | Transliteration | Example |
|---------|---------|-----------------|---------|
| To [thing] | За [thing, accusative] | za [thing] | За дружбу (za drúzhbu) = To friendship |
| Let's drink to [thing] | Давайте выпьем за [thing, accusative] | daváyte výp'yem za [thing] | Давайте выпьем за успех проекта (daváyte výp'yem za uspékh proyékta) = Let's drink to the success of the project |
| I want to raise a toast to [thing] | Я хочу поднять тост за [thing, accusative] | ya khochú podnyát' tost za [thing] | Я хочу поднять тост за нашу команду (ya khochú podnyát' tost za náshu komándu) = I want to raise a toast to our team |

### Common Toasts

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| To our meeting | За встречу | za vstréchu | Often the first toast |
| To getting acquainted | За знакомство | za znakómstvo | With new people |
| To health | За здоровье | za zdoróv'ye | The classic. Do not say на здоровье as a toast |
| To you | За вас | za vas | To the person or group you face |
| To the hosts | За хозяев | za khozyáyev | A guest says it |
| To friendship | За дружбу | za drúzhbu | Standard |
| To success | За успех | za uspékh | At work events |
| To love | За любовь | za lyubóv' | Weddings and late toasts |
| To our team | За нашу команду | za náshu komándu | At work events |
| To those who are not with us | За тех, кого нет с нами | za tekh, kogó net s námi | Serious. People do not clink glasses |
| Cheers | Будем | búdem | Short and casual, literally "we will be" |
| Bottoms up | До дна | do dna | Casual |

### Declining a Drink

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| I do not drink | Я не пью | ya ne p'yu | Clear and accepted |
| I am driving | Я за рулём | ya za rulyóm | The strongest excuse |
| I cannot, for health reasons | Мне нельзя | mne nel'zyá | No more questions follow |
| Juice for me, please | Мне сок, пожалуйста | mne sok, pozháluysta | Join the toast with any drink |

- Wait for the toast before you drink.
- Look at people when you clink glasses.
- На здоровье answers "thank you" for food. It is not a toast.

## Superstitions That Change What You Say

Many people follow these customs without believing in them. They are part of polite behaviour.

| Situation | Russian | Transliteration | What to do |
|-----------|---------|-----------------|------------|
| Someone goes to an exam or an interview | Ни пуха ни пера | ni púkha ni perá | This is the wish for luck. It means "neither fur nor feather" |
| You receive that wish | К чёрту | k chyórtu | This is the only correct reply. Never answer with спасибо |
| You say something hopeful about the future | Тьфу-тьфу-тьфу | t'fu-t'fu-t'fu | Say it and knock on wood, so that you do not jinx the plan |
| Someone has a birthday next week | Заранее не поздравляют | zaráneye ne pozdravlyáyut | "People do not congratulate in advance." Wait for the day |
| Before the New Year | С наступающим | s nastupáyushchim | The one greeting you may say early: "Happy coming holiday" |
| You greet someone at their door | Через порог не здороваются | chérez poróg ne zdoróvayutsya | Do not shake hands or pass things across the threshold. Step in first |
| Before a long journey | Присядем на дорожку | prisyádem na dorózhku | Everyone sits in silence for a moment, then leaves |
| Someone whistles indoors | Не свисти, денег не будет | ne svistí, déneg ne búdet | "Do not whistle, or there will be no money." |
| Someone sneezes | Будьте здоровы | búd'te zdoróvy | Always say it |
| Someone comes out of a bath or sauna | С лёгким паром | s lyógkim párom | A fixed greeting: "with light steam" |
| Someone leaves on a trip | Счастливого пути | schastlívogo putí | "Have a good journey" |
| You talk about a hoped result | Дай бог | day bog | "God willing." Non-religious people say it too |
| You talk about a bad possibility | Не дай бог | ne day bog | "God forbid" |
| A problem ends well | Слава богу | sláva bógu | "Thank God" |

### The Word "Last"

Pilots, climbers and many office workers avoid последний (poslédniy), "last", because it can mean "final". They say крайний (kráyniy), "outermost". You will hear крайний раз for "last time". Standard Russian uses последний. Understand крайний, and use последний in formal speech.

## Russian as a Shared Language

Russian works as a common language in much of Central Asia and the Caucasus. People from different countries use it with each other. Its status and its welcome differ by country and by generation.

### Where and How Much

| Country | Status of Russian | What to expect |
|---------|-------------------|----------------|
| Kazakhstan | Used officially alongside Kazakh | Very common in cities. Many people speak it as a first language |
| Kyrgyzstan | Official language alongside Kyrgyz | Very common in Bishkek, less in villages |
| Uzbekistan | No official status | Common in Tashkent and among older people. Younger people may prefer Uzbek or English |
| Tajikistan | Named in the constitution as the language between ethnic groups | Common in Dushanbe and in offices |
| Turkmenistan | No official status | Limited. Mostly the capital and older people |
| Armenia | No official status | Widely understood. Many people speak it well |
| Azerbaijan | No official status | Common in Baku |
| Georgia | No official status | Older people often speak it. Many younger people prefer English |

### Etiquette for Using Russian Abroad

- Ask first. Do not assume that a person wants to speak Russian.
- Learn "hello" and "thank you" in the local language. People value it.
- Use вы with strangers and elders. Respect for age is stronger than in Russia.
- If a person answers in English, continue in English.

| Is English or Russian better for you? | Вам удобнее по-английски или по-русски? | vam udóbneye po-anglíyski íli po-rússki | Gives a choice |
