# Core Communication Phrases

This chapter gives the phrases that every other chapter builds on. It starts with **pattern phrases** (templates with slots) and continues with **fixed phrases** (ready expressions). Say each phrase aloud. The stressed vowel carries an accent in the transliteration.

---

## Three Levels of Politeness

Russian marks politeness with the word for "you" and with the shape of the request. Learn one request at all three levels. Then apply the same logic everywhere.

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| Help me (casual) | Помоги, пожалуйста | pomogí, pozháluysta | Casual, ты: friends, family |
| Please help me (polite) | Помогите, пожалуйста | pomogíte, pozháluysta | Polite, вы: the default with adults |
| Could you help me? (very polite) | Не могли бы вы мне помочь? | ne moglí by vy mne pomóch' | Very polite: managers, officials, strangers when you ask a favour |
| Can you repeat? (casual) | Можешь повторить? | mózhesh' povtorít' | Casual, ты |
| Can you repeat? (polite) | Вы можете повторить? | vy mózhete povtorít' | Polite, вы |
| Could you repeat? (very polite) | Вы не могли бы повторить? | vy ne moglí by povtorít' | Very polite |

- **Casual (ты):** verb forms end in -ешь or -ишь, imperatives have no -те.
- **Polite (вы):** verb forms end in -ете or -ите, imperatives end in -те.
- **Very polite:** не могли бы вы plus an infinitive. A name and patronymic makes it more formal still.
- **пожалуйста** means "please" and "you are welcome". It sounds like "pozhálusta".

## Pattern Phrase Templates

Each pattern has a slot in `[brackets]`. The Russian column names the case that the slot takes. Chapter 2 explains the cases.

### Requesting Something

| Pattern | Russian | Transliteration | Example |
|---------|---------|-----------------|---------|
| [thing], please | [thing, accusative], пожалуйста | [thing], pozháluysta | Воду, пожалуйста (vódu, pozháluysta) = Water, please |
| Give me [thing], please | Дайте, пожалуйста, [thing, accusative] | dáyte, pozháluysta, [thing] | Дайте, пожалуйста, меню (dáyte, pozháluysta, menyú) = The menu, please |
| For me [thing], please | Мне, пожалуйста, [thing, accusative] | mne, pozháluysta, [thing] | Мне, пожалуйста, кофе (mne, pozháluysta, kófe) = A coffee for me, please |
| I would like [thing] | Я хотел бы [thing, accusative] | ya khotél by [thing] | Я хотел бы чай (ya khotél by chay) = I would like tea. Feminine: хотела бы (khotéla by) |
| I need [thing] | Мне нужен [thing, nominative] | mne núzhen [thing] | Мне нужен врач (mne núzhen vrach) = I need a doctor. Feminine noun: нужна (nuzhná). Neuter: нужно (núzhno). Plural: нужны (nuzhný) |
| I want to [action] | Я хочу [infinitive] | ya khochú [infinitive] | Я хочу заплатить (ya khochú zaplatít') = I want to pay |
| Please [action] | [imperative, вы form], пожалуйста | [imperative], pozháluysta | Повторите, пожалуйста (povtoríte, pozháluysta) = Please repeat |
| Could you [action]? | Не могли бы вы [infinitive]? | ne moglí by vy [infinitive] | Не могли бы вы подождать? (ne moglí by vy podozhdát') = Could you wait? |

### Asking Questions

| Pattern | Russian | Transliteration | Example |
|---------|---------|-----------------|---------|
| Where is [place]? | Где [place, nominative]? | gde [place] | Где туалет? (gde tualét) = Where is the toilet? |
| Where is [place] located? | Где находится [place, nominative]? | gde nakhóditsya [place] | Где находится банк? (gde nakhóditsya bank) = Where is the bank located? |
| How do I walk to [place]? | Как пройти к [place, dative]? | kak proytí k [place] | Как пройти к вокзалу? (kak proytí k vokzálu) = How do I walk to the station? |
| How do I ride to [place]? | Как доехать до [place, genitive]? | kak doyékhat' do [place] | Как доехать до центра? (kak doyékhat' do tséntra) = How do I get to the centre? |
| Do you have [thing]? | У вас есть [thing, nominative]? | u vas yest' [thing] | У вас есть вай-фай? (u vas yest' vay-fáy) = Do you have Wi-Fi? |
| Is there [thing] here? | Здесь есть [thing, nominative]? | zdes' yest' [thing] | Здесь есть банкомат? (zdes' yest' bankomát) = Is there a cash machine here? |
| How much is [thing]? | Сколько стоит [thing, nominative]? | skól'ko stóit [thing] | Сколько стоит билет? (skól'ko stóit bilét) = How much is a ticket? |
| What is [word]? | Что такое [word, nominative]? | chto takóye [word] | Что такое сырники? (chto takóye sýrniki) = What are syrniki? |
| When does [place] open? | Когда открывается [place, nominative]? | kogdá otkryváyetsya [place] | Когда открывается аптека? (kogdá otkryváyetsya aptéka) = When does the pharmacy open? |

## Emergencies

In Russia the single emergency number is **112**. An ambulance also answers on **103** and the police on **102**. Check the numbers for the country you are in.

| Stop! | Стойте! | stóyte | Command to a person |
