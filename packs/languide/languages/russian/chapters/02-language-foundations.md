# Language Foundations: Quick Reference

This chapter gives the minimum grammar behind the phrases in this guide. Read one section per session. Return to it when a word ending in another chapter surprises you.

---

## Three Facts That Save Time

### No Articles

Russian has no "a" and no "the". Context shows which one English needs.

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| This is a bank | Это банк | éto bank | No article |
| Where is the manager? | Где менеджер? | gde ménedzher | No article |
| I need a ticket | Мне нужен билет | mne núzhen bilét | No article |

### No Present Tense "To Be"

Russian leaves out "am", "is" and "are" in the present tense. In writing, a dash often stands between two nouns where English has "is". After a pronoun, nothing stands there.

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| I am an engineer | Я инженер | ya inzhenér | Pronoun plus noun |
| She is at home | Она дома | oná dóma | Pronoun plus place |
| This is my colleague | Это мой коллега | éto moy kolléga | это means "this is" |
| The office is closed | Офис закрыт | ófis zakrýt | Noun plus short adjective |
| I was at work | Я был на работе | ya byl na rabóte | The past needs был. Feminine: была (bylá) |
| I will be at work | Я буду на работе | ya búdu na rabóte | The future needs буду |

### Flexible Word Order

The endings show who does what, so the word order can move. The neutral order is subject, verb, object. New or important information goes to the end of the sentence.

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| Anna is reading the report | Анна читает отчёт | Ánna chitáyet otchyót | Neutral order |
| It is Anna who is reading the report | Отчёт читает Анна | otchyót chitáyet Ánna | Stress on "Anna" |
| I love you | Я тебя люблю | ya tebyá lyublyú | Pronoun objects often go before the verb |

## Gender of Nouns

Every noun is **masculine**, **feminine** or **neuter**. The last letter of the dictionary form shows the gender. Adjectives, the past tense and the word "one" agree with it.

### Finding the Gender

| Dictionary form ends in | Gender | Russian | Transliteration | Meaning |
|-------------------------|--------|---------|-----------------|---------|
| A consonant or й | Masculine | стол, музей | stol, muzéy | table, museum |
| а or я | Feminine | карта, неделя | kárta, nedélya | card, week |
| о or е | Neuter | окно, море | oknó, móre | window, sea |
| ь | Masculine or feminine | день, ночь | den', noch' | day (masculine), night (feminine) |
| мя | Neuter | время, имя | vrémya, ímya | time, name |

### Common Exceptions

| English | Russian | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|---------|-----------------|-------|
| dad | папа | pápa | Masculine, though it ends in а |
| man | мужчина | muzhchína | Masculine, though it ends in а |
| colleague | коллега | kolléga | Masculine or feminine, by the person |
| coffee | кофе | kófe | Masculine. It never changes its ending |
| metro | метро | metró | Neuter. It never changes its ending |
| taxi | такси | taksí | Neuter. It never changes its ending |
| cafe | кафе | kafé | Neuter. It never changes its ending |

## The Six Cases

A case is an ending that shows the job of a noun in the sentence. English does this with word order and with prepositions. Each case has one main job. Learn that job first.

### One Job Each

| Case | Russian name | Transliteration | Main job | Example |
|------|--------------|-----------------|----------|---------|
| Nominative | именительный | imenítel'nyy | The subject. The dictionary form | Офис здесь (ófis zdes') = The office is here |
| Genitive | родительный | rodítel'nyy | "Of", absence, and quantity | Нет времени (net vrémeni) = There is no time |
| Dative | дательный | dátel'nyy | The receiver: "to" or "for" a person | Я пишу коллеге (ya pishú kollége) = I am writing to a colleague |
| Accusative | винительный | vinítel'nyy | The direct object, and the destination | Я читаю книгу (ya chitáyu knígu) = I am reading a book |
| Instrumental | творительный | tvorítel'nyy | The tool, "with", and your job | Чай с молоком (chay s molokóm) = Tea with milk |
| Prepositional | предложный | predlózhnyy | The location, and "about" | Я в офисе (ya v ófise) = I am in the office |

|------|-----------|-----------------|----------|-----------------|--------|-----------------|
| Nominative | телефоны | telefóny | карты | kárty | блюда | blyúda |

| Dative | телефонам | telefónam | картам | kártam | блюдам | blyúdam |

- **Living beings:** A masculine noun for a person or animal takes the genitive ending in the accusative: Я знаю Ивана (ya znáyu Ivána) = I know Ivan.
- **Soft endings:** Nouns in я, ь and й take soft versions of the same endings: неделя, недели, неделю (nedélya, nedéli, nedélyu).
- **Spelling rule:** After г, к, х, ж, ч, ш, щ write и, not ы: книги (knígi) = books.
- **Moving stress:** In many nouns the stress moves between forms: вода, воду (vodá, vódu). The tables in this guide show the stress of each form.

The past tense agrees with the gender and number of the subject. Take the infinitive, remove ть, and add л, ла, ло or ли.

| говорить (govorít') | to speak | говорил (govoríl) | говорила (govoríla) | говорило (govorílo) | говорили (govoríli) |

## Aspect

Most English verbs match a **pair** of Russian verbs. The **imperfective** verb shows a process, a habit or a repeated action. It answers "What were you doing?" The **perfective** verb shows one complete action with a result. It answers "What did you get done?" Я писал отчёт (ya pisál otchyót) means "I was writing the report". Я написал отчёт (ya napisál otchyót) means "I wrote the report and it is finished".

The choice follows three rules. Use the imperfective for the present tense, for "usually" and "often", and for "how long". Use the perfective for one finished action in the past and for one planned result in the future. A perfective verb has no present tense. Its present-style forms mean the future: я напишу (ya napishú) = I will write.

| to read | читать | chitát' | прочитать | prochitát' |

| to say, to speak | говорить | govorít' | сказать | skazát' |
| to buy | покупать | pokupát' | купить | kupít' |
