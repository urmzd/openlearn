# Language Foundations: Quick Reference

This chapter gives the grammar that a learner needs. A heritage speaker already uses all of it, but has no names for it. For that reader the chapter does two jobs: it names the parts, and it shows which home forms change in writing.

Tajik grammar is regular. There is no grammatical gender. There are no articles. Nouns have no case endings. Verbs carry the work.

---

## Sentence Structure

### Basic Word Order

The verb goes last. The order is **subject, time, place, object, verb**.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| I read a book | Ман китоб мехонам | من کتاب می خوانم | Man kitob mekhonam | Word for word: I book read |
| He lives in Dushanbe | Ӯ дар Душанбе зиндагӣ мекунад | او در دوشنبه زندگی می کند | Ū dar Dushanbe zindagī mekunad | Ӯ means "he" and "she" |
| We go to the bazaar tomorrow | Мо фардо ба бозор меравем | ما فردا به بازار می رویم | Mo fardo ba bozor meravem | Time before place |
| I saw you | Ман туро дидам | من تو را دیدم | Man turo didam | Object before verb |
| The bread is hot | Нон гарм аст | نان گرم است | Non garm ast | "Is" goes last |
| I do not know | Намедонам | نمی دانم | Namedonam | The subject is dropped. The ending shows it |

Key points:

- The verb ending shows the person. The pronoun is optional: "Меравам" is a full sentence.
- Adjectives follow the noun: нони гарм, "bread hot".
- Prepositions come before the noun: ба бозор, "to bazaar".

### Questions

A question keeps the same word order. The voice rises at the end. The question word stays where the answer will be.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| Are you coming? | Шумо меоед? | شما می آیید؟ | Shumo meoyed? | Intonation only |
| Are you coming? | Оё шумо меоед? | آیا شما می آیید؟ | Oyo shumo meoyed? | Written. Оё opens a yes or no question |
| Where are you going? | Шумо ба куҷо меравед? | شما به کجا می روید؟ | Shumo ba kujo meraved? | Question word before the verb |
| What do you want? | Шумо чӣ мехоҳед? | شما چه می خواهید؟ | Shumo chī mekhohed? |  |
| Who came? | Кӣ омад? | کی آمد؟ | Kī omad? |  |
| When does it begin? | Кай сар мешавад? | کی سر می شود؟ | Kay sar meshavad? | Iran: کی شروع می شود؟ (key shoru' mishavad?) |
| Is it not so? | Ҳамин тавр не? | همین طور نه؟ | Hamin tavr ne? | Tag question. Iran: مگر نه؟ (magar na?) |

## The Izofat Link

**Izofat** (изофа, اضافه) is a short unstressed **-и** that links a noun to the word that describes it. It links noun to adjective, noun to owner and noun to name. A heritage speaker says it in every sentence.

- Cyrillic always writes it: **-и**.
- Persian script usually does not write it. The reader adds it from the meaning.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| my book | китоби ман | کتاب من | kitobi man | Noun plus owner. Nothing written in Persian script |
| hot bread | нони гарм | نان گرم | noni garm | Noun plus adjective |
| the Tajik language | забони тоҷикӣ | زبان تاجیکی | zaboni tojikī |  |
| your name | номи шумо | نام شما | nomi shumo |  |
| the city of Dushanbe | шаҳри Душанбе | شهر دوشنبه | shahri Dushanbe | Noun plus name |
| my good friend | дӯсти хуби ман | دوست خوب من | dūsti khubi man | A chain: two links |
| a big house | хонаи калон | خانه ی کلان | khonai kalon | After final ه Persian writes ی or a small sign above the ه, or nothing |
| the Vakhsh river | дарёи Вахш | دریای وخش | daryoi Vakhsh | After ا Persian writes ی |
| a place of work | ҷойи кор | جای کار | joyi kor |  |
| a red fish | моҳии сурх | ماهی سرخ | mohii surkh | Final ӣ becomes и before the link: моҳӣ, моҳии |

Rules for the Persian script:

| Word ends in | Persian script writes | Example | Read as |
|--------------|-----------------------|---------|---------|
| A consonant | Nothing | کتاب من | kitobi man |
| ا or و | ی | هوای گرم | havoi garm |
| Silent ه | ی after a space, or a small sign above the ه, or nothing | خانه ی من | khonai man |
| ی | Nothing | ماهی سرخ | mohii surkh |

## The Object Marker -ро

**-ро** (را) marks a specific direct object: "the book", "this one", "him". An unspecific object takes no marker.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| I read the book | Ман китобро хондам | من کتاب را خواندم | Man kitobro khondam | A specific book |
| I read a book | Ман китоб хондам | من کتاب خواندم | Man kitob khondam | Any book. No marker |
| I know him | Ӯро мешиносам | او را می شناسم | Ūro meshinosam |  |
| Forgive me | Маро бубахшед | مرا ببخشید | Maro bubakhshed | ман plus -ро is маро |
| I want this one | Инро мехоҳам | این را می خواهم | Inro mekhoham |  |
| Close the door | Дарро бандед | در را بندید | Darro banded |  |
| I saw you | Шуморо дидам | شما را دیدم | Shumoro didam |  |

- Cyrillic joins -ро to the word: китобро. Persian script writes را as a separate word: کتاب را.
- Spoken Tajik shortens -ро to -а after a consonant and -ра or -я after a vowel: китоба, хонара. Always write -ро.

### Attached Pronouns

A short ending on a noun means "my, your, his". It replaces the izofat phrase: китоби ман and китобам both mean "my book".

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|

| your book (casual) | китобат | کتابت | kitobat | -ат |
| his book, her book | китобаш | کتابش | kitobash | -аш |
| our book | китобамон | کتابمان | kitobamon | -амон |

| their book | китобашон | کتابشان | kitobashon | -ашон |

### The Prefix ме-

**ме-** (می) marks an action that continues or repeats.

- With the present stem it makes the present and the near future: меравам, "I go, I will go".
- With the past stem it makes the past continuous: мерафтам, "I was going, I used to go".
- Cyrillic joins it to the verb. Persian script writes می as a separate piece: می روم.
- The verbs доштан (to have) and будан (to be) take no ме- in the present: дорам, ҳастам.

### Negation

The prefix **на-** (ن) goes at the front of the verb. It takes the stress.

Three rules cover most cases:

- **Write the full ending.** мерам is меравам, мегам is мегӯям, мешад is мешавад.
- **Write -ро, -ҳо, аст and нест in full.** Speech cuts all four.
- **Put the preposition in front.** бозорба is ба бозор, хонада is дар хона, ман катӣ is бо ман.

## Tajik Versus Iranian Persian Grammar

The grammar is the same in its base. The table shows the points where a Tajik speaker and an Iranian speaker build a sentence in different ways. Iranian forms marked "Spoken" belong to everyday Tehran speech, which is far from written Persian.

| Feature | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Iranian Persian |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| Action in progress | Ман рафта истодаам | من رفته ایستاده ام | Man rafta istodaam | دارم می روم (dāram miravam) |
| Ability | Ман рафта метавонам | من رفته می توانم | Man rafta metavonam | می توانم بروم (mitavānam beravam) |
| Second verb after "want" | Мехоҳам равам | می خواهم روم | Mekhoham ravam | می خواهم بروم (mikhāham beravam). Iran needs the prefix be- |
| Polite command | Нависед | نویسید | Navised | بنویسید (benevisid). Iran needs be- |
| Object marker in speech | Китобро гирифтам | کتاب را گرفتم | Kitobro giriftam | Spoken: کتابو گرفتم (ketābo gereftam) |
| "Is" in speech | Ин хуб аст | این خوب است | In khub ast | Spoken: این خوبه (in khube) |
| Plural in speech | китобҳо | کتاب ها | kitobho | Spoken: کتابا (ketābā) |
| Comparison | Ӯ аз ман дида калонтар аст | او از من دیده کلانتر است | Ū az man dida kalontar ast | او از من بزرگتر است (u az man bozorgtar ast). Iran has no دیده here |
| He, she in speech | вай | وی | vay | Spoken: اون (un). Written: او (u) |
| Result state in speech | Вай рафтагӣ | وی رفتگی | Vay raftagī | او رفته است (u rafte ast). Iran has no -gi form |
| Reported fact ("I hear that") | Вай бемор будааст | وی بیمار بوده است | Vay bemor budaast | می گویند مریض است (miguyand mariz ast) |
| Place and verb order in speech | Ба хона меравам | به خانه می روم | Ba khona meravam | Spoken: می رم خونه (miram khune). The place can follow the verb |
| "A, one" in speech | як китоб | یک کتاب | yak kitob | Spoken: یه کتاب (ye ketāb) |
| Long a before n and m | хона, нон | خانه، نان | khona, non | Spoken: خونه، نون (khune, nun) |
| Verb endings in speech | меравед, мераванд | می روید، می روند | meraved, meravand | Spoken: می رید، می رن (mirid, miran) |
| Yes or no question in speech | Рафтӣ? | رفتی؟ | Raftī? | رفتی؟ (rafti?). Northern Tajik speech can add -мӣ. Iran never does |

For a heritage speaker: written Tajik and written Iranian Persian are close. Spoken Tajik and spoken Tehran Persian are far apart. Read Iranian text first, and listen to Iranian speech later.
