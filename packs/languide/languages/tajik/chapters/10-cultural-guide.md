# Cultural Guide

This chapter covers the differences that affect speech: Tajik versus Iranian Persian versus Dari, formality, hospitality formulas, Russian and Uzbek influence, and how to be understood across borders.

A heritage speaker needs this chapter for one reason above all. The Tajik you speak at home is correct Tajik. It is also full of words that an Iranian or an Afghan does not use. This chapter shows which words to change, and when.

---

## Three Standards of One Language

| Point | Tajik | Iranian Persian | Dari |
|-------|-------|-----------------|------|
| Country | Tajikistan | Iran | Afghanistan |
| Name used by speakers | тоҷикӣ | فارسی (fārsi) | دری (dari), فارسی (fārsi) |
| Script | Cyrillic | Persian script | Persian script |
| Main source of modern loans | Russian, Uzbek | French, English | English, Pashto |
| Vowels | Six. Keeps е and ӯ apart | Six. Has merged them | Eight. Keeps all old vowels |
| қ and ғ | Two sounds | One sound | Two sounds |
| Everyday word for "thanks" | раҳмат | مرسی (mersi) | تشکر (tashakkor) |
| Closest relative | Dari | Dari in writing | Tajik in speech |

Rule of thumb: a Tajik speaker and a Kabul speaker understand each other in a few minutes. A Tajik speaker and a Tehran speaker need a few days of listening. In writing, all three are close.

## Formality: ту and шумо

| Situation | Use | Note |
|-----------|-----|------|
| Stranger of any age | шумо | Always |
| Older person, also in the family | шумо | Many Tajik families use шумо for parents and grandparents |
| Colleague, client, official | шумо | Always at work |
| Close friend of the same age | ту | After the friendship is clear |
| Child | ту | |
| Husband and wife | ту or шумо | Both are common. It depends on the family |
| Speaking to God, in poetry | ту | |

Rules:

- Start with шумо. Wait for the other person to move to ту.
- A heritage speaker who uses ту at home with brothers and sisters must switch to шумо outside the home.
- In Iran the rule is the same, and a shop assistant or driver is always شما.
- Never use ту with a person older than you unless that person asks for it.

How the greeting runs in practice:

1. The younger person or the person who arrives greets first.
2. Each side asks two or three questions: health, family, work.
3. The answers are short and positive. "Шукр" is enough.
4. Business starts after this exchange. To skip it is rude.

## Hospitality Formulas and Taarof

### In Tajikistan

A guest is honoured. The host offers the best food and the best seat, far from the door. The guest must not refuse everything and must not accept at once.

### Taarof in Iran

**Taarof** (تعارف) is the Iranian system of ritual politeness. Both sides offer more than they mean and refuse more than they mean. A Tajik speaker knows the idea from home, but Iran takes it further.

| Iran says | Reading | Word for word | Real meaning | Tajik equivalent |
|-----------|---------|---------------|--------------|------------------|
| بفرمایید | befarmāyid | Command, please | Go ahead, come in, sit, eat, after you | марҳамат |
| قابل ندارد | ghābel nadārad | It is not worthy of you | Said by a seller or driver when you ask the price. You must still pay | no equivalent |
| خواهش می کنم | khāhesh mikonam | I request | You are welcome | намеарзад |
| قربان شما | ghorbān-e shomā | Your sacrifice | Thank you, goodbye | саломат бошед |
| شرمنده | sharmande | Ashamed | Sorry, or thank you very much | бубахшед |
| چشم | chashm | Eye | Yes, I will do it | хуб шудааст |
| مزاحم نمی شوم | mozāhem nemishavam | I will not be a nuisance | A polite refusal of an invitation | ташвиш намедиҳам |
| ناقابل است | nāghābel ast | It is unworthy | Said when giving a gift | ночиз аст |
| قدم شما روی چشم | ghadam-e shomā ru-ye chashm | Your step on my eye | You are very welcome | хуш омадед |
| تعارف نکنید | ta'ārof nakonid | Do not do taarof | Please accept, I mean it | такаллуф накунед |

Rules for taarof:

- **Refuse an offer once or twice before you accept.** Food, tea, a seat, a lift home.
- **Offer three times.** If the other person refuses three times, the refusal is real.
- **Always try to pay.** When a driver or seller says قابل ندارد, thank them and pay the full price.
- **An invitation made at the door is often taarof.** "Come in for dinner" at the end of a chance meeting is not a real plan.
- **Do not take compliments on objects too far.** The owner may feel bound to offer the object.

How to handle code switching:

- **At home:** speak as your family speaks. Mixed speech is normal Tajik.
- **In writing:** use the Tajik word. Formal Tajik avoids Russian loans where a Tajik word exists.
- **With Iranians and Afghans:** remove the Russian words. They do not know them. Use the word from the Tajik column.
- **Numbers and dates:** many speakers say prices, years and phone numbers in Russian. Practise them in Tajik with chapter 2.
- **Names:** many Tajik surnames end in -ов or -ова and many documents show a father's name in -ович or -овна. Newer names use -зода, -ӣ or -иён.

## Uzbek Influence

Tajiks and Uzbeks have lived in the same cities for centuries. Northern Tajik dialects in particular share words and some grammar with Uzbek. These words are normal Tajik. An Iranian will not know most of them.

Grammar habits of northern speech that come from contact with Uzbek:

- The question particle **-мӣ**: Рафтӣ-мӣ? Written Tajik: Оё рафтӣ?
- The word **катӣ** after the noun for "with": ман катӣ. Written Tajik: бо ман.
- The endings **-ба** and **-да** after the noun for "to" and "in": бозорба, хонада. Written Tajik: ба бозор, дар хона.

## Etiquette That Affects Speech

| Rule | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Greet first** | The younger person greets the older one. A hand on the heart goes with the greeting between men, and between a man and a woman who do not shake hands. |
| **Ask about the family** | Health, parents and children come before any business. |
| **Respect the bread** | Do not put bread face down or on the ground. Break it by hand. |
| **Shoes off** | Take off your shoes at the door of a home. Ask "Пойафзолро дар куҷо кашам?" |
| **Tea** | The host pours a little tea into the bowl. A full bowl can mean "drink up and go". |
| **The prayer after the meal** | The oldest person says a short prayer. All say "Омин" and pass both hands over the face. |
| **Titles in Iran** | Use آقا (āghā) or خانم (khānom) with the family name. First names are for friends. |
| **Avoid** | Comparing which country speaks "real" Persian. Each standard is a source of pride. |
