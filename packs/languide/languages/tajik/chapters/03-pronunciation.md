# Pronunciation Guide

Tajik spelling in Cyrillic follows the sound closely. One letter has one sound, with few exceptions. This chapter explains the Latin transliteration used in the guide, the six vowels, the consonants and the stress.

It has one section for each reader. The learner section covers the sounds that English lacks. The heritage speaker section explains how Iranian and Afghan speech sounds different from Tajik, and why.

---

## The Transliteration Scheme

The Transliteration column shows Tajik pronunciation. It follows the Cyrillic letter by letter.

### Vowels

| Cyrillic | Latin | Sound |
|----------|-------|-------|
| а | a | Like "a" in "father", but short |
| о | o | Like "o" in "more". Rounded and long |
| е, э | e | Like "e" in "bed", but longer. At the start of a word and after a vowel, е is "ye" |
| и | i | Like "i" in "machine". Often very short when it has no stress |
| ӣ | ī | The same sound as и, long and stressed. Only at the end of a word |
| у | u | Like "oo" in "moon". Often very short when it has no stress |
| ӯ | ū | Between "o" and "u", with rounded lips. Close to the vowel of French "peu" in the north |
| ё | yo | й plus о |
| ю | yu | й plus у |
| я | ya | й plus а |

### Consonants That Need a Note

| Cyrillic | Latin | Sound |
|----------|-------|-------|
| ғ | gh | A voiced sound at the back of the throat, like the French "r" in "Paris" |
| қ | q | A "k" made far back in the throat |
| х | kh | Like "ch" in Scottish "loch" or German "Bach" |
| ҳ | h | Like English "h". It is also spoken at the end of a syllable |
| ҷ | j | Like "j" in "jam" |
| ч | ch | Like "ch" in "chair" |
| ш | sh | Like "sh" in "ship" |
| ж | zh | Like "s" in "measure". Rare |
| й | y | Like "y" in "yes" |
| ъ | ' | A short break in the voice, or a longer vowel before it |
| в | v | Like "v" in "voice" |
| р | r | A short tap of the tongue, as in Spanish "pero" |

The other consonants are б (b), г (g), д (d), з (z), к (k), л (l), м (m), н (n), п (p), с (s), т (t), ф (f). They sound close to English. Say т, д and н with the tongue on the teeth.

Notes on the scheme:

- **sh, kh, gh, zh and ch are single sounds.** Each one stands for one Cyrillic letter.
- A few words have с, к, г or з next to ҳ, for example изҳор (izhor). The Latin then looks like one of the pairs above. The Cyrillic is always clear.
- **Iranian forms** in the Notes column use Iranian values: ā for the long "a" of Iran, and e and o for the short vowels. Example: Iran: نان (nān). Both ق and غ are written gh in Iranian forms, because Iran says them the same way.
- **Dari forms** use ā, and keep q and gh apart. Example: Dari: کلان (kalān).

## The Six Tajik Vowels

Tajik has six vowel sounds: и, е, а, о, у, ӯ. The letters э, ӣ, ё, ю and я are spelling variants, not new vowels.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| milk | шир | شیر | shir | и: lips spread, tongue high and front |
| lion | шер | شیر | sher | е: like "e" in "bed", held longer |
| door, in | дар | در | dar | а: short and open |
| bread | нон | نان | non | о: lips rounded, like "o" in "more" |
| far | дур | دور | dur | у: lips rounded, tongue high and back |
| day | рӯз | روز | rūz | ӯ: lips rounded, tongue in the middle |

### Stable and Unstable Vowels

| Group | Vowels | Behaviour |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| Stable | е, о, ӯ | Always full and clear, with or without stress |
| Unstable | и, у, а | Short. They become very short, or nearly vanish, in an open syllable with no stress |

Examples of unstable vowels in speech:

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| book | китоб | کتاب | kitob | Sounds like "ktob". The и is very short |
| brother | бародар | برادر | barodar | The first а is very short |
| you | шумо | شما | shumo | Sounds like "shmo" in fast speech |
| to hear | шунидан | شنیدن | shunidan | The у is very short |
| grandfather | бобо | بابا | bobo | Both о are stable and full |

This is the reason why the Persian script works. It writes the stable vowels and leaves out most unstable ones. Chapter 4 uses this rule.

### The Vowel ӯ

- In the north (Khujand, Samarkand, Bukhara) and in the standard language, ӯ is a separate sound.
- In much of the south (Kulob, parts of Khatlon) ӯ sounds like у. A heritage speaker from the south may say руз for рӯз.
- Write ӯ where the standard has it. Common words: рӯз (day), дӯст (friend), гӯшт (meat), кӯҳ (mountain), ӯ (he, she), бӯй (smell), рӯй (face), мӯй (hair), кӯдак (child), хӯрдан (to eat), нӯшидан (to drink), гӯш (ear), кӯча (street), зӯр (strong).
- Before ҳ and ъ a short "u" is written ӯ: мӯҳлат (deadline), шӯъба (department), кӯҳна (old), мӯҳр (seal).

## How Tajik Vowels Map to Iranian Persian

Classical Persian had eight vowels. Tajik kept six of them apart. Iranian Persian also kept six, but it merged different pairs and changed the short vowels. Dari kept all eight. The Persian script was fixed before these changes, so one spelling serves all three.

| Change | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Iran says |
|--------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-----------|
| Tajik а is Iran a | дар | در | dar | dar |
| Tajik о is Iran ā | нон | نان | non | nān. Spoken before n and m: nun |
| Tajik о is Iran ā | об | آب | ob | āb |
| Tajik short и is Iran e | дил | دل | dil | del |
| Tajik short и is Iran e | китоб | کتاب | kitob | ketāb |
| Tajik long и is Iran i | шир | شیر | shir | shir (milk) |
| Tajik е is Iran i | шер | شیر | sher | shir (lion). Same as "milk" in Iran |
| Tajik е is Iran i | мева | میوه | meva | mive |
| Tajik е before ҳ or ъ is Iran e | меҳмон | مهمان | mehmon | mehmān |
| Tajik short у is Iran o | гул | گل | gul | gol |
| Tajik short у is Iran o | шумо | شما | shumo | shomā |
| Tajik long у is Iran u | дур | دور | dur | dur |
| Tajik ӯ is Iran u | рӯз | روز | rūz | ruz |
| Tajik ӯ is Iran u | дӯст | دوست | dūst | dust |
| Tajik ӯ before ҳ or ъ is Iran o | кӯҳна | کهنه | kūhna | kohne |
| Tajik ай is Iran ey | хайр | خیر | khayr | kheyr |
| Tajik ав is Iran ow | нав | نو | nav | now |
| Tajik final а is Iran e | хона | خانه | khona | khāne. Spoken: khune |
| Tajik final а is Iran e | ҳафта | هفته | hafta | hafte |

### Distinctions That Iranian Persian Has Merged

Tajik keeps these pairs apart in sound and in Cyrillic. Iran says both words the same way. The Persian script writes both words the same way.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| lion | шер | شیر | sher | Iran: shir. Dari: shēr |
| milk | шир | شیر | shir | Iran: shir. Dari: shīr |
| full, not hungry | сер | سیر | ser | Iran: sir |
| garlic | сир | سیر | sir | Iran: sir |
| day | рӯз | روز | rūz | Iran: ruz. Dari: rōz |
| far | дур | دور | dur | Iran: dur. Dari: dūr |
| blind | кӯр | کور | kūr | Iran: kur. Dari: kōr |
| sound of у in "money" | пул | پول | pul | Iran: pul. The long у is written و |
| sound of у in "bridge" | пул | پل | pul | Iran: pol. The short у is not written |

The last two rows show the reverse case. Tajik writes "money" and "bridge" the same way. Iran says them in two ways and Persian script spells them in two ways.

## Consonants

### The Pair қ and ғ

Tajik has two different sounds. Iranian Persian says both as one sound. Dari keeps them apart, like Tajik.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| sugar cube | қанд | قند | qand | қ is ق. Iran: ghand |
| sorrow | ғам | غم | gham | ғ is غ. Iran: gham |
| mould, form | қолиб | قالب | qolib | Iran: ghāleb |
| winner | ғолиб | غالب | gholib | Iran: ghāleb. Same sound as "mould" in Iran |
| near, almost | қариб | قریب | qarib | Iran: gharib |
| stranger, poor man | ғариб | غریب | gharib | Iran: gharib |
| heart, centre | қалб | قلب | qalb | Iran: ghalb |
| garden | боғ | باغ | bogh | Iran: bāgh |

A heritage speaker has an advantage here. The Tajik sound tells you which Persian letter to write: қ is always ق, and ғ is always غ. An Iranian must learn each spelling by heart.

### The Pair ҳ and х

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| every | ҳар | هر | har | ҳ: soft, like English "h" |
| donkey | хар | خر | khar | х: rough, at the back of the mouth |
| state, condition | ҳол | حال | hol |  |
| mole on the skin | хол | خال | khol |  |
| mountain | кӯҳ | کوه | kūh | Say the final ҳ |
| nine | нӯҳ | نه | nūh | Say the final ҳ |

- Some southern dialects drop ҳ or say it as х. The written language keeps it.
- Tajik has one letter ҳ. Persian script has two letters for it: ه and ح. Chapter 4 explains how to choose.

### The Sign ъ

ъ (аломати сакта) stands for the Arabic letters ع and ء. It appears only in words of Arabic origin.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| after | баъд | بعد | ba'd | After a vowel: the vowel becomes longer |
| known, clear | маълум | معلوم | ma'lum |  |
| Friday | ҷумъа | جمعه | jum'a | After a consonant: a short break |
| problem, issue | масъала | مسئله | mas'ala |  |
| poem | шеър | شعر | she'r |  |
| verb, action | феъл | فعل | fe'l |  |
| candle | шамъ | شمع | sham' | At the end: almost silent |

At the start of a word and between two vowels, Tajik does not write ъ: илм (علم), соат (ساعت), таом (طعام), шоир (شاعر).

### Other Notes on Consonants

| Point | Explanation | Examples |
|-------|-------------|----------|
| Double consonants | Say both. The sound is held longer. | аввал (first), муаллим (teacher), ҳатто (even), албатта (of course), диққат (attention) |
| Final clusters | Tajik allows two consonants at the end of a word. Do not add a vowel between them. | дӯст (friend), гӯшт (meat), нағз (good), вақт (time), субҳ (morning) |
| в | Always "v" in Tajik and in Iran. In Dari it is "w". | вақт: Tajik vaqt, Dari waqt |
| р | A single tap. Never the English "r". | рӯз, бародар, кор |
| ж | Rare. In a few native words and in loans. | жола (hail), мижа (eyelash) |

## Stress

Stress falls on the **last syllable** of most words. Some endings never take stress. Two verb prefixes always take it.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| book | китоб | کتاب | kitob | Stress: ki-TOB |
| books | китобҳо | کتاب ها | kitobho | The plural takes the stress: kitob-HO |
| Tajik (language) | тоҷикӣ | تاجیکی | tojikī | The ending -ӣ takes the stress: tojik-Ī |
| my book | китоби ман | کتاب من | kitobi man | Izofat -и has no stress: ki-TO-bi |
| the book (object) | китобро | کتاب را | kitobro | -ро has no stress: ki-TOB-ro |
| my book | китобам | کتابم | kitobam | The attached pronoun has no stress: ki-TO-bam |
| I am Tajik | тоҷикам | تاجیکم | tojikam | The short "am" has no stress: to-JI-kam |
| I go | меравам | می روم | meravam | ме- takes the stress: ME-ravam |
| I do not go | намеравам | نمی روم | nameravam | на- takes the stress: NA-meravam |
| I went | рафтам | رفتم | raftam | The stem takes the stress: RAF-tam |
| I did not go | нарафтам | نرفتم | naraftam | NA-raftam |
| but | аммо | اما | ammo | Linking words: first syllable. AM-mo |
| yes | бале | بله | bale | BA-le |

Unstressed endings in one list:

- The izofat **-и**
- The object marker **-ро**
- The attached pronouns **-ам, -ат, -аш, -амон, -атон, -ашон**
- The short forms of "to be" **-ам, -ӣ, -ем, -ед, -анд**
- The verb endings in the past tense
- The linking **-у** and **-ю** ("and")
- The indefinite **-е**: китобе, "a book"

The final **ӣ** shows stress in spelling. Compare дӯстӣ (friendship, stress on -ӣ) with дӯсти ман (my friend, no stress on -и).

## For Learners: Sounds That English Lacks

| Sound | How to make it | Common error |
|-------|----------------|--------------|
| қ | Say "k", then move the contact point back to the soft part at the back of the mouth. No puff of air. | Saying "k". Then қанд (sugar) sounds like канд (he dug) |
| ғ | Gargle softly with voice. It is the French "r". | Saying "g" |
| х | Raise the back of the tongue and let air rub. No voice. | Saying "h" or "k" |
| ҳ at the end | Breathe out softly after the vowel: кӯҳ, даҳ, субҳ. | Dropping it |
| р | Tap the tongue once behind the upper teeth. | Using the English "r" |
| ъ | Stop the voice for a moment, as in the middle of "uh-oh". | Ignoring it |
| о | Round the lips. Keep it long. | Saying "a" as in "cat" or "o" as in "hot" |
| ӯ | Round the lips as for "o". Push the tongue forward a little. | Saying a plain "u" |
| Double consonants | Hold the consonant for two beats. | Saying one consonant |

## For Heritage Speakers: How Iranian and Afghan Speech Sounds

You will understand written Persian sooner than spoken Persian. The words are the same, but the vowels move. This section gives the pattern, so your ear can adjust.

### Five Shifts That Explain Tehran Speech

| Shift | Tajik | Tehran | Tip |
|-------|-------|--------|-----|
| о becomes ā | нон, об, кор | nān, āb, kār | Open the mouth more and remove the lip rounding |
| Short и becomes e | дил, китоб, имрӯз | del, ketāb, emruz | Every short "i" turns to "e" |
| Short у becomes o | гул, шумо, бузург | gol, shomā, bozorg | Every short "u" turns to "o" |
| е and ӯ merge into i and u | шер, рӯз | shir, ruz | Expect words to sound alike |
| қ and ғ merge | қанд, ғам | ghand, gham | One sound for both |

Spoken Tehran Persian also shortens words. These forms are not written in formal text.

| Written Persian | Tehran speech | Tajik equivalent |
|-----------------|---------------|------------------|
| است (ast) | -e | аст |
| را (rā) | -o, -ro | -ро |
| ها (hā) | -ā | -ҳо |
| آن (ān) | un | он |
| خانه (khāne) | khune | хона |
| می روم (miravam) | miram | меравам |
| می گویم (miguyam) | migam | мегӯям |
| می خواهم (mikhāham) | mikhām | мехоҳам |
| می توانم (mitavānam) | mitunam | метавонам |
| می شود (mishavad) | mishe | мешавад |

### Why Afghan Speech Is Easier

- Dari keeps е and ӯ as separate vowels, as Tajik does: shēr, rōz.
- Dari keeps қ and ғ apart.
- Dari keeps the final "a": khāna, hafta.
- Dari keeps short "i" and "u": kitāb, gul.
- Dari shares many everyday words with Tajik: کلان (big), گپ زدن (to speak), مانده (tired).

Two points differ. Dari says "ā" where Tajik says о: nān for нон. Dari says "w" where Tajik says в: waqt for вақт.
