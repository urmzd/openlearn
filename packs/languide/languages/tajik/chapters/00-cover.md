# Tajik and Persian Communication Guide

### One Language, Two Scripts: Tajik Cyrillic and Persian Script Side by Side

**Version 1.0, November 2026**

---

## About This Guide

Tajik (тоҷикӣ) and Persian (فارسی) are one language. Tajikistan writes it in Cyrillic. Iran and Afghanistan write it in the Perso-Arabic script, where it is called Persian (Farsi) and Dari. A speaker from Dushanbe, a speaker from Tehran and a speaker from Kabul can hold a conversation, but each one may be unable to read what the other writes.

This guide teaches practical communication and both scripts at the same time. Every phrase appears in Tajik Cyrillic, in Persian script and in a Latin transliteration. The Tajik form is the base form. Where Iranian Persian or Dari uses a different word, the Notes column shows it.

## One Language, Two Scripts

| Variety | Where | Script | Name used by speakers |
|---------|-------|--------|-----------------------|
| Tajik | Tajikistan, parts of Uzbekistan | Cyrillic, 35 letters, left to right | тоҷикӣ, забони тоҷикӣ |
| Iranian Persian | Iran | Perso-Arabic, 32 letters, right to left | فارسی |
| Dari | Afghanistan | Perso-Arabic, 32 letters, right to left | دری, فارسی |

What is the same in all three:

- The grammar: verb at the end, the izofat link between words, the object marker, the verb stems.
- The core vocabulary: family, numbers, body, food, time, most verbs.
- The classical literature: Rudaki, Firdavsi, Hofiz and Saadi belong to all three.

What is different:

- The script. This is the largest barrier, and chapter 4 removes it.
- The vowels. Tajik нон and Iranian نان are the same word with a different vowel colour.
- Modern loan words. Tajik borrowed from Russian and Uzbek. Iranian Persian borrowed from French. Dari borrowed from English and Pashto.
- Some everyday words and a few grammar habits. Chapters 2 and 10 list them.

## Who This Guide Is For

The guide has two readers. The first one has priority.

| Reader | Starting point | What this guide gives |
|--------|----------------|-----------------------|
| Heritage speaker | Speaks Tajik at home. Cannot read or write it. | Both alphabets, spelling rules, the written and formal register, and the differences from Iranian Persian and Dari. |
| Learner | Starts from zero. Speaks English. | Phrases by situation, pattern templates, grammar in small steps, pronunciation and both scripts. |

The goal is the same for both readers: casual conversation and professional use, with 30 minutes of study each day.

A heritage speaker already knows what most phrases in this guide mean. For that reader the phrase chapters are graded reading practice. The English column becomes the answer key, not the lesson.

## Route for Heritage Speakers

1. **Start at chapter 4.** Learn the Cyrillic alphabet first. Most heritage speakers read it in one or two weeks, because every letter has one sound.
2. **Read chapter 3, the heritage speaker section.** It explains why Iranian and Afghan speech sounds different.
3. **Read chapter 1 aloud in Cyrillic.** Cover the transliteration column. You know the phrases, so your ear corrects your reading.
4. **Go back to chapter 4, Part B.** Learn the Persian script. Plan three to four weeks.
5. **Read chapters 5 to 9 aloud, one script at a time.** First the Tajik column, then the Persian script column of the same table.
6. **Read chapter 2 for the two comparison tables.** "Spoken Tajik versus written Tajik" shows which home forms change on paper. "Tajik versus Iranian Persian grammar" shows what changes across the border.
7. **Read chapters 10 and 11 last.** They hold the formal register, the workplace vocabulary and the words that differ in Iran and Afghanistan.

## Route for Learners

1. **Start at chapter 1.** Learn the pattern templates and the greetings with the transliteration column.
2. **Read chapter 3.** Fix the sounds before bad habits form.
3. **Read chapter 2.** Learn word order, the izofat link, the object marker and one model verb.
4. **Learn the Cyrillic alphabet in chapter 4, Part A.** After this step, stop reading the transliteration column.
5. **Work through chapters 5 to 9 by need.** Pick the situation you will meet first.
6. **Learn the Persian script in chapter 4, Part B and Part C.** Then read the earlier chapters again in the Persian script column.
7. **Finish with chapters 10 and 11.**

## How to Read the Five-Column Tables

Every phrase table has the same five columns.

| English | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|-------|
| Thank you | Ташаккур | تشکر | Tashakkur | Polite. Iran: ممنون (mamnun), مرسی (mersi) |
| Where is the station? | Вокзал дар куҷост? | واکزال در کجاست؟ | Vokzal dar kujost? | Russian loan. Iran: ایستگاه (istgāh) |

| Column | What it holds |
|--------|---------------|
| English | The meaning. |
| Tajik | Standard written Tajik in Cyrillic. |
| Persian script | The same Tajik words in Persian script, with standard Persian spelling. It is not a translation into Iranian usage. |
| Transliteration | The Tajik pronunciation in Latin letters. Chapter 3 explains the scheme. |
| Notes | The register, and three kinds of flag. |

The three flags in the Notes column:

| Flag | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| Iran: | Iranian Persian uses a different word or form. The Iranian pronunciation follows in brackets. | Iran: بیمارستان (bimārestān) |
| Dari: | Afghan Persian uses a different word. The guide adds this flag only for very common words. | Dari: شفاخانه (shafākhāna) |
| Spoken: | The form that many Tajik speakers use in daily speech. It is different from the written form in the Tajik column. | Spoken: мерам |

Two spelling conventions in the Persian script column:

- Persian uses a narrow joining space inside some words, for example after the verb prefix می. This guide writes a normal space there.
- Russian loan words have no traditional Persian spelling. This guide spells them letter by letter with full vowel letters, in the way that Persian spells European loans.

## A 30-Minute Session

| Minutes | Heritage speaker | Learner |
|---------|------------------|---------|
| 0 to 5 | Review yesterday's letters or spelling rule from chapter 4. | Review yesterday's phrases aloud. |
| 5 to 15 | Read one phrase table aloud in the Tajik column. Cover the other columns. | Learn 10 new phrases from one table. Say each one three times. |
| 15 to 22 | Read the same table in the Persian script column. Check each row against the Tajik column. | Fill the slots of two pattern templates with new words. |
| 22 to 27 | Copy five phrases by hand in each script. | Copy five phrases in Cyrillic. Later, copy them in Persian script. |
| 27 to 30 | Do the Practice table at the end of the chapter. | Do the Practice table at the end of the chapter. |

Rules for every session:

- Read aloud. Silent reading does not build the link between letter and sound.
- Cover the transliteration column when you can read the Cyrillic.
- Write by hand. Handwriting fixes letter shapes faster than typing.
- Stop after 30 minutes. A short session every day works better than a long session once a week.

## Key Features

### Pattern Phrases (Templates)

Pattern phrases have slots in `[brackets]`. Change the word in the slot to make a new sentence.

| Pattern | Tajik | Persian script | Transliteration | Example |
|---------|-------|----------------|-----------------|---------|
| [thing], please | [чиз], лутфан | [چیز]، لطفاً | [chiz], lutfan | Об, лутфан = Water, please |
| Where is [place]? | [ҷой] дар куҷост? | [جای] در کجاست؟ | [joy] dar kujost? | Бозор дар куҷост? = Where is the bazaar? |
| I need [thing] | Ба ман [чиз] лозим аст | به من [چیز] لازم است | Ba man [chiz] lozim ast | Ба ман духтур лозим аст = I need a doctor |

### Politeness Levels

Each chapter marks the register of a phrase.

| Level | Marker | Use with |
|-------|--------|----------|
| Casual | ту (you, singular) | Close friends, children, younger family members |
| Polite | шумо (you, plural or respectful) | Strangers, older people, staff, colleagues. This is the default. |
| Very polite | Formal formulas and courtesy phrases | Elders, officials, guests, business letters, ceremonies |

## A Note on the Standard Used

The Tajik column follows the standard literary language of Tajikistan and its current spelling rules. Spoken Tajik changes from region to region. The Spoken flag shows common forms, not every dialect.

The Iran flag follows the standard language of Tehran. The Dari flag follows the standard language of Kabul.

No form in this guide is the "correct" Persian. All three standards are correct in their own country.
