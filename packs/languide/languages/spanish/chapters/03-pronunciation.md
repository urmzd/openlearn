# Pronunciation Guide

## Introduction

Spanish pronunciation is highly regular and phonetic — words are pronounced almost exactly as they're written. Once you learn the basic rules, you can pronounce virtually any Spanish word correctly. This makes Spanish one of the most accessible languages for English speakers to learn to pronounce.

**Key principle:** Spanish is a syllable-timed language (like Japanese), meaning each syllable gets roughly equal time. English is stress-timed, so English speakers need to resist the urge to rush through unstressed syllables.

---

## Vowels

Spanish has only **5 pure vowel sounds**, and they are **always pronounced the same way**, regardless of context. This consistency is one of the best features of Spanish pronunciation.

### The Five Vowels

| Vowel | Sound | English Approximation | Example | Meaning |
|-------|-------|----------------------|---------|---------|
| **a** | "ah" | f**a**ther | **a**gua (AH-gwah) | water |
| **e** | "eh" | b**e**d | **e**sto (EHS-toh) | this |
| **i** | "ee" | mach**i**ne | **i**sla (EES-lah) | island |
| **o** | "oh" | g**o** (short, no glide) | **o**cho (OH-choh) | eight |
| **u** | "oo" | f**oo**d | **u**no (OO-noh) | one |

### Common Mistakes to Avoid

| Mistake | Wrong | Right | Example |
|---------|-------|-------|---------|
| Making "a" sound like "cat" | /æ/ | /ah/ | gracias = GRAH-syahs, not GRASS-ee-us |
| Gliding the "o" like English "go" | /oʊ/ | /o/ | no = noh (short, clean) |
| Reducing unstressed vowels | /uh/ | Keep pure | momento = moh-MEHN-toh, not muh-MEN-toh |
| Making "e" sound like "ee" | /iː/ | /eh/ | café = kah-FEH, not kah-FEE |

## Consonants

Most Spanish consonants sound similar to English, with some important differences.

### Consonants That Sound Like English

| Letter | Sound | Example | Notes |
|--------|-------|---------|-------|
| ch | "ch" in "church" | mu**ch**o (MOO-choh) | Same as English |
| f | "f" in "fun" | **f**iesta (FYEHS-tah) | Same as English |
| k | "k" in "kite" | **k**ilo (KEE-loh) | Same as English |
| l | "l" in "like" | **l**ugar (loo-GAHR) | Same as English |
| m | "m" in "map" | **m**esa (MEH-sah) | Same as English |
| n | "n" in "no" | **n**ada (NAH-dah) | Same as English |
| p | "p" in "spot" | **p**or (por) | Less aspirated than English |
| s | "s" in "sun" | **s**í (see) | Always unvoiced (like "ss") |
| t | "t" in "stop" | **t**odo (TOH-doh) | Tongue touches teeth, no puff of air |

### Consonants That Differ from English

| Letter | Sound | Example | Key Difference |
|--------|-------|---------|----------------|
| **b/v** | Both = "b" | **b**ueno, **v**ino | Identical sound; soft between vowels |
| **c** (before e/i) | "s" (LatAm) or "th" (Spain) | **c**ine | See seseo vs. distinción below |
| **c** (before a/o/u) | "k" | **c**asa (KAH-sah) | Like English "k" |
| **d** | Soft "th" between vowels | na**d**a (NAH-thah) | Like "th" in "the" between vowels |
| **g** (before e/i) | "h" (throaty) | **g**ente (HEHN-teh) | Like English "h" but stronger |
| **g** (before a/o/u) | "g" in "go" | **g**ato (GAH-toh) | Same as English |
| **h** | **Silent** | **h**ola (OH-lah) | Never pronounced |
| **j** | "h" (throaty) | **j**ugo (HOO-goh) | Stronger than English "h" |
| **ll** | "y" (varies by region) | **ll**amar (yah-MAHR) | See regional section below |
| **ñ** | "ny" in "canyon" | espa**ñ**ol (ehs-pah-NYOHL) | Unique to Spanish |
| **qu** | "k" | **qu**e (keh) | u is always silent after q |
| **r** | Tapped "r" | pe**r**o (PEH-roh) | Like quick "d" in American "butter" |
| **rr** | Trilled "r" | pe**rr**o (PEH-rroh) | Multiple tongue taps |
| **x** | "ks" or "s" | ta**x**i (TAHK-see) | In Mexico: "sh" or "h" in some words |
| **z** | "s" (LatAm) or "th" (Spain) | cere**z**a | See seseo vs. distinción below |

## Special Sounds

### Ñ — The Unique Spanish Letter

The **ñ** represents the "ny" sound (like "canyon" or "onion"). It is considered a separate letter in the Spanish alphabet.

| Example | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---------|---------------|---------|
| español | ehs-pah-NYOHL | Spanish |
| mañana | mah-NYAH-nah | tomorrow/morning |
| señor | seh-NYOHR | Mr./sir |
| año | AH-nyoh | year |
| niño/niña | NEE-nyoh/NEE-nyah | boy/girl |
| baño | BAH-nyoh | bathroom |

### RR — The Rolled R

The double **rr** is trilled (tongue vibrates against roof of mouth). Single **r** at the start of a word is also trilled.

| Spelling | Sound | Example | Meaning |
|----------|-------|---------|---------|
| rr (between vowels) | Trilled | pe**rr**o (PEH-rroh) | dog |
| r (start of word) | Trilled | **r**ojo (RROH-hoh) | red |
| r (after n, l, s) | Trilled | al**r**ededor | around |
| r (other positions) | Tapped | pe**r**o (PEH-roh) | but |

**Tip:** If you can't roll your R's, a strong single tap is perfectly understandable.

### LL — Regional Variation

The pronunciation of **ll** varies significantly by region.

| Region | Sound | Example: "llamar" |
|--------|-------|-------------------|
| Most of Spain | "ly" (like "million") | lyah-MAHR |
| Most of Latin America | "y" (like "yes") | yah-MAHR |
| Argentina/Uruguay | "sh" or "zh" | shah-MAHR |
| Some rural areas | "j" (like English "jelly") | jah-MAHR |

### Silent H

The letter **h** is always silent in Spanish.

| Word | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|------|---------------|---------|
| hola | OH-lah | hello |
| hotel | oh-TEHL | hotel |
| hospital | ohs-pee-TAHL | hospital |
| hora | OH-rah | hour |
| hay | eye | there is/are |

## Stress Rules

Spanish has very predictable stress rules, and accent marks (´) tell you when the rules are broken.

### Default Stress Rules

| Rule | Pattern | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| Words ending in vowel, **n**, or **s** | Stress **second-to-last** syllable | ha-**bla**-mos, re-**pi**-ten |
| Words ending in any other consonant | Stress **last** syllable | ha-**blar**, ciu-**dad**, co-**mer** |
| Written accent mark (´) | Stress the **marked** syllable | ca-**fé**, te-**lé**-fo-no, es-ta-**ción** |

### Accent Marks Also Distinguish Meaning

| Without Accent | Meaning | With Accent | Meaning |
|----------------|---------|-------------|---------|
| si | if | sí | yes |
| el | the | él | he |
| tu | your | tú | you |
| como | like/as | cómo | how? |
| que | that | qué | what? |
| donde | where (relative) | dónde | where? |

## Seseo vs. Distinción

This is the most notable pronunciation difference between Spain and Latin America.

### The Difference

| Feature | Spain (most regions) | Latin America |
|---------|---------------------|---------------|
| **c** before e/i | "th" (like "think") | "s" |
| **z** | "th" (like "think") | "s" |
| **s** | "s" | "s" |
| Term | **Distinción** | **Seseo** |

### Examples

| Word | Spain (Distinción) | Latin America (Seseo) |
|------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| gracias | GRAH-thyahs | GRAH-syahs |
| cerveza | thehr-BEH-thah | sehr-BEH-sah |
| zapato | thah-PAH-toh | sah-PAH-toh |
| cinco | THEEN-koh | SEEN-koh |
| plaza | PLAH-thah | PLAH-sah |

**Note:** Both are completely correct. Use whichever feels more natural. Southern Spain (Andalusia) and the Canary Islands also use seseo.

## Regional Accent Overview

| Region | Key Features |
|--------|-------------|
| **Central Spain (Madrid)** | Distinción (c/z = "th"), clear consonants, standard reference |
| **Southern Spain (Andalusia)** | Seseo, dropped final -s, relaxed consonants |
| **Mexico** | Clear pronunciation, moderate pace, "x" sometimes = "sh" or "h" |
| **Colombia (Bogotá)** | Considered very clear and "neutral," popular for language learning |
| **Argentina (Buenos Aires)** | ll/y = "sh," Italian-influenced intonation, vos instead of tú |
| **Caribbean (Cuba, PR, DR)** | Fast pace, dropped final consonants, aspirated -s |
| **Chile** | Fast speech, heavy slang, dropped -s, unique vocabulary |

## Pronunciation Tips for Common Tourist Phrases

| Phrase | Careful Pronunciation | Common Mistakes |
|--------|-----------------------|-----------------|
| Gracias | GRAH-syahs | Don't say "GRASS-ee-us" |
| Buenos días | BWEH-nohs DEE-ahs | Don't say "BWEN-ohs" |
| Por favor | por fah-BOR | Don't say "por FAY-vor" |
| ¿Dónde está? | DOHN-deh ehs-TAH | Don't say "DON-day ES-tah" |
| La cuenta | lah KWEHN-tah | Don't say "lah KWEN-tah" |
| Baño | BAH-nyoh | Don't forget the "ny" sound |
| Calle | KAH-yeh | Not "KAL-ee" |
| Cerveza | sehr-BEH-sah | Don't say "ser-VEZ-ah" |
