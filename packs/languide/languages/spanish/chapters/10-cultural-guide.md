## Regional Overviews

### Spain

#### Madrid

**Personality & Pace**
Madrid is Spain's vibrant capital — cosmopolitan, lively, and proud. Life revolves around social connection, with meals running long and evenings extending well past midnight. The city is direct, warm, and energetic.

**Social Behaviors**
- **Open and direct:** Madrileños are friendly and not shy about expressing opinions
- **Physical closeness:** Conversations happen at close distance; don't step back
- **Two-kiss greeting:** Standard between women, and between men and women. Men shake hands with other men in most settings
- **Punctuality flexible:** 15–30 minute delays are common for social events. Business meetings expect punctuality
- **Loud is normal:** Animated conversations at high volume are not arguments — it's passion

**Dining Culture**
- **Late meals:** Lunch 2:00–4:00 PM, dinner 9:00–11:00 PM
- **Menú del día:** Excellent-value set lunch at most restaurants (usually 10–15 €)
- **Tapas culture:** Order tapas at the bar; it's social, stand-up eating
- **Bread always provided:** Usually free with meals
- **Coffee after meals:** Café solo (espresso) is standard

**Key Vocabulary**
- Vale = "OK" (used constantly)
- Tío/Tía = "Dude" / "Girl" (casual address)
- Mola = "Cool" (slang)
- Caña = Small draft beer

#### Barcelona

**Personality & Pace**
Barcelona blends Catalan and Spanish identity. It's creative, Mediterranean, and proud of its distinct culture. Many locals speak **Catalan** as a first language, though everyone speaks Spanish too.

**Social Behaviors**
- **Bilingual:** Signs and announcements often in Catalan first, then Spanish
- **Mediterranean lifestyle:** Outdoors-focused, beach culture, late nights
- **Catalan pride:** Respect for Catalan identity is appreciated
- **International:** Very tourist-friendly, many English speakers

**Dining Culture**
- **Pa amb tomàquet:** Bread rubbed with tomato — a Catalan staple
- **Seafood:** Excellent seafood, especially in Barceloneta
- **Vermouth hour:** "Hora del vermut" before lunch is a tradition
- **Similar hours to Madrid** but slightly earlier meals

**Useful Catalan Words**
- Bon dia = Good morning (Catalan)
- Gràcies = Thank you (Catalan)
- Si us plau = Please (Catalan)
- Using even a word of Catalan is very appreciated

### Mexico

#### Mexico City (CDMX)

**Personality & Pace**
One of the world's largest cities — bustling, culturally rich, and warm. Mexicans are known for exceptional hospitality and politeness. Communication is indirect; people avoid saying "no" directly.

**Social Behaviors**
- **Warm greetings:** One cheek kiss between women, and between men and women. Handshake between men
- **Formality matters:** Use "usted" with strangers, older people, and service providers
- **Indirect communication:** "Ahorita" (right now) might mean "later" or "maybe." "Mañana" can mean "sometime in the future"
- **Family-centered:** Family is paramount; asking about family is welcome small talk
- **Punctuality flexible:** Social events start late (30–60 minutes). Business meetings are more punctual

**Dining Culture**
- **Lunch is the main meal:** La comida (2:00–4:00 PM) is the biggest meal of the day
- **Street food is excellent:** Tacos, tamales, elotes from street vendors are safe and delicious
- **Salsa always offered:** Usually multiple salsas; ask "¿pica?" (Is it spicy?) before committing
- **"Buen provecho":** Said when food arrives or when passing people eating — like "bon appétit"
- **Tortillas are bread:** Served with virtually every meal

**Key Vocabulary**
- ¿Mande? = "What?" / "Pardon?" (very polite, Mexican)
- Órale = "OK" / "Wow" / "Let's go" (versatile)
- Güey = "Dude" (extremely casual, only with friends)
- Chido/Padre = "Cool"
- Neta = "Really?" / "For real"
- Fresa = "Posh/preppy"

**Safety Tips**
- Use authorized taxis (pink/white in CDMX) or ride-hailing apps
- Keep valuables concealed in crowded areas
- Drink bottled water
- Ask locals about neighborhoods before exploring at night

### Argentina

#### Buenos Aires

**Personality & Pace**
Porteños (Buenos Aires residents) are expressive, intellectual, and passionate. Italian immigration deeply influenced the culture, food, and even the accent. Conversations are long, opinions are strong, and social life is rich.

**Social Behaviors**
- **One cheek kiss:** Universal greeting for everyone — men to men, women to women, everyone. This is more physical than most countries
- **Vos instead of tú:** Argentines use "vos" universally (¿Vos querés? instead of ¿Tú quieres?)
- **Animated conversations:** Loud, passionate debates are entertainment, not conflict
- **Late everything:** Dinner at 10:00 PM is normal, going out at midnight or 1:00 AM is standard
- **Mate culture:** Sharing mate (yerba mate tea) is a bonding ritual. Accept if offered; decline politely if not your thing

**Dining Culture**
- **Asado (barbecue):** The national obsession. Beef-centric cuisine is deeply cultural
- **Late dinner:** 9:00–10:00 PM start, sometimes later
- **Merienda:** Afternoon coffee/tea with pastries (medialunas = croissants) around 5:00–6:00 PM
- **Facturas:** Pastries from the panadería (bakery) — a morning and merienda staple
- **Wine:** Argentina is a major wine producer; Malbec is the signature grape

**Key Vocabulary**
- Che = "Hey" (friendly address, like "buddy")
- Boludo/a = "Dude" (casual; can be friendly or rude depending on tone)
- Bárbaro = "Great" / "Awesome"
- Pibe/Piba = "Kid" / "Guy/Girl"
- Re = Intensifier ("re lindo" = really pretty)
- Birra = Beer (from Italian)

**Vos Conjugation (Key Verbs)**

| Verb | Tú form | Vos form |
|------|---------|----------|
| to be | tú eres | vos sos |
| to have | tú tienes | vos tenés |
| to want | tú quieres | vos querés |
| to be able | tú puedes | vos podés |
| to come | tú vienes | vos venís |
| to go | tú vas | vos vas (same) |

## Etiquette Guide

### Greetings

| Situation | Spain | Mexico | Argentina |
|-----------|-------|--------|-----------|
| Meeting someone | Two cheek kisses (women, mixed gender) | One cheek kiss (women, mixed gender) | One cheek kiss (everyone) |
| Business meeting | Handshake | Handshake | Handshake (first), then kiss once established |
| Entering a shop | "Hola, buenos días" | "Buenos días" | "Hola" or "Buen día" |
| Leaving a shop | "Adiós" or "Hasta luego" | "Gracias, hasta luego" | "Chau" or "Hasta luego" |

### Dining Etiquette

| Rule | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Wait to be seated** | In nicer restaurants, wait at the entrance. In casual places and bars, find your own table. |
| **Both hands on the table** | Resting hands in your lap (American style) is considered slightly odd. Keep wrists on the table edge. |
| **Don't rush the waiter** | In Spain especially, service is intentionally relaxed. The table is "yours" for the evening. |
| **Splitting the bill** | Common among friends, but less common on dates. One person often pays. |
| **Compliments to the chef** | "Estaba riquísimo" (It was delicious) is always appreciated. |
| **"Buen provecho"** | Say this when food arrives to your table, or when walking past people eating. Universal. |

### Gift Giving

| Situation | What to Bring |
|-----------|--------------|
| Invited to someone's home | Wine, dessert, or flowers |
| Flowers to avoid | Dahlias (Spain — funeral flower), yellow flowers (some LatAm — grief/betrayal) |
| Business gifts | Nice wine, quality chocolate, or something from your home country |

### Topics to Avoid

| Topic | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Comparing countries negatively | "Mexico is cheaper than Spain" — don't compare |
| Politics (unless invited) | Strong opinions exist; let locals bring it up |
| Calling all Spanish "Mexican" | Each country has distinct identity and pride |
| Bullfighting opinions | Polarizing in Spain; don't assume everyone supports it |
| Falklands/Malvinas | Extremely sensitive topic in Argentina |
| The Spanish Civil War | Sensitive historical topic in Spain |

### Safe Conversation Topics

- Food and local cuisine (always a winner)
- Football/soccer (universal passion — learn the local team)
- Travel and your experiences in their country
- Family (especially in Latin America)
- Music and festivals
- Asking for recommendations

## Punctuality Norms

| Context | Spain | Mexico | Argentina |
|---------|-------|--------|-----------|
| Business meetings | On time | On time (5–10 min grace) | On time (10 min grace) |
| Social dinner at someone's home | 15–30 min late is normal | 30–60 min late is normal | 30–60 min late is normal |
| Restaurant reservation | On time | On time | On time (10 min grace) |
| Party/gathering | 30+ min late is fine | 1+ hour late is normal | 1+ hour late is normal |

**Rule of thumb:** Be on time for business and reservations. For social events, arriving "on time" may mean you arrive before the host is ready.

## Safety Guide

### General Safety (All Spanish-Speaking Countries)

| Tip | Details |
|-----|---------|
| **Keep valuables concealed** | Don't flash expensive phones or jewelry in crowded areas |
| **Use hotel safe** | Store passport, extra cash, and valuables |
| **Carry copies** | Keep photocopies of passport and important documents |
| **Stay aware** | Be alert in tourist areas, transit stations, and markets |
| **Use ATMs inside banks** | Avoid standalone street ATMs, especially at night |
| **Drink bottled water** | In most of Latin America. Tap water is safe in Spain |

### Country-Specific Safety Notes

**Spain**
- Very safe overall; pickpocketing is the main risk in tourist areas (Las Ramblas, Gran Vía, metro)
- Watch for the "bracelet trick" and "petition signing" scams
- Pharmacies (green cross) are available everywhere for minor health issues

**Mexico**
- Use authorized transportation (Uber, DiDi, sitio taxis)
- In Mexico City: avoid hailing random taxis on the street
- Drink bottled water; avoid ice from unknown sources
- Most tourist areas are very safe; ask locals about specific neighborhoods
- Keep a low profile with valuables in crowded areas

**Argentina**
- Buenos Aires is generally safe; be careful in La Boca (away from Caminito) and at night
- "Express kidnappings" (secuestro exprés) are rare but worth knowing about — don't resist
- Inflation means cash handling can involve large bills; use cards when possible
- Blue/parallel dollar exchange rate exists; understand the system before exchanging money
