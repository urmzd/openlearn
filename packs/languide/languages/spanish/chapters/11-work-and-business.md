# Work and Business

This chapter gives you the Spanish you need at work: introductions, meetings, email, calls, small talk and formal register. Each section uses **pattern phrases** (templates with slots you can fill in) and **fixed phrases** (ready-to-use expressions). Phrases use **usted** unless the notes say otherwise, because usted is the safe choice with clients and new contacts.

## Tú versus Usted at Work

Both words mean "you". **Tú** is informal and **usted** is formal. The choice changes the verb ending.

### Which One to Use

| Situation | Spain | Latin America |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| Colleagues at your level | tú from the first day | tú in most countries. usted is common in Colombia and Costa Rica |
| Your manager | usually tú | often usted until the manager offers tú |
| Clients, first contact | usted, then tú when the client offers | usted |
| Older or very senior people | usted | usted |
| First email to a person you do not know | usted | usted |
| A group ("you all") | vosotros (informal), ustedes (formal) | ustedes in all situations |
| Argentina and Uruguay | not applicable | vos replaces tú |

- Start with usted if you are not sure. Nobody is offended by usted.
- Change to tú when the other person uses tú with you or offers it.

## Numbers, Dates and Money

- Spain and much of South America write 1.500,50: a period for thousands and a comma for decimals.
- Mexico writes 1,500.50, like English.
- Dates put the day first: 14/03 is March 14.
- Written times use the 24-hour clock: 15:30.

### Softening Patterns

| Pattern | Spanish | Pronunciation | Example |
|---------|---------|---------------|---------|

| Would you mind [action]? | ¿Le importaría [infinitivo]? | leh eem-pohr-tah-REE-ah [inf.] | ¿Le importaría esperar un momento? = Would you mind waiting a moment? |
| I would like to [action] | Me gustaría [infinitivo] | meh goos-tah-REE-ah [inf.] | Me gustaría proponer otra fecha = I would like to propose another date |
| I would be grateful if you [action] | Le agradecería que [subjuntivo] | leh ah-grah-deh-seh-REE-ah keh [subj.] | Le agradecería que revisara el contrato = I would be grateful if you reviewed the contract |

### Three Politeness Tiers

| English | Spanish | Pronunciation | Notes |
|---------|---------|---------------|-------|
| Send me the report? | ¿Me mandas el informe? | meh MAHN-dahs ehl een-FOHR-meh | Casual (tú) |
| Can you send me the report? | ¿Me puede enviar el informe? | meh PWEH-deh ehn-BYAHR ehl een-FOHR-meh | Polite (usted) |
| Would you be so kind as to send me the report? | ¿Sería tan amable de enviarme el informe? | seh-REE-ah tahn ah-MAH-bleh deh ehn-BYAHR-meh ehl een-FOHR-meh | Very polite |
| I need this today | Necesito esto hoy | neh-seh-SEE-toh EHS-toh oy | Casual, direct |

| I would be grateful if you sent it to me today | Le agradecería que me lo enviara hoy | leh ah-grah-deh-seh-REE-ah keh meh loh ehn-BYAH-rah oy | Very polite |
| Sorry | Perdona | pehr-DOH-nah | Casual (tú) |
| Excuse me | Disculpe | dees-KOOL-peh | Polite (usted) |
| I apologise | Le pido disculpas | leh PEE-doh dees-KOOL-pahs | Very polite |
| I cannot | No puedo | noh PWEH-doh | Casual, direct |

- Casual: colleagues you address with tú.
- Polite: the default with usted.
- Very polite: first contact, senior people, complaints and written requests.
