# Language Foundations: Quick Reference

## Sentence Structure

Spanish follows **Subject-Verb-Object (SVO)** order, similar to English. However, Spanish is more flexible — the subject is often dropped because verb conjugation already indicates who is acting.

### Basic Word Order

| English | Spanish | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| I want water | (Yo) quiero agua | Subject "yo" is optional |
| She speaks Spanish | (Ella) habla español | Subject "ella" is optional |
| We have a reservation | (Nosotros) tenemos una reserva | Subject often dropped |
| The restaurant closes at ten | El restaurante cierra a las diez | Subject needed when it's a noun |

### Questions

Questions in Spanish are formed by intonation (raising voice at end) or by inverting subject and verb. Written questions use inverted question marks: **¿...?**

| English | Spanish | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| Do you have a table? | ¿Tiene una mesa? | Verb-first |
| Where is the exit? | ¿Dónde está la salida? | Question word first |
| How much does it cost? | ¿Cuánto cuesta? | Question word first |

## Gendered Nouns

All Spanish nouns are either **masculine** or **feminine**. This affects articles (el/la) and adjectives.

### General Rules

| Rule | Masculine | Feminine | Exceptions |
|------|-----------|----------|------------|
| Ends in -o | el libro (book) | — | la mano (hand), la foto (photo) |
| Ends in -a | — | la mesa (table) | el día (day), el mapa (map) |
| Ends in -ción/-sión | — | la estación (station) | — |
| Ends in -dad/-tad | — | la ciudad (city) | — |
| Ends in consonant | Varies | Varies | Check case by case |

### Articles

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---------|--------|
| **Masculine** | el (the), un (a) | los (the), unos (some) |
| **Feminine** | la (the), una (a) | las (the), unas (some) |

## Ser vs. Estar (Both Mean "To Be")

This is a key distinction in Spanish. Both mean "to be" but are used in different contexts.

### When to Use Each

| Use **Ser** for | Use **Estar** for |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Identity: Soy americano (I'm American) | Location: Estoy en Madrid (I'm in Madrid) |
| Origin: Es de México (He's from Mexico) | Temporary states: Estoy cansado (I'm tired) |
| Profession: Soy profesor (I'm a teacher) | Emotions: Estoy contento (I'm happy) |
| Time: Son las tres (It's 3 o'clock) | Conditions: Está cerrado (It's closed) |
| Characteristics: Es grande (It's big) | Health: Estoy enfermo (I'm sick) |
| Materials: Es de madera (It's made of wood) | Actions in progress: Estoy comiendo (I'm eating) |

### Conjugation for Travelers

| Pronoun | Ser | Estar |
|---------|-----|-------|
| yo (I) | soy | estoy |
| tú (you, informal) | eres | estás |
| usted (you, formal) | es | está |
| él/ella (he/she) | es | está |
| nosotros (we) | somos | estamos |
| ustedes (you all) | son | están |
| ellos/ellas (they) | son | están |

## Essential Verb Conjugation

Spanish verbs end in **-ar**, **-er**, or **-ir**. Here are the present tense patterns with the most useful verbs for travelers.

### Most Useful Verbs

| Infinitive | Meaning | Yo | Tú | Usted/Él/Ella | Nosotros | Ustedes/Ellos |
|------------|---------|----|----|----------------|----------|---------------|
| hablar | to speak | hablo | hablas | habla | hablamos | hablan |
| necesitar | to need | necesito | necesitas | necesita | necesitamos | necesitan |
| querer | to want | quiero | quieres | quiere | queremos | quieren |
| poder | to be able | puedo | puedes | puede | podemos | pueden |
| tener | to have | tengo | tienes | tiene | tenemos | tienen |
| ir | to go | voy | vas | va | vamos | van |
| saber | to know (facts) | sé | sabes | sabe | sabemos | saben |
| conocer | to know (people/places) | conozco | conoces | conoce | conocemos | conocen |
| hacer | to do/make | hago | haces | hace | hacemos | hacen |
| decir | to say/tell | digo | dices | dice | decimos | dicen |
| pagar | to pay | pago | pagas | paga | pagamos | pagan |
| comer | to eat | como | comes | come | comemos | comen |

### Hay — "There is / There are"

One of the most useful words: **hay** (eye) means both "there is" and "there are."

| English | Spanish | Pronunciation |
|---------|---------|---------------|
| Is there a bathroom? | ¿Hay un baño? | eye oon BAH-nyoh |
| There is no Wi-Fi | No hay Wi-Fi | noh eye |
| Are there seats available? | ¿Hay asientos disponibles? | eye ah-SYEHN-tohs dees-poh-NEE-blehs |

## Pronouns

### Subject Pronouns

| English | Spanish | Notes |
|---------|---------|-------|
| I | yo | Often dropped |
| you (informal) | tú | Friends, same age, casual |
| you (formal) | usted (Ud.) | Strangers, older people, service |
| he | él | |
| she | ella | |
| we | nosotros/nosotras | -os = masc/mixed, -as = all female |
| you all (informal, Spain) | vosotros/vosotras | Spain only |
| you all (formal/LatAm) | ustedes (Uds.) | Universal plural "you" |
| they | ellos/ellas | |

### Tú vs. Usted vs. Vos

| Pronoun | When to Use | Region |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| **tú** | Friends, family, children, peers, casual | Universal |
| **usted** | Strangers, elderly, service staff, formal settings | Universal |
| **vos** | Same as tú (replaces tú entirely) | Argentina, Uruguay, parts of Central America |
| **vosotros** | Plural informal "you all" | Spain only |
| **ustedes** | Plural "you all" (formal and informal) | Latin America (all contexts), Spain (formal) |
