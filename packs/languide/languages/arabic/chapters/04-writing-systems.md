# Writing System: The Arabic Script

Arabic has one script for every country and every dialect. You can learn to read it in about two weeks of daily practice. This chapter shows the vowel marks on the teaching examples. The survival words at the end have no marks, because signs, menus and screens do not have them.

---

## How the Script Works

- Arabic is written and read **from right to left**. A book starts at what an English reader calls the back cover.
- The alphabet has **28 letters**. All of them are consonants or long vowels.
- The script is **cursive**. Letters in a word connect, in print and in handwriting.
- Each letter has up to **four forms**: isolated, initial, medial and final. The form depends on the position in the word.
- There are **no capital letters**.
- **Short vowels are not written** in normal text. The reader supplies them from the context.
- **Numbers run from left to right**, also inside an Arabic sentence.

Most letters keep one basic shape. The initial and medial forms lose the tail. The dots do not change.

### Letter Families

Many letters have the same shape. Only the dots are different. Learn each family as one shape.

| Shape family | Letters | How to identify each letter |
|--------------|---------|-----------------------------|
| The "tooth" | ب ت ث | One dot below, two dots above, three dots above |
| The "tooth" at the start or in the middle | ن ي | ن has one dot above. ي has two dots below |
| The "hook" | ج ح خ | A dot in the middle, no dot, a dot above |
| The short curve | د ذ | No dot, one dot above |
| The long curve below the line | ر ز | No dot, one dot above |
| The three teeth | س ش | No dots, three dots above |
| The loop with a tooth | ص ض | No dot, one dot above |
| The loop with a vertical line | ط ظ | No dot, one dot above |
| The open curve | ع غ | No dot, one dot above |
| The small loop | ف ق | One dot above, two dots above |
| The tall letters | ا ل ك | ا is a plain line. ل has a hook to the left. ك has a small mark inside or a top stroke |
| The round letters | م ه و | م is a small closed circle. ه has two loops in the middle. و has a tail and never connects to the left |

### The Six Letters That Do Not Connect to the Left

Six letters connect to the letter before them, but never to the letter after them: **ا د ذ ر ز و**. After one of these letters there is a small gap, and the next letter uses its initial form. The gap is not a space between words.

| Word | Transliteration | Meaning | What happens |
|------|-----------------|---------|--------------|
| بَاب | bāb | door | ا does not connect to the last ب |
| دَرْس | dars | lesson | د and ر do not connect. All three letters are separate |
| وَرْد | ward | roses | No letter in this word connects |
| زَارَ | zāra | he visited | No letter in this word connects |
| مُدِير | mudīr | manager | م connects to د. Then د stops. ي connects to ر |
| مَشْرُوع | mashrūʿ | project | ر and و break the word into three parts |

## Vowel Marks (Harakat)

The vowel marks are small signs above or below a letter. Their Arabic name is **ḥarakāt**. You see them in textbooks, in children's books, in the Qur'an, and on a single word when the meaning is not clear. Normal text does not have them.

| Mark on ب | Name | Sound | Example | Transliteration | Meaning |
|-----------|------|-------|---------|-----------------|---------|
| بَ | fatḥa | a | كَتَبَ | kataba | he wrote |
| بِ | kasra | i | مِن | min | from |
| بُ | ḍamma | u | كُتُب | kutub | books |
| بْ | sukūn | No vowel | مَكْتَب | maktab | office |
| بّ | shadda | Doubled consonant | سُكَّر | sukkar | sugar |
| بًا | tanwīn fatḥ | -an | شُكْرًا | shukran | thank you |
| بٍ | tanwīn kasr | -in | كِتَابٍ | kitābin | "of a book" in a full formal reading. Careful speech says kitāb |
| بٌ | tanwīn ḍamm | -un | كِتَابٌ | kitābun | "a book" in a full formal reading. Careful speech says kitāb |

- **Fatḥa** and **ḍamma** are above the letter. **Kasra** is below it.
- **Sukūn** is a small circle. It shows that the consonant has no vowel.
- **Shadda** looks like a small w. A vowel mark can sit on it.
- **Tanwīn** is a doubled vowel mark. It adds an n sound at the end of a word. The -an form is common in adverbs, and many people write it in normal text: شكرًا (shukran), جدًّا (jiddan), أهلًا (ahlan).

### Long Vowels in Writing

A long vowel is a short vowel mark plus a letter.

| Long vowel | Spelling | Example | Transliteration | Meaning |
|------------|----------|---------|-----------------|---------|
| ā | fatḥa + ا | كِتَاب | kitāb | book |
| ī | kasra + ي | مُدِير | mudīr | manager |
| ū | ḍamma + و | سُوق | sūq | market |

In unvowelled text, an ا, a و or a ي in the middle of a word is usually a long vowel. That is a strong help for the reader.

In chat and on social media, many people simplify the spelling.

| Standard | Casual | Transliteration | Meaning |

## Reading Text Without Vowel Marks

Use these four steps.

1. **Remove the article.** If the word starts with ال, the rest is the noun.
2. **Find the long vowels.** An ا, و or ي in the middle is usually ā, ū or ī.
3. **Find the pattern.** A word that starts with م and has three more consonants is often a place (maf-ʿal) or a participle (mu-).
4. **Use the context.** The sentence tells you which word is possible.

| Unvowelled | With marks | Transliteration | Meaning |
|------------|------------|-----------------|---------|
| مكتب | مَكْتَب | maktab | office |
| شركة | شَرِكَة | sharika | company |
| مدير | مُدِير | mudīr | manager |
| اجتماع | اِجْتِمَاع | ijtimāʿ | meeting |
| مطعم | مَطْعَم | maṭʿam | restaurant |
| مشروع | مَشْرُوع | mashrūʿ | project |
| تقرير | تَقْرِير | taqrīr | report |
| كتب | كَتَبَ or كُتُب | kataba or kutub | "he wrote" or "books". The context decides |

## Numerals

Arabic uses two sets of numerals. The **Eastern Arabic numerals** are common in Egypt, the Levant and the Gulf. The **Western numerals** (0 to 9) are standard in Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia, and they are common in business everywhere. You must read both.

### The Ten Digits

| Western | Eastern | Arabic name | Transliteration |
|---------|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| 0 | ٠ | صِفْر | ṣifr |
| 1 | ١ | وَاحِد | wāḥid |
| 2 | ٢ | اِثْنَان | ithnān |
| 3 | ٣ | ثَلَاثَة | thalātha |
| 4 | ٤ | أَرْبَعَة | arbaʿa |
| 5 | ٥ | خَمْسَة | khamsa |
| 6 | ٦ | سِتَّة | sitta |
| 7 | ٧ | سَبْعَة | sabʿa |
| 8 | ٨ | ثَمَانِيَة | thamāniya |
| 9 | ٩ | تِسْعَة | tisʿa |

### Digits That Are Easy to Confuse

| Eastern digit | Value | Warning |
|---------------|-------|---------|
| ٠ | 0 | It is a dot. It is not a decimal point |
| ٥ | 5 | It looks like a Western zero |
| ٦ | 6 | It looks like a Western seven |
| ٧ | 7 | A V shape, open at the top |
| ٨ | 8 | A V shape, open at the bottom |
| ٢ and ٣ | 2 and 3 | ٢ has one curve at the top. ٣ has two |

### Numbers Run From Left to Right

The text runs right to left, but the digits of a number are in the same order as in English. The largest unit is on the left.

| Meaning | Eastern numerals | Western numerals |
|---------|------------------|------------------|
| The year 2026 | ٢٠٢٦ | 2026 |
| A price of 150 | ١٥٠ | 150 |
| The time 10:30 | ١٠:٣٠ | 10:30 |
| The date 26 November 2026 | ٢٦/١١/٢٠٢٦ | 26/11/2026 |
| Room 407 | ٤٠٧ | 407 |
| 25 percent | ٢٥% | 25% |

Dates are written day, month, year.

## Typing Arabic

### Setting Up

1. Add the Arabic keyboard in the language settings of your phone or computer.
2. Type one letter at a time. The software connects the letters and selects the correct form.
3. Set the paragraph direction to right to left. Most editors do this when the first character is Arabic.
4. Type numbers and punctuation in the normal way. The software puts them in the correct place.

### Keyboard Facts

| Topic | What to know |
|-------|--------------|
| Letter forms | You never select a form. One key gives all four forms |
| The home row | On the standard computer layout, the home row is ش س ي ب ل ا ت ن م |
| Hamza forms | أ, إ, آ, ؤ, ئ and ء each have a key. On a phone, hold ا to see the alif forms |
| ة and ى | Each has its own key. On a phone, ة is often on the main layout |
| Vowel marks | On a computer they are on the Shift layer. On a phone, hold a key or open the symbols page. Most people never type them |
| Arabic comma and question mark | ، and ؟ are on the Arabic layout |
| Mixed text | An English word inside an Arabic line can appear in an unexpected position. Check the line after you type it |
| Direction keys | In many Windows programs, Ctrl with the right Shift key sets right to left. Ctrl with the left Shift key sets left to right |

### Arabizi: Arabic in Latin Letters

In chat, many people write dialect with Latin letters and digits. The digits represent the Arabic sounds that have no Latin letter. This style is called **Arabizi**. It has no fixed spelling. It is for casual chat only, never for work documents.

| Digit | Arabic letter | Sound | Arabizi example | Arabic | Meaning |
|-------|---------------|-------|-----------------|--------|---------|
| 2 | ء | ʾ | la2 | لا | no (Levantine) |
| 3 | ع | ʿ | 3afwan | عفوا | excuse me, you're welcome |
| 5 | خ | kh | 5alas | خلص | enough, done |
| 7 | ح | ḥ | mar7aba | مرحبا | hello |
| 6 | ط | ṭ | 6ayeb | طيب | OK |
| 9 | ص | ṣ | 9aba7 | صباح | morning. Mainly in the Gulf |
| 3' | غ | gh | 3'ali | غالي | expensive. Many people write gh |

The four digits that you must know are **2, 3, 5 and 7**. In the Levant, 2 also represents the letter ق, because people pronounce it as a hamza.
