# Language Foundations: Quick Reference

This chapter gives the grammar that you need to build your own sentences. The base is Modern Standard Arabic (MSA). Levantine forms are labelled.

---

## How an Arabic Sentence Works

Five facts make Arabic sentences easy to start.

- The present tense has **no verb "to be"**. "I am a student" is "I student".
- There is **no word for "a" or "an"**. A noun without "the" is indefinite.
- The **adjective follows the noun** and agrees with it.
- The verb shows the subject. A separate pronoun is optional.
- Word order is flexible. A sentence can start with the verb or with the subject.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| I am a student | أنا طالب | anā ṭālib | No "am", no "a" |
| a new office | مكتب جديد | maktab jadīd | Noun, then adjective |
| I work at a company | أعمل في شركة | aʿmal fī sharika | No pronoun: the verb shows "I" |
| The manager arrived | وصل المدير | waṣala al-mudīr | Verb first. Common in writing |
| The manager arrived | المدير وصل | al-mudīr waṣala | Subject first. Common in speech |

## The Root and Pattern System

Most Arabic words come from a **root** of three consonants. The root carries a general meaning. A **pattern** of vowels and extra letters makes each word. If you know the root, you can guess new words.

### One Root, Many Words: K-T-B (writing)

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| he wrote | كتب | kataba | The base verb |
| he writes | يكتب | yaktub | Present tense |
| book | كتاب | kitāb | Noun |
| books | كتب | kutub | Same spelling as "he wrote". The context decides |
| writer | كاتب | kātib | The person who does the action |
| writing | كتابة | kitāba | The activity |
| office, desk | مكتب | maktab | The place of writing |
| library, bookshop | مكتبة | maktaba | A place, feminine form |
| written | مكتوب | maktūb | The result of the action |

### Common Patterns

In the pattern names, **f**, **ʿ** and **l** represent the three root consonants.

| Pattern | Meaning | Example 1 | Example 2 | Example 3 |
|---------|---------|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| fāʿil | The doer | كاتب (kātib) writer | عامل (ʿāmil) worker | سائق (sāʾiq) driver |
| mafʿal | The place | مكتب (maktab) office | مطعم (maṭʿam) restaurant | مدخل (madkhal) entrance |
| mafʿūl | The thing done | مفتوح (maftūḥ) open | مشغول (mashghūl) busy | ممنوع (mamnūʿ) forbidden |
| faʿʿāl | A profession | خباز (khabbāz) baker | طباخ (ṭabbākh) cook | نجار (najjār) carpenter |
| faʿīl | An adjective | كبير (kabīr) big | جديد (jadīd) new | بعيد (baʿīd) far |
| tafʿīl | An action noun | تسجيل (tasjīl) registration | تحديث (taḥdīth) update | تأخير (taʾkhīr) delay |

### More Root Families

| Root | Idea | Word 1 | Word 2 | Word 3 |
|------|------|--------|--------|--------|
| D-R-S | study | درس (dars) lesson | مدرسة (madrasa) school | مدرس (mudarris) teacher |
| ʿ-M-L | work | عمل (ʿamal) work | عامل (ʿāmil) worker | عميل (ʿamīl) client |
| S-F-R | travel | سفر (safar) travel | مسافر (musāfir) traveller | سفارة (sifāra) embassy |
| J-M-ʿ | gather | اجتماع (ijtimāʿ) meeting | جامعة (jāmiʿa) university | مجموع (majmūʿ) total |
| Ḥ-S-B | count | حساب (ḥisāb) bill, account | محاسب (muḥāsib) accountant | حاسوب (ḥāsūb) computer |

## The Definite Article and Sun and Moon Letters

"The" is **ال** (al-). It is attached to the front of the noun. It never changes for gender or number.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| a book | كتاب | kitāb | Indefinite |
| the book | الكتاب | al-kitāb | Definite |
| a company | شركة | sharika | Indefinite |
| the company | الشركة | ash-sharika | The l changes to sh |

### The Rule

Before a **sun letter**, the l of al- is not pronounced. The first consonant of the noun is doubled. The spelling does not change. Before a **moon letter**, the l is pronounced.

| Type | Letters | Example | Transliteration |
|------|---------|---------|-----------------|
| Sun letters (14) | ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن | الشمس | ash-shams (the sun) |
| Moon letters (14) | ء ب ج ح خ ع غ ف ق ك م ه و ي | القمر | al-qamar (the moon) |

A simple test: sun letters are made with the tip or the front of the tongue, near the place of l.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| the ticket | التذكرة | at-tadhkira | Sun letter ت |
| the lesson | الدرس | ad-dars | Sun letter د |
| the man | الرجل | ar-rajul | Sun letter ر |
| the market | السوق | as-sūq | Sun letter س |
| the morning | الصباح | aṣ-ṣabāḥ | Sun letter ص |
| the road | الطريق | aṭ-ṭarīq | Sun letter ط |
| the light | النور | an-nūr | Sun letter ن |
| the door | الباب | al-bāb | Moon letter ب |
| the university | الجامعة | al-jāmiʿa | Moon letter ج |

In Levantine speech the article is **il-**: البيت (il-bēt), الشمس (ish-shams).

## Gender

Every noun is **masculine** or **feminine**. Most feminine nouns end in **ة** (tāʾ marbūṭa), pronounced -a. Adjectives take the same ending.

|---------|-----------|-----------------|----------|-----------------|
| student | طالب | ṭālib | طالبة | ṭāliba |
| manager | مدير | mudīr | مديرة | mudīra |
| engineer | مهندس | muhandis | مهندسة | muhandisa |
| colleague | زميل | zamīl | زميلة | zamīla |
| big | كبير | kabīr | كبيرة | kabīra |
| new | جديد | jadīd | جديدة | jadīda |
| busy | مشغول | mashghūl | مشغولة | mashghūla |
| Arab, Arabic | عربي | ʿarabī | عربية | ʿarabiyya |

### Agreement

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| a big office | مكتب كبير | maktab kabīr | Masculine noun, masculine adjective |
| a big company | شركة كبيرة | sharika kabīra | Feminine noun, feminine adjective |
| the big office | المكتب الكبير | al-maktab al-kabīr | "The" is on the noun and on the adjective |
| The office is big | المكتب كبير | al-maktab kabīr | "The" is only on the noun: a full sentence |
| the big companies | الشركات الكبيرة | ash-sharikāt al-kabīra | A plural of things takes a feminine singular adjective |

- Some feminine nouns have no ة: شمس (shams) sun, أرض (arḍ) land, يد (yad) hand.
- Names of cities and most countries are feminine.

## Sentences Without "To Be"

A sentence that has no verb is a **nominal sentence**. Put the subject first and the information second.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| The room is big | الغرفة كبيرة | al-ghurfa kabīra | Subject, then adjective |
| He is at the office | هو في المكتب | huwa fī al-maktab | Subject, then place |
| This is a book | هذا كتاب | hādhā kitāb | Demonstrative, then noun |
| The manager is busy | المدير مشغول | al-mudīr mashghūl | Levantine: المدير مشغول (il-mudīr mashghūl) |
| I am not a student | لست طالبا | lastu ṭāliban | Negative with ليس. Levantine: أنا مش طالب (ana mish ṭālib) |
| The office is not far | المكتب ليس بعيدا | al-maktab laysa baʿīdan | Levantine: المكتب مش بعيد (il-maktab mish bʿīd) |
| I was at the office | كنت في المكتب | kuntu fī al-maktab | Past tense uses كان (kāna) "was" |
| The meeting was long | كان الاجتماع طويلا | kāna al-ijtimāʿ ṭawīlan | Levantine: الاجتماع كان طويل (il-ijtimāʿ kān ṭawīl) |
| There is a problem | هناك مشكلة | hunāka mushkila | Also يوجد (yūjad). Levantine: في مشكلة (fī mishkle) |
| There is no problem | لا توجد مشكلة | lā tūjad mushkila | Levantine: ما في مشكلة (mā fī mishkle) |

### "To Have"

Arabic has no verb "to have". Use the preposition **عند** (ʿinda) "at" with an attached pronoun.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| I have a question | عندي سؤال | ʿindī suʾāl | Literally "at me a question" |
| Do you have time? | هل عندك وقت؟ | hal ʿindaka waqt? | To a woman: ʿindaki. Levantine: عندك وقت؟ (ʿindak waʾt?) |
| He has a meeting | عنده اجتماع | ʿindahu ijtimāʿ | Levantine: عنده اجتماع (ʿindu ijtimāʿ) |
| We have a problem | عندنا مشكلة | ʿindanā mushkila | Same in Levantine |
| I don't have time | ليس عندي وقت | laysa ʿindī waqt | Levantine: ما عندي وقت (mā ʿindī waʾt) |

## Pronouns

### Independent and Attached Pronouns

An **independent pronoun** is a separate word. An **attached pronoun** is a suffix. On a noun it means "my, your". On a verb it means "me, you". On a preposition it means "me, you".

| English | Independent | Transliteration | Attached | With كتاب (book) | Levantine |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|----------|------------------|-----------|
| I, my | أنا | anā | ي (-ī) | كتابي (kitābī) | أنا (ana), كتابي (ktābī) |
| you, your (to a man) | أنت | anta | ك (-ka) | كتابك (kitābuka) | إنت (inta), كتابك (ktābak) |
| you, your (to a woman) | أنت | anti | ك (-ki) | كتابك (kitābuki) | إنتي (inti), كتابك (ktābik) |
| he, his | هو | huwa | ه (-hu) | كتابه (kitābuhu) | هو (huwwe), كتابه (ktābu) |
| she, her | هي | hiya | ها (-hā) | كتابها (kitābuhā) | هي (hiyye), كتابها (ktābhā) |
| we, our | نحن | naḥnu | نا (-nā) | كتابنا (kitābunā) | نحنا (niḥnā), كتابنا (ktābnā) |
| you, your (plural) | أنتم | antum | كم (-kum) | كتابكم (kitābukum) | إنتو (intū), كتابكم (ktābkum) |
| they, their | هم | hum | هم (-hum) | كتابهم (kitābuhum) | هم (humme), كتابهم (ktābhum) |

- On a verb, "me" is **ني** (-nī): ساعدني (sāʿidnī) "help me".
- The plural "you" أنتم (antum) is also a polite form for one person in formal writing.
- A noun that ends in ة changes it to t before a pronoun: سيارة (sayyāra) car, سيارتي (sayyāratī) my car.

### Pronouns After Prepositions

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| with me | معي | maʿī | مع (maʿa) "with" |
| with you | معك | maʿaka | To a woman: maʿaki. Levantine: معك (maʿak) |
| for me, to me | لي | lī | ل (li-) "for" |
| for you | لك | laka | To a woman: laki. Levantine: إلك (ilak) |
| from him | منه | minhu | من (min) "from" |
| to her | إليها | ilayhā | إلى (ilā) becomes ilay- before a pronoun |
| on us | علينا | ʿalaynā | على (ʿalā) becomes ʿalay- before a pronoun |
| in it | فيه | fīhi | في (fī) "in" |

### Demonstratives

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| this (masculine) | هذا | hādhā | Levantine: هاد (hād) |
| this (feminine) | هذه | hādhihi | Levantine: هاي (hāy) |
| that (masculine) | ذلك | dhālika | Levantine: هداك (hadāk) |

## The Idafa (Noun + Noun)

Arabic joins two nouns to show possession or type: "manager the-company" means "the company's manager". This structure is the **iḍāfa** (إضافة).

Three rules:

1. The first noun never takes ال.
2. The second noun usually takes ال, or it is a name, or it has a pronoun.
3. A first noun that ends in ة is pronounced with -at.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| the company's manager | مدير الشركة | mudīr ash-sharika | Manager + the company |
| the meeting room | غرفة الاجتماعات | ghurfat al-ijtimāʿāt | ة is pronounced -at |
| the passport | جواز السفر | jawāz as-safar | Literally "permit of travel" |
| the train station | محطة القطار | maḥaṭṭat al-qiṭār | ة is pronounced -at |
| the project manager | مدير المشروع | mudīr al-mashrūʿ | Job titles use iḍāfa |
| the office door | باب المكتب | bāb al-maktab | Door + the office |
| a cup of coffee | فنجان قهوة | finjān qahwa | Indefinite: the second noun has no ال |
| my friend's car | سيارة صديقي | sayyārat ṣadīqī | The second noun has a pronoun |
| the company's new manager | مدير الشركة الجديد | mudīr ash-sharika al-jadīd | The adjective follows the full phrase |

In Levantine you can also use **تبع** (tabaʿ) "belonging to": الكتاب تبعي (il-ktāb tabaʿī) "my book".

## Verbs

Arabic has two main tenses: **past** (completed action) and **present** (current or habitual action). The dictionary form is the past "he" form. The model verb is كتب (kataba) "to write".

### Present Tense

A **prefix** shows the person. Some forms also have a suffix.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Levantine |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| I write | أكتب | aktub | بكتب (baktub) |
| you write (to a man) | تكتب | taktub | بتكتب (btiktub) |
| you write (to a woman) | تكتبين | taktubīn | بتكتبي (btiktubī) |
| he writes | يكتب | yaktub | بيكتب (byiktub) |
| she writes | تكتب | taktub | بتكتب (btiktub) |
| we write | نكتب | naktub | منكتب (mniktub) |
| you write (plural) | تكتبون | taktubūn | بتكتبوا (btiktubū) |
| they write | يكتبون | yaktubūn | بيكتبوا (byiktubū) |

- Levantine adds **b-** to the present tense. After بدي (biddī), لازم (lāzim) and ممكن (mumkin), the b- is not used: بدي أكتب (biddī aktub) "I want to write".
- After أن (an) "to", MSA uses the same present form: أريد أن أكتب (urīd an aktub) "I want to write".

### Past Tense

A **suffix** shows the person.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Levantine |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| I wrote | كتبت | katabtu | كتبت (katabt) |
| you wrote (to a man) | كتبت | katabta | كتبت (katabt) |
| you wrote (to a woman) | كتبت | katabti | كتبتي (katabtī) |
| he wrote | كتب | kataba | كتب (katab) |
| she wrote | كتبت | katabat | كتبت (katbat) |
| we wrote | كتبنا | katabnā | كتبنا (katabnā) |
| you wrote (plural) | كتبتم | katabtum | كتبتوا (katabtū) |
| they wrote | كتبوا | katabū | كتبوا (katabū) |

The unvowelled spelling كتبت represents four forms. The context tells you which one.

## Negation

MSA uses a different negative word for each tense. Levantine uses only two words.

### MSA Negation

| Pattern | Arabic | Transliteration | Example |
|---------|--------|-----------------|---------|
| not (present) | لا [فعل] | lā [fiʿl] | لا أفهم (lā afham) = I don't understand |
| did not (past) | لم [فعل] | lam [fiʿl] | لم أفهم (lam afham) = I did not understand |
| will not (future) | لن [فعل] | lan [fiʿl] | لن أذهب (lan adhhab) = I will not go |
| is not (no verb) | ليس [صفة] | laysa [ṣifa] | الأمر ليس صعبا (al-amr laysa ṣaʿban) = The matter is not difficult |
| did not (past, alternative) | ما [فعل] | mā [fiʿl] | ما فهمت (mā fahimtu) = I did not understand |
| don't! (command) | لا [فعل] | lā [fiʿl] | لا تنتظر (lā tantaẓir) = Don't wait |

- After لم (lam), use the **present** form of the verb. The meaning is past.
- After ما (mā), use the **past** form of the verb.
- ليس (laysa) changes for the person: لست (lastu) I am not, لست (lasta) you are not, ليس (laysa) he is not, ليست (laysat) she is not, لسنا (lasnā) we are not.

### Levantine Negation

| Pattern | Arabic | Transliteration | Example |
|---------|--------|-----------------|---------|
| not (with any verb) | ما [فعل] | mā [fiʿl] | ما بعرف (mā baʿrif) = I don't know |
| did not | ما [فعل] | mā [fiʿl] | ما فهمت (mā fhimt) = I did not understand |
| not (with a noun or an adjective) | مش [صفة] | mish [ṣifa] | مش مشكلة (mish mishkle) = It is not a problem |
| there is no | ما في [شي] | mā fī [shī] | ما في وقت (mā fī waʾt) = There is no time |
| will not | مش رح [فعل] | mish raḥ [fiʿl] | مش رح أروح (mish raḥ arūḥ) = I will not go |

## Questions

### Yes or No Questions

Put **هل** (hal) at the start of a statement. Levantine has no question word: the voice rises at the end.

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-------|
| The office is far | المكتب بعيد | al-maktab baʿīd | Statement |
| Is the office far? | هل المكتب بعيد؟ | hal al-maktab baʿīd? | Levantine: المكتب بعيد؟ (il-maktab bʿīd?) |
| Do you work here? | هل تعمل هنا؟ | hal taʿmal hunā? | To a woman: تعملين (taʿmalīn). Levantine: بتشتغل هون؟ (btishtighil hōn?) |

### Question Words

| English | Arabic | Transliteration | Levantine |
|---------|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| What? (before a noun) | ما | mā | شو (shū) |
| What? (before a verb) | ماذا | mādhā | شو (shū) |
| Who? | من | man | مين (mīn) |
| Where? | أين | ayna | وين (wēn) |
| From where? | من أين | min ayna | من وين (min wēn) |
| When? | متى | matā | إيمتى (ēmta) |
| Why? | لماذا | limādhā | ليش (lēsh) |
| How? | كيف | kayfa | كيف (kīf) |
| How many? | كم | kam | كم (kam) |
| How much? (price) | بكم | bi-kam | قديش (ʾaddēsh) |

After كم (kam), the noun is singular: كم يوما؟ (kam yawman?) "How many days?"

The **unit comes before the ten**, joined with و (wa) "and". Hundreds and thousands come first.

| Number | Numeral | Arabic | Transliteration |
|--------|---------|--------|-----------------|
| 21 | ٢١ | واحد وعشرون | wāḥid wa ʿishrūn |

### The Basic Agreement Rule

| Number | Rule | Arabic | Transliteration |
|--------|------|--------|-----------------|
| 1 | Singular noun, then the number. Same gender | كتاب واحد | kitāb wāḥid (one book) |
| 1 | Feminine: add ة to the number | ساعة واحدة | sāʿa wāḥida (one hour) |
| 2 | Use the dual ending. No number word is necessary | كتابان | kitābān (two books) |
| 3 to 10 | Number, then a plural noun. The number takes the opposite gender | ثلاثة كتب | thalāthat kutub (three books) |
| 3 to 10 | With a feminine noun, the number loses its ة | ثلاث ساعات | thalāth sāʿāt (three hours) |
| 11 to 99 | Number, then a singular noun | عشرون ساعة | ʿishrūn sāʿa (twenty hours) |
| 100, 1000 | Number, then a singular noun | مئة دولار | miʾat dūlār (one hundred dollars) |

A beginner who uses the masculine number with every noun is understood. Dialects use a simpler system, and Arabic speakers do not expect perfect number grammar from a learner.
