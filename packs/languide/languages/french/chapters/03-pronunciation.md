# Pronunciation Guide

French spelling looks difficult, but the sounds follow rules. This chapter explains the respelling used in the Pronunciation column of every table, then covers the sounds that English speakers find hardest: the vowel "u", the nasal vowels, the French "r", silent letters and liaison.

---

## The Respelling Scheme

The Pronunciation column uses English letters. Read each syllable as an English speaker would, with these fixed values.

### Vowels

| Respelling | Sounds like | French spellings | Example |
|------------|-------------|------------------|---------|
| ah | "a" in "father", but short | a, à, â | la = lah |
| ay | "ay" in "day", cut short with no glide | é, final -er, -ez, the word "et" | café = kah-fay |
| eh | "e" in "bed" | è, ê, ai, ei, e before two consonants | mère = mehr |
| ee | "ee" in "see" | i, î, y | ici = ee-see |
| oh | "o" in "go", cut short with no glide | o, ô, au, eau | beau = boh |
| o | "o" in "more", short | o before a pronounced consonant | porte = port |
| oo | "oo" in "food" | ou, où, oû | vous = voo |
| ew | No English sound. Say "ee" with rounded lips. | u, û | tu = tew |
| uh | "a" in "about", with rounded lips | e in short words, eu, œu | je = zhuh, deux = duh |

### Nasal Vowels

| Respelling | Sounds like | French spellings | Example |
|------------|-------------|------------------|---------|
| ahn | "ah" through the nose. No "n" sound. | an, am, en, em | grand = grahn |
| ohn | "oh" through the nose. No "n" sound. | on, om | bon = bohn |
| ehn | "eh" through the nose. No "n" sound. | in, im, ain, aim, ein, un, um | vin = vehn |

**The nasal rule:** In this guide, only the three respellings "ahn", "ohn" and "ehn" are nasal. Do not pronounce their "n". After any other vowel respelling (an, on, en, een, ewn, uhn, oon), pronounce the "n" or "m" as a real consonant.

| Word | Respelling | The "n" is |
|------|------------|------------|
| bon (good, masculine) | bohn | silent, the vowel is nasal |
| bonne (good, feminine) | bon | pronounced |
| un (a, masculine) | ehn | silent, the vowel is nasal |
| une (a, feminine) | ewn | pronounced |
| prochain (next, masculine) | proh-shehn | silent, the vowel is nasal |
| prochaine (next, feminine) | proh-shen | pronounced |

### Glides and Consonants

| Respelling | Sounds like | French spellings | Example |
|------------|-------------|------------------|---------|
| wah | "wa" in "watt" | oi, oy | moi = mwah |
| wee | "we" | oui, ui | oui = wee, nuit = nwee |
| wehn | "w" + nasal "ehn" | oin | loin = lwehn |
| y | "y" in "yes" | i before a vowel, ill, final -il | bien = byehn, fille = feey |
| ye | "eye" | ail, aille | travail = trah-vye |
| zh | "s" in "measure" | j, g before e or i | je = zhuh |
| sh | "sh" in "shoe" | ch | chez = shay |
| ny | "ny" in "canyon" | gn | ligne = leeny |
| s, ss | "s" in "see". Never "z". | s, ss, ç, c before e or i | place = plass |
| k | "k" in "key" | c before a, o or u, qu, k | quatre = katr |
| g | "g" in "go" | g before a, o or u, gu | gare = gar |
| r | The French "r", made in the throat | r, rr | rue = rew |

### Hyphens

A hyphen separates syllables inside a word. A hyphen that joins two words shows a **liaison**: "vous avez" is written voo-zah-vay because the "s" of "vous" links to "avez" as a "z" sound.

## Rhythm and Stress

English stresses one syllable in each word and weakens the others. French does not.

- Give every syllable the same length and the same clear vowel.
- Make the **last syllable of each phrase** slightly longer. That is the only stress.
- Do not reduce vowels. In "restaurant", all three vowels are full: res-toh-rahn.
- Let your voice rise at the end of a yes or no question and fall at the end of a statement.

| English habit | French target | Example |
|---------------|---------------|---------|
| im-POR-tant | Equal syllables, last one longer | ehn-por-tahn |
| in-for-MA-tion | Equal syllables, last one longer | ehn-for-mah-syohn |
| CA-na-da | Equal syllables, last one longer | kah-nah-dah |
| TE-le-phone | Equal syllables, last one longer | tay-lay-fon |

### U Versus Ou

English has "oo" but not "ew". French uses both, and the difference changes the meaning.

**How to make "ew":** Say "ee". Keep your tongue exactly where it is. Now round your lips tightly as if to whistle. The sound that comes out is the French "u".

| French | Pronunciation | Meaning | French | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|--------|---------------|---------|--------|---------------|---------|
| tu | tew | you | tout | too | all |
| vu | vew | seen | vous | voo | you |
| rue | rew | street | roue | roo | wheel |
| su | sew | known | sous | soo | under |
| pur | pewr | pure | pour | poor | for |
| bu | bew | drunk (past of boire) | bout | boo | end |
| lu | lew | read (past of lire) | loup | loo | wolf |
| dessus | duh-sew | on top | dessous | duh-soo | underneath |

## Nasal Vowels

To make a nasal vowel, say the vowel and let the air pass through your nose and mouth together. Your tongue does **not** touch the roof of your mouth. There is no "n" at the end.

| Sound | Spellings | Example | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|-------|-----------|---------|---------------|---------|
| ahn | an, am, en, em | France, temps, enfant | frahnss, tahn, ahn-fahn | France, time, child |
| ohn | on, om | non, nom, bonjour | nohn, nohn, bohn-zhoor | no, name, hello |
| ehn | in, im, ain, aim, ein | vin, simple, pain, faim, plein | vehn, sehnpl, pehn, fehn, plehn | wine, simple, bread, hunger, full |
| ehn | un, um | un, lundi, parfum | ehn, lehn-dee, par-fehn | a, Monday, perfume |

**France:** Most speakers pronounce "un" and "in" the same way. This guide writes both as "ehn". **Quebec:** Speakers keep a separate, rounder sound for "un", so "brun" (brown) and "brin" (a strand) sound different.

### Three Nasals Side by Side

| ahn | ohn | ehn |
|-----|-----|-----|
| cent (sahn) = hundred | son (sohn) = his, her | saint (sehn) = saint |
| vent (vahn) = wind | vont (vohn) = they go | vin (vehn) = wine |
| banc (bahn) = bench | bon (bohn) = good | bain (behn) = bath |
| temps (tahn) = time | ton (tohn) = your | teint (tehn) = complexion |

Practice phrase: **un bon vin blanc** (ehn bohn vehn blahn) = a good white wine. It uses every nasal vowel.

### When "N" and "M" Are Not Nasal

A vowel + n or m is nasal when the n or m ends the word or comes before another consonant. It is **not** nasal when a vowel follows, or when the n or m is doubled.

| Nasal | Pronunciation | Not nasal | Pronunciation |
|-------|---------------|-----------|---------------|
| an (year) | ahn | année (year) | ah-nay |
| bon (good) | bohn | bonne (good, feminine) | bon |
| américain (American) | ah-may-ree-kehn | américaine (feminine) | ah-may-ree-ken |
| canadien (Canadian) | kah-nah-dyehn | canadienne (feminine) | kah-nah-dyen |
| il vient (he comes) | eel vyehn | ils viennent (they come) | eel vyen |
| il prend (he takes) | eel prahn | ils prennent (they take) | eel pren |
| cousin (male cousin) | koo-zehn | cousine (female cousin) | koo-zeen |
| important | ehn-por-tahn | immeuble (building) | ee-muhbl |

### The French R

The French "r" is made at the back of the mouth, not with the tongue tip.

1. Say "ah" and keep your tongue tip down behind your lower teeth.
2. Raise the back of your tongue toward the soft part of the roof of your mouth, where you make "k" and "g".
3. Let air pass through with a light friction, like a very soft gargle.
4. Keep it gentle. The sound is softer than the "ch" in the Scottish word "loch".

| Position | Example | Pronunciation | Notes |
|----------|---------|---------------|-------|
| Start of a word | rue, rouge, restaurant | rew, roozh, res-toh-rahn | Clear friction |
| After a consonant | très, prendre, gris | treh, prahndr, gree | Blends with the consonant |
| Between vowels | Paris, parent | pah-ree, pah-rahn | Light |
| End of a word | bonjour, soir, pour | bohn-zhoor, swahr, poor | Very light. Do not add a vowel after it |
| End, after a consonant | quatre, votre, prendre | katr, votr, prahndr | Almost whispered. Often dropped in casual speech: "kat" |

An English "r" in a French word is understood, but it is the strongest marker of an English accent. Practice the "r" with the words "merci", "au revoir" and "très bien" every day.

## Silent Final Letters

Most final consonants are silent. The consonants in the English word **CaReFuL** (c, r, f, l) are usually pronounced.

| Ending | Rule | Examples | Exceptions (pronounced or silent) |
|--------|------|----------|-----------------------------------|
| -s | silent | vous (voo), pas (pah), Paris (pah-ree) | bus (bewss), fils (feess), mars (marss), sens (sahnss) |
| -t | silent | petit (puh-tee), et (ay), restaurant | huit (weet), sept (set), ouest (west), internet |
| -d | silent | grand (grahn), chaud (shoh), froid (frwah) | sud (sewd), week-end |
| -x | silent | deux (duh), prix (pree), choix (shwah) | six (seess), dix (deess), index |
| -z | silent | chez (shay), parlez (par-lay), nez (nay) | gaz (gahz) |
| -p | silent | beaucoup (boh-koo), trop (troh) | stop, cap |
| -e (no accent) | silent | table (tahbl), porte (port), une (ewn) | It makes the consonant before it sound |
| -ent (verb ending) | silent | ils parlent (eel parl) | In other words it is "ahn": moment (moh-mahn) |
| -er | ay | parler (par-lay), premier (pruh-myay) | "ehr" in short words: mer, hiver, cher, hier |
| -c | pronounced | avec (ah-vek), sac (sak), parc (park) | blanc (blahn), porc (por), tabac (tah-bah) |
| -r | pronounced | pour (poor), soir (swahr), sur (sewr) | -er verbs, monsieur (muh-syuh) |
| -f | pronounced | neuf (nuhf), chef (shef), œuf (uhf) | clef (klay), œufs (uh), nerf (nehr) |
| -l | pronounced | il (eel), hôtel (oh-tel), avril (ah-vreel) | gentil (zhahn-tee), outil (oo-tee) |

A final **-e** changes the word: "petit" is puh-tee, "petite" is puh-teet. This is how you hear the feminine form of most adjectives.

## Liaison

A liaison happens when a silent final consonant is pronounced because the next word begins with a vowel or a silent "h". The consonant joins the next syllable.

### Liaison Sounds

| Written letter | Liaison sound | Example | Pronunciation |
|----------------|---------------|---------|---------------|
| s, x, z | z | les amis, deux heures, chez elle | lay-zah-mee, duh-zuhr, shay-zel |
| t, d | t | petit ami, grand homme, quand il | puh-tee-tah-mee, grahn-tom, kahn-teel |
| n | n | un ami, on a, en avance | ehn-nah-mee, ohn-nah, ahn-nah-vahnss |
| f | v, in two cases only | neuf heures, neuf ans | nuh-vuhr, nuh-vahn |

### Obligatory Liaisons

Always make these. The respelling in this guide shows them with a hyphen.

| Context | Example | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------------|---------|
| Article or determiner + noun | les enfants, mes amis, ces idées | lay-zahn-fahn, may-zah-mee, say-zee-day | the children, my friends, these ideas |
| Number + noun | deux heures, trois ans, dix euros | duh-zuhr, trwah-zahn, dee-zuh-roh | two o'clock, three years, ten euros |
| Subject pronoun + verb | vous avez, ils ont, on a | voo-zah-vay, eel-zohn, ohn-nah | you have, they have, we have |
| Verb + pronoun (inversion) | est-il, allez-y, ont-ils | eh-teel, ah-lay-zee, ohn-teel | is he, go ahead, have they |
| Adjective + noun | petit ami, bon appétit, prochain arrêt | puh-tee-tah-mee, bon-nah-pay-tee, proh-shen-nah-reh | boyfriend, enjoy your meal, next stop |
| Short preposition + word | en avance, dans un an, chez elle | ahn-nah-vahnss, dahn-zehn-nahn, shay-zel | early, in a year, at her place |
| "très" + adjective | très important | treh-zehn-por-tahn | very important |
| Fixed expressions | tout à fait, de temps en temps, États-Unis | too-tah-feh, duh tahn-zahn tahn, ay-tah-zew-nee | absolutely, from time to time, United States |

### Forbidden Liaisons

Never make these.

| Context | Example | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|---------|---------|---------------|---------|
| After "et" | et un café | ay ehn kah-fay | and a coffee |
| Before an aspirated "h" | les haricots, en haut | lay ah-ree-koh, ahn oh | the beans, upstairs |
| After a singular noun | le train arrive | luh trehn ah-reev | the train is arriving |
| After a name | Jean est là | zhahn eh lah | Jean is here |
| Before "onze" and "oui" | les onze joueurs, mais oui | lay ohnz zhoo-uhr, meh wee | the eleven players, but yes |

### Optional Liaisons

Careful and formal speech makes more liaisons. Casual speech makes fewer. As a learner, make the obligatory ones and skip the rest.

| Context | Careful speech | Casual speech | Meaning |
|---------|----------------|---------------|---------|
| After forms of "être" | je suis américain: zhuh swee-zah-may-ree-kehn | zhuh swee ah-may-ree-kehn | I am American |
| After "pas" | pas encore: pah-zahn-kor | pah ahn-kor | not yet |
| After a plural noun | des amis américains: day-zah-mee-zah-may-ree-kehn | day-zah-mee ah-may-ree-kehn | American friends |
| After "mais" | mais il: meh-zeel | meh eel | but he |

### Enchaînement

A consonant that is **already pronounced** also links to a following vowel. Nothing new is added. The words simply run together.

| French | Word by word | As spoken | Meaning |
|--------|--------------|-----------|---------|
| il a | eel ah | ee-lah | he has |
| elle est | el eh | eh-leh | she is |
| une amie | ewn ah-mee | ew-nah-mee | a friend |
| avec elle | ah-vek el | ah-veh-kel | with her |
| quel âge | kel ahzh | keh-lahzh | what age |
| notre équipe | notr ay-keep | noh-tray-keep | our team |
| cette année | set ah-nay | seh-tah-nay | this year |

This guide writes such words separately so that you can recognise each one. In speech, join them.

## Elision

A short word that ends in a vowel loses that vowel before a word that starts with a vowel or a silent "h". An apostrophe replaces it.

| Full form | Elided | Example | Pronunciation | Meaning |
|-----------|--------|---------|---------------|---------|
| je | j' | j'ai, j'habite | zhay, zhah-beet | I have, I live |
| le, la | l' | l'hôtel, l'adresse | loh-tel, lah-dress | the hotel, the address |
| de | d' | d'accord, beaucoup d'eau | dah-kor, boh-koo doh | okay, a lot of water |
| ne | n' | je n'ai pas | zhuh nay pah | I don't have |
| que | qu' | qu'est-ce que, parce qu'il | kess kuh, pars keel | what, because he |
| me, te, se | m', t', s' | je m'appelle, il s'appelle | zhuh mah-pel, eel sah-pel | my name is, his name is |
| ce | c' | c'est | seh | it is |
| si | s' | s'il vous plaît | seel voo pleh | please. Only before "il" and "ils" |

There is no elision with "tu" in writing: "tu as", not "t'as". In casual speech you will hear "t'as" and "t'es".

### Silent H and Aspirated H

The letter "h" is never pronounced. There are two kinds.

| Type | Effect | Examples | Pronunciation |
|------|--------|----------|---------------|
| Silent h (h muet) | Elision and liaison happen | l'hôtel, les hôtels, l'heure, les hommes | loh-tel, lay-zoh-tel, luhr, lay-zom |
| Aspirated h (h aspiré) | No elision, no liaison | le héros, les haricots, le hockey, en haut, le huit | luh ay-roh, lay ah-ree-koh, luh oh-keh, ahn oh, luh weet |

Dictionaries mark the aspirated "h" with a symbol before the word. Common aspirated-h words: haut, hasard, héros, haricot, hockey, hamburger, handicap, honte, hors, huit.

## Quebec Pronunciation Features

Formal Quebec French, as heard on the news, is close to the standard of France. Everyday Quebec speech has features of its own. You do not need to imitate them, but you need to recognise them.

### Affrication of T and D

Before the vowels "i" and "u", a Quebec "t" sounds like "ts" and a "d" sounds like "dz". This is the clearest sign of a Quebec accent, and it is present in formal speech too.

| French | France | Quebec | Meaning |
|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| tu | tew | tsew | you |
| petit | puh-tee | puh-tsee | small |
| mardi | mar-dee | mar-dzee | Tuesday |
| dire | deer | dzeer | to say |
| du | dew | dzew | some, of the |
| étudiant | ay-tew-dyahn | ay-tsew-dzyahn | student |

### Diphthongs and Long Vowels

Long vowels in a closed syllable glide into a second vowel. This is typical of casual speech.

| French | France | Quebec | Meaning |
|--------|--------|--------|---------|
| père | pehr | pah-ehr | father |
| fête | fet | fah-eht, close to English "fight" | party |
| neige | nezh | nah-ezh | snow |
| cœur | kuhr | kah-uhr | heart |
| garage | gah-rahzh | gah-rawzh | garage |
| pâte | paht | pawt | dough, pasta |

### Other Features

| Feature | France | Quebec | Example |
|---------|--------|--------|---------|
| Short "i", "u" and "ou" in a closed syllable | Tense vowel: vite = veet | Relaxed vowel: vite sounds like English "vit" | vite, jupe, route |
| Final "a" | ah | "aw" in casual speech | Canada = kah-nah-daw, là = law |
| "oi" in "moi" and "toi" | mwah, twah | mway, tway in casual speech | moi, toi |
| "un" and "in" | Same sound | Two different sounds | brun, brin |
| Long and short vowel pairs | Often the same | Kept different | pâte and patte, maître and mettre |
| "je suis" | chuis | chu (shew) | Chu fatigué = I'm tired |
| "il" and "elle" before a verb | il, elle | often "y" and "a" | Y vient = He's coming |
| "puis" (then, and) | pwee | pis (pee) | Pis toi? = And you? |
| "bien" as "very" or "well" | byehn | ben (behn) | C'est ben correct = It's quite all right |
| Yes or no question | Rising voice | Verb + "tu" | Ça va-tu? = Is it going okay? |

## Tricky Words

These common words do not follow the rules. Learn them one by one.

| French | Pronunciation | Meaning | Notes |
|--------|---------------|---------|-------|
| monsieur | muh-syuh | sir, Mr | Plural "messieurs" = may-syuh |
| femme | fam | woman, wife | "e" sounds like "a" |
| fils | feess | son | Silent "l", pronounced "s" |
| oignon | oh-nyohn | onion | The "i" is silent |
| second | suh-gohn | second | "c" sounds like "g" |
| août | oot | August | Also "oo" |
| œuf / œufs | uhf / uh | egg / eggs | The plural drops the "f" sound |
| œil / yeux | uhy / yuh | eye / eyes | Irregular plural |
| automne | oh-ton | autumn | Silent "m" |
| pays | pay-ee | country | Two syllables |
| examen | eg-zah-mehn | exam | Final nasal |
| album | al-bom | album | "um" sounds like "om" |
| plus | plew / plewss | no more / more | "s" is silent in "ne ... plus" and pronounced when "plus" means "more" at the end of a phrase |
| tous | too / tooss | all | "too" before a noun: tous les jours. "tooss" alone: merci à tous |
| six, dix | seess, deess | six, ten | "see" and "dee" before a consonant: six mois (see mwah) |
| Montréal | mohn-ray-al | Montreal | The "t" is silent |
| Québec | kay-bek | Quebec | "qu" sounds like "k" |

## Practice

Cover the Answer column. Say each answer aloud, then check.

| Prompt | Answer |
|--------|--------|
| Explain how to make the French "u" sound. | Say "ee", keep the tongue still, and round the lips. |
| Read aloud: "tu" and "tout". | tew / too |
| Read aloud: "bon" and "bonne". Which one has a real "n"? | bohn / bon. "Bonne" has a real "n". |
| Read aloud: "un bon vin blanc". | ehn bohn vehn blahn |
| Which final consonants are silent: avec, petit, hôtel, trop, neuf? | The "t" of petit and the "p" of trop. |
| Read aloud with the liaison: "vous avez deux heures". | voo-zah-vay duh-zuhr |
| Is there a liaison in "et un café"? | No. There is never a liaison after "et". |
| Why do we write "l'hôtel" but "le héros"? | "Hôtel" has a silent h. "Héros" has an aspirated h, which blocks elision. |
| How does a Quebec speaker pronounce "tu" and "mardi"? | tsew / mar-dzee |
| Read aloud: "neuf heures" and "neuf euros". | nuh-vuhr / nuhf uh-roh |
