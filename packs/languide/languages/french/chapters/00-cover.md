# French Communication Guide

### Practical Phrases for Conversation and Work in France and Quebec

**Version 1.0, November 2026**

---

## About This Guide

This guide focuses on **practical communication**: the phrases and patterns you need for real conversations in French, at home, in town and at work. You will not find tourism tips here. You will find scenario-based phrases in tables, reusable pattern templates, and a short practice table at the end of every chapter.

The base is the **standard French of France**. Wherever **Quebec French** differs in vocabulary, meals, numbers, greetings or register, both forms are given and marked "France:" and "Quebec:" in the Notes column.

## Who This Guide Is For

- English-speaking adult learners who study about 30 minutes a day
- Learners who want casual conversation and enough French for professional use at work
- Returning learners who studied French at school and have forgotten most of it
- Beginners who prefer practical scenarios to grammar theory
- Anyone who works with colleagues, clients or service staff in France or Quebec

## What's Inside

- **Pattern phrase templates:** Reusable structures such as "Could you [action]?" with slots you fill in
- **Scenario-based phrases:** Organized by situation (greetings, directions, transport, restaurants, shopping, hotels, work)
- **Communication tables:** English, French, pronunciation and usage notes in one row
- **Politeness tiers:** Casual (tu), polite (vous) and very polite (conditional and formal formulas)
- **Regional variants:** France and Quebec side by side wherever they differ
- **A full work chapter:** Meetings, email, calls, office vocabulary and the formal register
- **Practice tables:** Ten prompts per chapter that make you produce French from memory

## How to Use This Guide

1. **Take the self-check** in the next section if you studied French before.
2. **Start with Core Phrases** (Chapter 1) for the essential patterns and greetings.
3. **Read Chapter 3 early.** It explains the pronunciation column used in every table.
4. **Work through one section per day.** Say every phrase aloud at least twice.
5. **Use the pattern templates** to build your own sentences by swapping the slot words.
6. **Finish each chapter with its Practice table.** Cover the right column and answer aloud.

Count your "yes" answers.

| Score | What to do |
|-------|------------|
| 0 to 3 | Work as a beginner. Study chapters 1 to 4 in order, then chapters 5 to 11. |
| 4 to 7 | Skim chapters 1, 3 and 4. Study the parts of chapter 2 that you missed. Study chapters 5 to 11. |

Two more rules apply at every score:

## Key Features

### Pattern Phrases (Templates)

Pattern phrases are templates with slots marked by `[brackets]`. Learn one pattern and you can say many things.

| Pattern | French | Pronunciation | Example |
|---------|--------|---------------|---------|
| [thing], please | [chose], s'il vous plaît | [thing] seel voo pleh | Un café, s'il vous plaît = A coffee, please |
| Can I [action]? | Est-ce que je peux [infinitif]? | ess kuh zhuh puh [action] | Est-ce que je peux payer par carte? = Can I pay by card? |
| Where is [place]? | Où est [lieu]? | oo eh [place] | Où est la gare? = Where is the train station? |

### Phrase Tables

All phrases use the same four columns for quick scanning.

| English | French | Pronunciation | Notes |
|---------|--------|---------------|-------|
| Thank you | Merci | mehr-see | Universal |
| Excuse me | Excusez-moi | ex-kew-zay mwah | Polite. Getting attention |
| You're welcome | De rien | duh ryehn | France and Quebec. Quebec: also "Bienvenue" |

### Politeness Levels

Each chapter marks the register of a phrase in the Notes column.

| Level | Form | Use with |
|-------|------|----------|
| Casual | tu | Friends, family, children, close colleagues |
| Polite | vous | Strangers, service staff, clients, senior colleagues |
| Very polite | vous with the conditional and formal formulas | Formal email, requests to people you do not know, complaints |

### The Pronunciation Column

The Pronunciation column is an English-friendly respelling. Chapter 3 explains it in full. Three points are enough to start.

| You see | It means | Example |
|---------|----------|---------|
| ahn, ohn, ehn | A nasal vowel. Do not pronounce the "n". | bon = bohn |
| Any other vowel before "n" (on, en, ewn, uhn) | A normal vowel, then a real "n" | bonne = bon |
| ew | The French "u". Say "ee" with rounded lips. | tu = tew |
| zh | The "s" in "measure" | je = zhuh |
| A hyphen that joins two words | A liaison. The final consonant links to the next word. | vous avez = voo-zah-vay |

French does not stress one syllable of each word the way English does. Give every syllable the same weight, and let the last syllable of each phrase run slightly longer.

## A Note on Regional Variation

French is an official language on five continents. This guide covers the two varieties an English-speaking learner in North America or Europe meets most often.

- **France:** The reference variety for dictionaries, courses and international business.
- **Quebec:** The French of about seven million speakers in Canada, with its own words for meals, shopping, technology and work.

Speakers from both regions understand each other without difficulty in standard speech. Belgian and Swiss French get one table in Chapter 10, for the numbers 70, 80 and 90.

You do not need to choose one variety on the first day. Learn the France form, add the Quebec form where your life or work needs it, and stay consistent within one conversation.

## Conventions in This Guide

| Convention | Meaning |
|------------|---------|
| France: | The word or usage is typical of France |
| Quebec: | The word or usage is typical of Quebec |
| désolé / désolée | Masculine form, then feminine form. A man says the first, a woman the second. |
| [chose] | A slot. Replace it with your own word. |
| ? and ! | Written here with no space before them, which is the Quebec convention. France adds a thin space. See Chapter 4. |
