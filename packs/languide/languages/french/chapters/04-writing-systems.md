# Writing Systems: Reading and Typing French

French uses the same 26 letters as English, plus five accent marks and two ligatures. This chapter shows what each accent does, how spelling maps to sound, how to read signs and menus, and how to type accents on a Canadian Multilingual keyboard, a US keyboard and a phone.

## Accents and What Each One Changes

Accents are part of the spelling. A word without its accent is misspelled, and it can become a different word.

| Mark | French name | Letters | What it changes | Examples |
|------|-------------|---------|-----------------|----------|
| ´ | accent aigu | é | The sound. "é" is always "ay". | été (ay-tay), café (kah-fay) |
| \` | accent grave | è | The sound. "è" is always "eh". | père (pehr), très (treh) |
| \` | accent grave | à, ù | The meaning only. The sound stays the same. | à (to), où (where), là (there) |
| ^ | accent circonflexe | ê | The sound. "ê" is "eh". | fête (fet), être (etr) |
| ^ | accent circonflexe | ô | The sound. "ô" is a closed "oh". | hôtel (oh-tel), côté (koh-tay) |
| ^ | accent circonflexe | â | A longer "ah". Quebec: a clearly different, deeper sound. | pâte (paht), âge (ahzh) |
| ^ | accent circonflexe | î, û | Nothing in the sound. It separates a few word pairs. | île (eel), sûr (sewr) |
| ¨ | tréma | ë, ï, ü | The vowel is pronounced on its own, not joined to the vowel before it. | Noël (noh-el), naïf (nah-eef), maïs (mah-eess) |
| ¸ | cédille | ç | "c" sounds like "s" before a, o or u. | français, ça, reçu, garçon |

### The Circumflex and the Lost S

A circumflex often replaces an "s" that Old French once had. English kept the "s". This helps you guess meanings.

| French | English | French | English |
|--------|---------|--------|---------|
| forêt | forest | hôpital | hospital |
| île | isle, island | côte | coast |
| fête | feast, party | pâte | paste, pasta |
| intérêt | interest | coût | cost |
| août | August | maître | master |

### Accents on Capital Letters

Capital letters keep their accents: **É**tats-Unis, **À** bientôt, **Ç**a va. Quebec style guides require this. France recommends it, but you will often see capitals without accents on signs and in older documents.

### The 1990 Spelling Reform

A reform made the circumflex optional on "i" and "u" in most words. Both spellings are correct, and you will see both.

| Traditional | Reformed | Meaning |
|-------------|----------|---------|
| s'il vous plaît | s'il vous plait | please |
| coût | cout | cost |
| août | aout | August |
| maître | maitre | master |
| week-end | weekend | weekend |

## Ligatures: Œ and Æ

| Ligature | Sound | Examples | Pronunciation | Notes |
|----------|-------|----------|---------------|-------|
| œ + u | uh | sœur, cœur, œuf, œuvre | suhr, kuhr, uhf, uhvr | Very common |
| œ + il | uhy | œil | uhy | "eye" |
| æ | ay | curriculum vitæ, ex æquo | vee-tay, eg-zay-koh | Rare. Latin words only |

Writing "oe" instead of "œ" is accepted when the ligature is hard to type: "soeur", "coeur". Use "œ" in formal documents.

## Common Silent Endings

Many written endings sound the same. This is why French is easier to speak than to spell.

| Written endings | Sound | Examples | Pronunciation |
|-----------------|-------|----------|---------------|
| -er, -ez, -é, -ée, -és, -ées | ay | parler, parlez, parlé, parlée, parlés | par-lay for all five |
| -e, -es, -ent (verb forms) | silent | je parle, tu parles, ils parlent | parl for all three |
| -ais, -ait, -aient | eh | je parlais, il parlait, ils parlaient | par-leh for all three |
| -s, -x (plural) | silent | les tables, les bureaux | lay tahbl, lay bew-roh |
| -t, -d, -ts, -ds | silent | petit, petits, grand, grands | puh-tee, puh-tee, grahn, grahn |
| -i, -is, -it, -ie | ee | fini, je finis, il finit, finie | fee-nee for all four |
| -u, -us, -ue, -ues | ew | vu, vus, vue, vues | vew for all four |
| -au, -aux, -eau, -eaux, -ot, -os | oh | au, aux, beau, bureaux, mot, gros | oh, oh, boh, bew-roh, moh, groh |
| -ant, -ent, -and, -end, -ans, -emps | ahn | restaurant, client, grand, il prend, dans, temps | ahn at the end of each |
| -on, -ons, -ont, -ond | ohn | bon, nous parlons, ils sont, second | ohn at the end of each |

Because the plural "s" is silent, you hear the plural in the article: "le bureau" (luh bew-roh) and "les bureaux" (lay bew-roh).

## Punctuation, Numbers and Capitals

| Item | France | Quebec |
|------|--------|--------|
| Space before ? ! ; | A thin space: "Ça va ?" | No space: "Ça va?" |
| Space before a colon | Yes: "Objet : réunion" | Yes: "Objet : réunion" |
| Quotation marks | « guillemets », with a space inside each mark | « guillemets », with a space inside each mark |
| Decimal sign | A comma: 3,5 | A comma: 3,5 |
| Thousands | A space: 10 000 | A space: 10 000 |
| Money | 12,50 € | 12,50 $ |
| Time | 14 h 30, also 14h30 | 14 h 30 |
| Date in numbers | 03/12/2026 (day, month, year) | 2026-12-03 (year, month, day) |
| Percent | 20 %, with a space | 20 %, with a space |

This guide writes ? and ! with no space before them, the Quebec convention. In France, word processors set to French add the thin space for you.

French uses fewer capital letters than English.

| English | French | Notes |
|---------|--------|-------|
| Monday, Tuesday | lundi, mardi | Days take no capital |
| January, February | janvier, février | Months take no capital |
| French, English (languages) | le français, l'anglais | Languages take no capital |
| a Canadian company | une entreprise canadienne | Adjectives of nationality take no capital |
| a Canadian, the French (people) | un Canadien, les Français | Nouns for people take a capital |
| Sainte-Catherine Street | rue Sainte-Catherine | "rue", "avenue" and "boulevard" take no capital |

## Typing Accents

### Canadian Multilingual Standard Keyboard

This is the standard French keyboard in Quebec. It keeps the QWERTY letters and gives the common accented letters their own keys. The labels are printed on the keys. The table shows which US key position each one uses.

| Character | Key | Notes |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| é | The key where a US keyboard has / | Shift gives É |
| è | The key where a US keyboard has the apostrophe | Shift gives È |
| à | The key where a US keyboard has the backslash | Shift gives À |
| ç | The key where a US keyboard has ] | Shift gives Ç |
| ù | The extra key between the left Shift key and Z | Shift gives Ù |
| â, ê, î, ô, û | Press the ^ key (where a US keyboard has [), then the vowel | The ^ key is a "dead key". Nothing appears until you type the vowel |
| ë, ï, ü | Press Shift + the ^ key, then the vowel | The same key gives the tréma with Shift |
| « and » | Right Alt + Z and Right Alt + X | On Windows |

To switch to this layout on Windows, add French (Canada) in the language settings and choose the keyboard named "Canadian Multilingual Standard". On macOS, add the input source whose name ends in "CSA".

| Character | Keys | Notes |

### The AZERTY Keyboard

Computers in France use the AZERTY layout. You may meet it on a colleague's computer.

| Difference from QWERTY | Detail |
|------------------------|--------|
| A and Q | Swapped |
| Z and W | Swapped |
| M | To the right of L |
| Numbers | Need Shift. Without Shift, the number row gives é, è, ç, à and symbols |
| é, è, ç, à, ù | Each has its own key |
| Full stop | Needs Shift |
