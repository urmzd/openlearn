# S-M12 q21

Claim: for $L(s) = -\log \frac{e^{s_1}}{\sum_k e^{s_k}}$ with $N \ge 2$ finite logits, $\partial L / \partial s_j = \operatorname{softmax}(s)_j - [j = 1]$; $L > 0$; and $L \to 0$ exactly when $s_1 - s_j \to \infty$ for every $j \ne 1$.

Method: rewrite the loss as a log-sum-exp and differentiate term by term.

**Gradient.** $L = -s_1 + \log \sum_k e^{s_k}$. The derivative of $-s_1$ with respect to $s_j$ is $-[j = 1]$. By the chain rule, $\frac{\partial}{\partial s_j} \log \sum_k e^{s_k} = \frac{e^{s_j}}{\sum_k e^{s_k}} = \operatorname{softmax}(s)_j$. So
$$\frac{\partial L}{\partial s_j} = \operatorname{softmax}(s)_j - [j = 1].$$
This is the cross-entropy gradient "prediction minus one-hot label", with the positive as the label: InfoNCE is a classification of the positive among $N$ candidates.

**Positivity.** $L = -\log \operatorname{softmax}(s)_1$. For finite logits every $e^{s_k} > 0$, so $\operatorname{softmax}(s)_1 = e^{s_1} / (e^{s_1} + \sum_{k \ne 1} e^{s_k}) < 1$ because $N \ge 2$, and therefore $L > 0$.

**When the loss vanishes.** Divide numerator and denominator by $e^{s_1}$:
$$L = \log\Big(1 + \sum_{j \ne 1} e^{s_j - s_1}\Big).$$
The sum is a sum of positive terms. $L \to 0$ iff the sum tends to 0 iff every term $e^{s_j - s_1} \to 0$ iff $s_1 - s_j \to \infty$ for every $j \ne 1$. So the loss is never exactly 0, and pushing it toward 0 pushes the positive's logit away from every negative's, which is the contrastive objective.
