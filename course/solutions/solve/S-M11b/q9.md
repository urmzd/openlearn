# S-M11b q9

Claim: among distributions $p = (p_1, \dots, p_V)$ with $p_i \ge 0$, $\sum_i p_i = 1$, and $\sum_i p_i E_i = \bar E$ (fixed energies $E_i$, and $\bar E$ strictly between $\min E_i$ and $\max E_i$), the entropy $H(p) = -\sum_i p_i \ln p_i$ is maximized by $p_i = e^{-\beta E_i} / Z(\beta)$, $Z(\beta) = \sum_j e^{-\beta E_j}$, for the $\beta$ that meets the mean constraint.

Method: Lagrange multipliers, one per equality constraint.

The Lagrangian is
$$\mathcal{L}(p, \lambda, \beta) = -\sum_i p_i \ln p_i - \lambda \Big(\sum_i p_i - 1\Big) - \beta \Big(\sum_i p_i E_i - \bar E\Big).$$
At an interior stationary point ($p_i > 0$) each partial derivative vanishes:
$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial p_i} = -\ln p_i - 1 - \lambda - \beta E_i = 0 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad p_i = e^{-1 - \lambda}\, e^{-\beta E_i}.$$
The factor $e^{-1-\lambda}$ is the same for every $i$; normalization $\sum_i p_i = 1$ fixes it to $1 / \sum_j e^{-\beta E_j} = 1/Z(\beta)$. So $p_i = e^{-\beta E_i}/Z(\beta)$, and $\beta$ is chosen so that $\sum_i p_i E_i = \bar E$ (the mean energy is continuous and strictly decreasing in $\beta$ unless all $E_i$ are equal, so such a $\beta$ exists for $\bar E$ strictly inside the range).

It is the maximum: $H$ is strictly concave on the simplex ($-t \ln t$ has second derivative $-1/t < 0$) and the constraints are linear, so the feasible set is convex and a stationary point of the Lagrangian is the unique global maximum. The boundary $p_i = 0$ cannot be optimal, because the derivative of $-t \ln t$ is $+\infty$ at $t = 0$.

Reading: with logits $z_i = -E_i$ and temperature $T = 1/\beta$, $p_i = e^{z_i/T} / \sum_j e^{z_j/T}$ is exactly the softmax of $z$ at temperature $T$. Softmax is the least committal distribution consistent with a given expected logit, and temperature is the Lagrange multiplier of that constraint; L8.1's sampler divides logits by $T$ for this reason.
