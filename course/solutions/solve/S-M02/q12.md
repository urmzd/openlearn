# S-M02 q12

**Claim.** If $u$ and $v$ are differentiable on $[a, b]$ with $u'v$ and $uv'$ continuous (hence integrable) there, then $\int_a^b u\,v'\,dx = \big[uv\big]_a^b - \int_a^b u'\,v\,dx$.

**Proof (direct).** By the product rule, $(uv)' = u'v + uv'$ on $[a, b]$. Integrate both sides from $a$ to $b$. The left side is the integral of a derivative of $uv$, so by the fundamental theorem of calculus it equals $u(b)v(b) - u(a)v(a) = [uv]_a^b$. The right side splits by linearity of the integral: $\int_a^b u'v\,dx + \int_a^b uv'\,dx$, both of which exist by the continuity assumption. So $[uv]_a^b = \int_a^b u'v\,dx + \int_a^b uv'\,dx$, and subtracting $\int_a^b u'v\,dx$ from both sides gives $\int_a^b uv'\,dx = [uv]_a^b - \int_a^b u'v\,dx$, the claim.
