# S-M02 q30

**Claim.** The harmonic series $\sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac1n$ diverges.

**Proof (grouping terms; a direct lower bound on partial sums).** Let $S_N = \sum_{n=1}^N \frac1n$. Group the terms after the first into blocks $B_k = \{2^{k-1} + 1, \dots, 2^k\}$ for $k = 1, 2, \dots$; block $B_k$ has $2^{k-1}$ terms, and every $n \in B_k$ satisfies $n \le 2^k$, so $\frac1n \ge \frac{1}{2^k}$. Hence $\sum_{n \in B_k} \frac1n \ge 2^{k-1} \cdot \frac{1}{2^k} = \frac12$. For $N = 2^m$ the partial sum contains the first term and the blocks $B_1, \dots, B_m$:
$$S_{2^m} \ge 1 + \frac{m}{2} .$$
The partial sums are increasing (all terms are positive) and $S_{2^m} \to \infty$ as $m \to \infty$, so they are unbounded and have no finite limit. The series therefore diverges, even though its terms $\frac1n$ tend to 0.
