# S-M07c q5

Claim: if $X_1, X_2, \dots$ are independent random variables, each with mean $\mu$ and finite variance $\sigma^2$, and $\bar X_n = \frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i$, then for every $\varepsilon > 0$, $P(|\bar X_n - \mu| \ge \varepsilon) \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

Symbols: $E$ is expectation, $\operatorname{Var}(Y) = E[(Y - EY)^2]$, $n \ge 1$ the sample size, $\varepsilon > 0$ fixed.

1. **Mean.** By linearity of expectation, $E[\bar X_n] = \frac{1}{n}\sum_i E[X_i] = \frac{1}{n} n\mu = \mu$.
2. **Variance.** For independent variables the covariances vanish, so the variance of a sum is the sum of the variances: $\operatorname{Var}(\sum_i X_i) = n\sigma^2$. Scaling by $1/n$ multiplies the variance by $1/n^2$, so $\operatorname{Var}(\bar X_n) = \sigma^2/n$. This is where $\sigma^2 < \infty$ is used: every term is a finite number.
3. **Chebyshev.** For any $Y$ with finite variance and $\varepsilon > 0$, apply Markov's inequality ($P(Z \ge a) \le E[Z]/a$ for $Z \ge 0$, $a > 0$) to $Z = (Y - EY)^2$ and $a = \varepsilon^2$: $P(|Y - EY| \ge \varepsilon) = P(Z \ge \varepsilon^2) \le \operatorname{Var}(Y)/\varepsilon^2$.
4. **Combine.** With $Y = \bar X_n$, steps 1 to 3 give $P(|\bar X_n - \mu| \ge \varepsilon) \le \frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2}$.
5. **Limit.** For fixed $\varepsilon$ the right side is a constant times $1/n$, which tends to 0; probabilities are at least 0, so the left side tends to 0 as well.

Hence $\bar X_n \to \mu$ in probability, which is the weak law of large numbers.
