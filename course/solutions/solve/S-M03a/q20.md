**Claim.** Any $n + 1$ vectors $v_1, \dots, v_{n+1}$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ are linearly dependent.

**Proof (direct, by elimination).** Let $A$ be the $n \times (n + 1)$ matrix with columns $v_1, \dots, v_{n+1}$. The vectors are dependent exactly when $Ac = 0$ for some $c \ne 0$, since $Ac = \sum_i c_i v_i$. Reduce $A$ to echelon form by row operations; these do not change the solutions of $Ac = 0$. Each pivot occupies its own row, and there are only $n$ rows, so there are at most $n$ pivot columns among the $n + 1$ columns: at least one column $j$ has no pivot, and $c_j$ is a free variable. Set $c_j = 1$, every other free variable to 0, and solve for the pivot variables by back substitution. This gives a solution $c$ of $Ac = 0$ with $c_j = 1$, so $c \ne 0$. Therefore $\sum_i c_i v_i = 0$ with not all $c_i$ zero, and the vectors are linearly dependent.
