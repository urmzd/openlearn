**Claim.** If $Av_1 = \lambda_1 v_1$ and $Av_2 = \lambda_2 v_2$ with $v_1, v_2 \ne 0$ and $\lambda_1 \ne \lambda_2$, then $v_1$ and $v_2$ are linearly independent.

**Proof (direct).** Suppose $c_1 v_1 + c_2 v_2 = 0$ (1); we show $c_1 = c_2 = 0$. Apply $A$ to (1): $c_1 \lambda_1 v_1 + c_2 \lambda_2 v_2 = 0$ (2). Multiply (1) by $\lambda_2$ and subtract it from (2): $c_1(\lambda_1 - \lambda_2) v_1 = 0$. Since $\lambda_1 - \lambda_2 \ne 0$ and $v_1 \ne 0$, this forces $c_1 = 0$. Then (1) reads $c_2 v_2 = 0$, and $v_2 \ne 0$ forces $c_2 = 0$. So the only combination giving 0 is the trivial one, and $v_1, v_2$ are linearly independent.
