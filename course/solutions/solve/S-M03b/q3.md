# S-M03b q3

Claim: for $a, b \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\lvert a \cdot b \rvert \le \lVert a \rVert \lVert b \rVert$, with equality exactly when one of $a, b$ is a scalar multiple of the other. Here $a \cdot b = \sum_i a_i b_i$ and $\lVert a \rVert = \sqrt{a \cdot a}$.

Method: a squared length is never negative; apply that to $a - t b$ for every real $t$.

If $b = 0$, both sides are $0$, and $b = 0 \cdot a$ is a multiple of $a$: the claim holds with equality.

Let $b \ne 0$. For every real $t$,
$$0 \le \lVert a - t b \rVert^2 = (a - tb) \cdot (a - tb) = \lVert a \rVert^2 - 2t\,(a \cdot b) + t^2 \lVert b \rVert^2 .$$
Choose $t = (a \cdot b) / \lVert b \rVert^2$ (allowed, since $\lVert b \rVert > 0$):
$$0 \le \lVert a \rVert^2 - 2\frac{(a \cdot b)^2}{\lVert b \rVert^2} + \frac{(a \cdot b)^2}{\lVert b \rVert^2} = \lVert a \rVert^2 - \frac{(a \cdot b)^2}{\lVert b \rVert^2}.$$
Multiplying by $\lVert b \rVert^2 > 0$ gives $(a \cdot b)^2 \le \lVert a \rVert^2 \lVert b \rVert^2$, and taking square roots of non-negative numbers, $\lvert a \cdot b \rvert \le \lVert a \rVert \lVert b \rVert$.

Equality: if equality holds, the chain above gives $\lVert a - t b \rVert^2 = 0$ for that $t$, so $a = t b$, a multiple of $b$. Conversely, if $a = s b$ then $\lvert a \cdot b \rvert = \lvert s \rvert \lVert b \rVert^2 = \lVert a \rVert \lVert b \rVert$; and if $b = s a$ the same computation with the roles swapped applies.

Cosine: for nonzero $a, b$, dividing by $\lVert a \rVert \lVert b \rVert > 0$ gives $-1 \le \cos\theta = \frac{a \cdot b}{\lVert a \rVert \lVert b \rVert} \le 1$, with $\pm 1$ exactly for parallel or opposite vectors. This is why M03.6's `cosine_sim` stays in $[-1, 1]$ up to rounding.
